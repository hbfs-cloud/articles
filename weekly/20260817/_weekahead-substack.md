## The week that closed (Friday 14 August)

Five lines, all prices as of the 14 August close, compared with the 7 August close.

- **S&P 500 (SPY) 776.34, +0.40%.** Sitting 0.4% under its best close of the year (777.88, set Thursday). Up 13.85% since January.
- **Russell 2000 (IWM) 305.09, +1.17%.** Its **highest close of 2026**. Small caps led.
- **Energy (XLE) +7.67%**, five times the second-best sector. Up 38.47% this year.
- **Consumer discretionary (XLY) −1.39%**: the only sector down on the week, and the only one still negative for 2026 (−1.01%).
- **Bitcoin 62,976, −3.00%.** Down 28% this year and 36% below its 2026 peak, while stocks sit near records.

And the thing almost nobody said out loud: **the rate cut disappeared**. On the prediction markets, a September cut is now priced at **1.15%**. A September *hike* is priced at **24.50%**. No change: 74.50%. Long-dated Treasuries (TLT) fell 0.87% on the week, which is exactly what happens when the market stops expecting easier money.

So small caps, the most rate-sensitive corner of the market, made their best close of the year while the story that was supposed to be driving them fell apart. Either they are climbing on something real, or someone is wrong.

## The week ahead: three things that matter

**1. Home Depot, Tuesday before the open.** Consensus is $4.64 per share on a $338bn company. Home improvement is money people spend when they feel comfortable, on projects they can postpone. It is the cleanest read on the US household budget you will get all month. Toll Brothers reports the same evening on new high-end homes — giving a second, independent look at the same question.

**2. Fed minutes, Wednesday 2pm New York.** These are the notes from the late-July meeting, and they land in a market that now assigns one-in-four odds to a rate *increase* in September. If the minutes sound as hawkish as the market already thinks, nothing much happens. If they show a committee more split than expected, that 24.50% deflates and bonds bounce.

**3. Lowe's, Target and TJX Wednesday morning; Walmart and Alibaba Thursday.** Seven retailers report between Tuesday and Thursday, worth **$1,725.8bn** combined. Watch the spread, not the headlines: TJX sells discounted overstock and *wins* when shoppers trade down. If TJX is strong while Target disappoints, the message is not "the consumer is fine" or "the consumer is broken" — it is that the consumer is trading down. That is bad for margins across the sector and it is the outcome the market is least positioned for.

Friday has no large-cap earnings at all. That is the session where you see what actually stuck.

## The one chart in your head

Technology trades **20.6% above** its 200-day average. Consumer discretionary trades **0.9% above** its own. Nearly twenty points between two sectors that ultimately depend on the same person having money.

That gap says the 2026 US rally is being paid for by corporate investment, not household spending. This week puts the household half on the stand.

- **XLY closes above 119.86** after earnings → the consumer is fine, our read is wrong, the gap closes from the top.
- **XLY closes below 117.10** → first break of a major US sector's long-term trend this year. Reduce cyclical exposure; do not go hunting for the bottom.
- **September hike odds go above 35%** after Wednesday's minutes → tightening is further along than equity prices reflect, and small caps become untenable.
- **VIX back above 16.02** → the low-volatility pillar holding up the whole risk-on regime cracks.
- **XLE closes above 62.56** → energy takes twelve-month leadership, the inflation channel reopens, and the Fed gets another reason to sit still.
- **IWM closes below 303.06** → Friday's breakout was false. No debate, no averaging down.

## The honest part

Last week we wrote "no adding energy" because the sector was falling. It then rose 7.67%. The reasoning was sound and the conclusion was wrong: we used a sector's recent trend to decide a *hedging* question, and a hedge is worth buying precisely when nobody likes it. Energy is negatively correlated to the S&P (−0.33), to small caps (−0.43) and to long bonds (−0.59) over sixty sessions. That is the argument, and it never needed the sector to be popular.

We are not chasing it at Friday's price either — 4.7% above its 20-day average, with a catalyst already priced at 98.45%. The buyable level is 59.50–59.16 on a pullback.
