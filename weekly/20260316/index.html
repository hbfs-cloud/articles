What is Triple Witching?

Triple Witching (sometimes called Quadruple Witching) occurs on the third Friday of March, June, September, and December when stock options, stock index options, stock index futures, and single-stock futures all expire on the same day. This creates massive rebalancing flows as institutional investors roll, close, or exercise positions. Volume typically surges 2-3x normal levels, with most of the action concentrated in the final 90 minutes ("the witching hour"). In a VIX 29 environment, expect amplified moves: what would normally be a 0.5% swing could become 1.5-2%. Practical tip: avoid opening new positions on OpEx day; widen your stop losses by 30-50% on Thursday evening to avoid getting stopped out by noise; and be aware that the close on Friday often reverses the morning direction.

Understanding Regime Scores

Our regime model assigns a score from 0 to 1 based on multiple factors: VIX level, breadth (percentage of stocks above key moving averages), credit spreads, yield curve shape, and momentum. A score above 0.65 indicates risk-on (favorable for equities). A score below 0.50 indicates risk-off (defensive posture recommended). At 0.44, we are firmly in risk-off territory. The screener model shows "early risk-off," which is a transitional state that sometimes precedes further deterioration but can also mark the bottom if a positive catalyst emerges (like a Fed dovish surprise or Hormuz de-escalation). In practical terms: reduce equity exposure, favor hard assets (gold, energy), raise cash, and avoid leveraged positions until the score recovers above 0.55 with confirmation.

How to Trade the FOMC Decision

The FOMC announcement at 2:00 PM ET triggers an immediate algorithmic reaction based on the rate decision and dot plot changes. This is typically a false move. The real move comes during Powell's press conference at 2:30 PM, when his tone, word choices, and answers to reporter questions reveal the committee's true thinking. The best strategy: (1) Do NOT open new positions between 1:30 PM and 3:00 PM — let the algos fight it out. (2) Watch for the "reversal pattern" — in 60% of FOMC days since 2020, the market reversed direction in the final 90 minutes. (3) The day-after-FOMC (Thursday) often continues the 3:00-4:00 PM Wednesday direction. (4) With PPI data dropping the same morning, expect two distinct volatility spikes: one at 8:30 AM (PPI) and one at 2:00 PM (FOMC). Position accordingly.

This is a healthy pullback, not a trend reversal. The fundamental drivers for gold (war premium, stagflation, central bank buying, weak dollar) remain intact. History shows that in extended crises, gold typically pulls back 3-5% before resuming its uptrend. Understanding Healthy Pullbacks in Bull Markets

Gold's −1.3% decline this week might seem alarming given that the geopolitical backdrop has actually worsened (Khamenei reportedly killed, Hormuz still closed). But this is a classic example of a healthy pullback in a structural bull market. Here is why pullbacks happen even when the fundamental thesis strengthens: (1) Profit-taking mechanics — after a 35%+ rally from September 2025 lows (~$3,800 to $5,248 ATH), institutional investors rebalance by trimming their winners. This is automatic for many pension funds and endowments. (2) Margin call funding — when equities fall sharply (SPY −5% from high), investors sell their most liquid profitable positions to cover losses elsewhere. Gold and treasuries are first in line. (3) Options expiry — gold options positioning ahead of Triple Witching can create temporary selling pressure. (4) Dollar dynamics — even small DXY bounces can trigger algorithmic gold selling. The key technical levels to watch: $5,000 (psychological support), $4,900 (previous breakout level), and $4,750 (200-day moving average). As long as gold holds above $4,900, the bull market structure is intact. Our trade at GLD $465-475 entry with a $440 stop provides 4.5% downside room — equivalent to gold at roughly $4,800, well below the structural support zone.

The Succession Wild Card

The reported death of Supreme Leader Khamenei creates an unprecedented variable. Historically, leadership successions in authoritarian states produce one of two outcomes: (1) De-escalation — the new leader seeks to consolidate internal power first and opens backchannel negotiations with adversaries (optimistic scenario, probability ~25%). (2) Escalation — the new leader attempts to demonstrate strength by escalating militarily, particularly if they lack the legitimacy of their predecessor (pessimistic scenario, probability ~40%). Mojtaba Khamenei is widely viewed as lacking his father's religious authority and political network, which makes Option 2 more likely as he attempts to prove himself. Markets should price in elevated tail risk for the next 2-4 weeks during this transition period. The wildcard: IRGC hardliners could sideline Mojtaba entirely and establish a military junta, which would be the most hawkish outcome for oil prices.

How to Price Leadership Transitions in Hostile States

When the leader of a country in active military conflict is killed or removed, financial markets typically respond in three phases. Phase 1 (0-48 hours): Uncertainty spike — VIX rises, oil jumps, equities dip. This is the "fog of war" phase where information is unreliable. Phase 2 (1-2 weeks): Assessment — markets begin to price the successor's likely stance. If the successor is perceived as more pragmatic, risk assets rally (Gorbachev after Brezhnev, for example). If the successor is perceived as more hawkish, risk-off deepens. Phase 3 (1-3 months): New equilibrium — markets settle into a new range based on actual policy actions. We are currently in late Phase 1 / early Phase 2 for the Khamenei succession. The market will spend the next 1-2 weeks interpreting Mojtaba's signals. Any public statement about Hormuz, negotiations, or military action will move oil by $3-5 in either direction. This is a binary-outcome environment where hedging (via options) is more appropriate than directional bets.

Why Cash Is a Position

Many investors feel guilty holding 13% cash when "the market always goes up over time." This is a dangerous misconception in risk-off environments. Cash serves three critical functions during periods of elevated uncertainty: (1) Optionality — after the FOMC decision Wednesday, you may have clarity on the rate path that creates high-conviction entry points. (3) Risk-adjusted return — money market funds are yielding 3.5%+ annualized, meaning your cash is earning while you wait. In the 1970s stagflation, the best-performing portfolios maintained 15-20% cash reserves and deployed them opportunistically during selloff capitulation events. We are not in capitulation yet (VIX 29 is fear, not panic — panic is VIX 40+), but we want to be ready if it comes.
