# Oracle: the median move misses the tail risk

Oracle reports on Thursday after the close. Its [official announcement](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2026/Oracle-Sets-the-Date-for-its-First-Quarter-Fiscal-Year-2027-Earnings-Announcement/default.aspx) confirms the date and timing.

The options-derived move is about **11.7%**, compared with a median absolute reaction of **10.1%** across the eight historical observations studied. That comparison alone does not establish that options are expensive. The option window also includes Friday’s inflation release.

A simple exercise subtracts each historical absolute reaction from today’s implied-move threshold. Six observations leave a positive balance, but the total is negative because one reaction reached **37.7%**. This is a fixed-premium illustration, not an options backtest: historical premiums, executable spreads, expiration timing, hedging and costs have not been reconstructed.

The large reaction followed the announcement dated September 9, 2025. That is the announcement date, not the reaction-session date. The historical windows run from the session before the announcement to the session after it and can therefore include movement before the release. Two September observations cannot establish seasonality.

## What the recent rise establishes

Oracle rose **12.4%** over three sessions, but **5.3%** over five. The shorter window begins at a monthly closing low. Volume on the three rising sessions ranged from **0.85 to 1.03 times** the recorded average.

That shows no unusual volume acceleration. It does not establish whether buyers were responding to information or adjusting positions. Missing analyst revisions in a collected feed do not establish that no analyst changed their view.

Broadcom was already falling before its announcement. Its pre-announcement monthly subperiod returned **−12.1%**, while the measured window around the release returned **−3.4%** and the full month **−14.9%**. These overlapping or differently anchored windows are not additive percentage-point contributions, and they do not isolate causation.

The three peer reactions examined were Salesforce **+22.5%**, Dell **+7.9%**, and Broadcom **−3.4%**. They are a selected comparison, not an exhaustive list of earnings in the infrastructure chain. NVIDIA also reported during the period. Three outcomes cannot establish that reducing exposure before earnings is a bad risk policy.

## The decision

No directional trade or options sale is proposed before Oracle reports. The eight historical observations split evenly between positive and negative reactions. Their small number provides no reliable directional edge.

For an existing holding, review how a large adverse gap would affect the whole portfolio. The largest observed move is a stress example, not a maximum possible loss. A stop cannot guarantee execution through a gap.

Seven historical reaction sessions closed above their opening prices. This does **not** mean the opening was the session low, nor does it establish a reliable rule for selling at the open.

After the report, assess guidance, the regular-session price response and related companies separately. A move beyond the implied threshold would invalidate a contained-move scenario; it would not prove what caused the rise before the report.

The week also brings US producer prices before consumer prices, followed by the Federal Reserve meeting the following week. Positions held across those events need an appropriate risk budget. Low short-dated implied volatility does not guarantee cheap protection or higher option prices after an event; implied volatility can fall once uncertainty clears.

*Market figures use the US close of September 4, 2026. Historical reactions use the archived event windows described above. Informational only; not investment advice.*
