The Regime Alignment Rule

This week revealed a critical pattern: strategy performance correlates almost perfectly with regime alignment. When the regime is EARLY RISK-OFF and deteriorating, only two strategy types have positive expected value: (1) short squeezes / oversold bounces on fundamentally solid names (HIMS, CF), and (2) pure defensive/commodity plays (GOLD, ADM, KR, WMT). Everything else — momentum, breakout, EU/Asia ETFs — gets stopped. The Mar 10 daytime scan was a textbook example of mistaking a VIX dip for a regime change. It wasn't one. It was a dead-cat bounce.

Lessons & Adjustments for Next Scans

What the Scanner Did Well

  • Regime detection was accurate: correctly identified EARLY RISK-OFF on Mar 9–10 and full RISK-OFF on Mar 12–13.
  • Defensive strategy names outperformed: HIMS, CF, ADM, KR, GOLD, WMT all had positive outcomes when aligned with regime.
  • Mar 12 scan was much cleaner: With full RISK-OFF regime confirmed, the bias correctly shifted to commodity/defensive. GOLD +11% was the reward.
  • Stop placement was generally adequate: losses were contained to 2–7% for most stops (except BBIO’s oversized 12.5%).
  • Hedge setups (SQQQ, SH) are both slightly positive and still running — correct inclusion in a RISK-OFF week.

What the Scanner Got Wrong

  • Mar 10 opportunistic scan: should not fire on intraday VIX dip — no regime change confirmed. Lost 6 positions in 48h.
  • Tech/growth names in EARLY RISK-OFF: ADBE, AMD, ASML, PLTR, SMH, BBIO, ARGX should all be filtered out when VIX >23.
  • EU/Asia ETFs on regime deterioration: EWG, EWJ, FEZ stopped cleanly — EU/Asia are higher beta to US risk-off, not safe havens.
  • GDX/NEM paradox: gold bullion goes up in risk-off, but gold miners have operating leverage to energy costs (oil spike hurts them). GDX −6%, NEM −5.8% while gold hit $5,100. Use GLD not GDX in this regime.
  • BBIO stop was too wide (12.5% risk on a volatile biotech) — cap single-name risk at 8% max stop.

5 Concrete Adjustments for Next Week

  1. No opportunistic intraday scans when VIX > 23: only generate scans when VIX has closed below a threshold for 2 consecutive days, or at scheduled 23:00 slot.
  2. RISK-OFF filter: blacklist high-beta tech: When regime is EARLY RISK-OFF or worse, automatically exclude names with beta > 1.5 and no dividend/commodity link (ADBE, AMD, ASML, PLTR, BBIO, ARGX are examples).
  3. GLD > GDX in oil-spike RISK-OFF: Gold miners have energy cost exposure. When Brent > $90, prefer GLD, GOLD (physical/royalty), or XAU over GDX/GDXJ.
  4. EU/Asia ETFs only in RECOVERY or RISK-ON: EWG, FEZ, EWJ, EWH are not safe havens — they amplify US risk-off. Only include EU/Asia ETFs when the regime is improving, not deteriorating.
  5. Max stop 8% on single names: BBIO’s 12.5% stop was disproportionate. Hard cap all stops at 8% to prevent one position from dominating the P&L.

Lesson for Readers: The Regime Is Not a Badge, It’s a Filter

One of the most instructive takeaways from this week is that the regime label affects not just which sectors to favor, but which strategy types are viable at all. In RISK-ON, momentum and breakout setups have >60% hit rate. In RISK-OFF, they have <20%. This isn’t about picking the right tickers — it’s about the strategy-regime match. Experienced traders know this instinctively: you don’t run momentum strategies into a VIX 28 environment. The scanner has to be smarter about filtering strategy types by regime, not just sector preferences.
