The Production Scanner Architecture

The 5-Stage Pipeline

1 Data Ingestion

Pull market data from your data provider(s). This includes end-of-day prices, volume, fundamentals, insider transactions, options flow, and sentiment data. The ingestion layer handles API rate limits, retries, and data validation.

  • Price data: EOD quotes for entire universe (~3,000 symbols)
  • Fundamentals: Quarterly financials, earnings estimates
  • Sentiment: Short interest, analyst ratings, news sentiment
  • Market regime: VIX, breadth, yield curve, credit spreads

2 Regime Detection

Before running any scanner logic, determine the current market regime. This sets the parameters for everything that follows — which strategies to deploy, position sizing multipliers, and exposure limits.

Output: Regime label (Risk-On, Transition, Risk-Off, Crisis) + confidence level (High/Medium/Low).

3 Filter & Score

Apply the DSL filters (Part 2) to define the candidate universe, then score all candidates (Part 3) using the composite scoring engine. Apply boosts and penalties. This is the core scanner logic.

Output: Ranked list of all scored candidates with grades.

4 Selection & Validation

Select the top N candidates based on grade threshold and diversification rules. Validate each pick: check spot price vs calculated entry (must be within 10%), verify volume is still sufficient, and cross-check for any breaking news that might invalidate the thesis.

Output: Final shortlist of 5-10 A+ picks with entry, stop, and target levels.

5 Output & Alert

Generate the scan report (HTML or PDF), send alerts to configured channels (Discord, email, Telegram), and log all results for retrospective analysis. Archive the full scoring data for audit trail.

Output: Published scan report + notifications + historical log entry.

Fail-Safe Design

Production systems fail. APIs go down, data feeds have gaps, your server reboots. Design your pipeline with fail-safe defaults:

  • If data ingestion fails: Don't run the scanner. Better no picks than picks based on stale data.
  • If regime detection fails: Default to Transition (cautious) with low confidence (0.5x exposure).
  • If scoring produces < 3 A+ picks: Report as "no actionable setups" rather than lowering the bar.
  • If alerting fails: Log to local file and retry. Don't trade without reviewing the output.

The golden rule: when in doubt, do nothing. No trade is always better than a bad trade based on faulty data.

Why Run at Night?

Running at night (after market close) has three advantages: (1) EOD data is complete and finalized — no partial candles or delayed feeds. (2) You have the entire overnight period to review results and prepare orders before market open. (3) Intraday scanning generates excessive noise — daily timeframe signals are more reliable for swing trading setups.

Some scanners also run a pre-market refresh at 7 AM ET to check for overnight news that might invalidate setups (earnings miss, geopolitical event, pre-market gap). This is a quick validation pass, not a full re-scan.

Data Quality Checks

Every data ingestion run should pass through quality checks before the scanner processes it. These are your data circuit breakers:

  • Completeness: Are all symbols in the universe present? Missing symbols = missed opportunities or stale positions.
  • Freshness: Is the latest bar from today? Stale data produces yesterday's picks with today's label.
  • Range validation: RSI between 0-100? Price > 0? Volume > 0? Catches data feed corruption.
  • Outlier detection: A stock that moved +500% in a day is either wrong data or an extraordinary event. Flag for manual review.
  • Cross-validation: Compare price from two independent sources. If they diverge by more than 1%, something is wrong.
Data Quality Rule
If quality_score < 95% → Abort scan and alert operator

The Backup Data Source

Always have a secondary data source configured. If your primary provider goes down (it happens more often than you'd expect), the pipeline should automatically fall back to the secondary source rather than skipping the scan entirely. Log the fallback event for investigation the next day. The key metrics to verify on fallback: price accuracy (<0.5% divergence from primary) and universe coverage (>95% of symbols available).

Monitoring & Debugging

Keeping Your Scanner Healthy

A production scanner is a living system. It can degrade silently — data quality drops, a signal stops working, market structure changes. Without monitoring, you won't notice until your P&L tells you, and by then the damage is done.

When to Pull the Kill Switch

Stop the scanner and investigate if any of these occur:

  • Rolling 14-day hit rate drops below 40%
  • Three consecutive scans produce 0 A+ picks (unless Crisis regime)
  • Data completeness below 90% for 2+ consecutive days
  • Max drawdown exceeds backtest worst-case by 50%
  • Scanner picks correlate > 0.8 with simple SPY buy-and-hold (no alpha being generated)

Pulling the kill switch is not failure — it's prudent risk management. Better to pause for a week of debugging than to bleed capital into a broken system.

Retrospective Analysis

The Weekly Retrospective

The retrospective is the most important process in running a production scanner. It closes the feedback loop: the scanner generates picks, the market delivers outcomes, and you analyze the delta. Without retrospectives, your scanner is static — it will inevitably degrade as markets evolve.

Retrospective Structure (45 minutes weekly)

SectionContentsTime
1. Results DashboardHit rate, avg P&L, best/worst picks, profit factor for the week5 min
2. Pick-by-Pick ReviewFor each pick: entry, current price, TP1 hit?, stop hit?, still open15 min
3. Strategy AnalysisHit rate by strategy type (momentum, value, breakout, short)5 min
4. Regime AccuracyWas the regime classifier correct? Did strategy allocation match?5 min
5. Lessons & AdjustmentsWhat worked, what didn't, parameter changes for next week10 min
6. Forward LookRegime outlook, earnings calendar, macro events, adjusted strategy5 min

The 5 Rules of Retrospectives

  1. Never skip it. Even when you're winning — especially when you're winning. Winning streaks breed complacency, and the retro catches brewing problems before they explode.
  2. Be brutally honest. Don't rationalize losers ("it would have worked if not for that tweet"). The market is always right. Your job is to understand why, not to argue.
  3. Track cumulative patterns. A single losing trade is noise. The same strategy losing 4 weeks in a row is a signal that demands action.
  4. Don't over-adjust. If last week's retro says "tighten stops," don't widen them this week because one tight stop was premature. Wait for 3+ data points before making parameter changes.
  5. Document everything. Every retro goes into a permanent log. In 6 months, you'll look back and understand exactly when and why your scanner evolved.

Cumulative Performance Tracking

Beyond weekly retros, track cumulative performance metrics that reveal the scanner's health over longer horizons:

  • Rolling 30-day hit rate — Should stay in the 55-65% corridor. Drift below 50% = investigate immediately.
  • Rolling 30-day Sharpe — Should stay above 1.0. Below 0.5 = the scanner is not generating alpha.
  • Hit rate by grade bracket — A+ should always outperform A, which outperforms B+. If this ordering breaks, scoring calibration is off.
  • Hit rate by regime — Monitors regime detection accuracy. If Risk-On hit rate matches Risk-Off hit rate, regime detection isn't working.
  • Unique tickers ratio — Are you scanning the whole market or recycling the same 20 names? Target: > 60% unique tickers month-over-month.
Continuous Improvement

A production scanner is never "done." Markets evolve, correlations shift, new data sources become available, and your own trading style matures. The improvement flywheel ensures your scanner keeps pace with these changes.

A/B Testing Scanner Variants

When you want to test a change (new signal, different weight, adjusted threshold), don't just apply it globally. Run two variants in parallel and compare results over 30+ trading days.

ElementVariant A (Control)Variant B (Test)
DescriptionCurrent production scannerProduction + new insider buying signal
Duration30 trading days minimum30 trading days minimum
Capital allocation80% of normal20% of normal (or paper trade)
TrackingHit rate, PF, Sharpe, MDDSame metrics, tracked separately
Decision ruleBaseline performancePromote if improvement > 10%

The 10% Improvement Threshold

Don't deploy a variant just because it's marginally better. Require a meaningful improvement (at least 10% better on key metrics) before promoting it to production. Small improvements may be noise — statistical fluctuations that don't represent genuine edge. Additionally, the variant must perform well across all walk-forward windows in a re-backtest, not just the recent 30-day test period.

Be especially cautious about adding complexity. A variant with 2 extra signals that improves hit rate by 3% but makes the scanner harder to maintain and debug is probably not worth deploying. Simplicity has compounding value in production systems — every additional dependency is a potential point of failure.

Scaling Considerations

As your scanner matures and proves its edge, you may want to scale along several dimensions. The critical rule: scale one dimension at a time with a 30-day validation period between changes.

If you scale multiple dimensions simultaneously and performance degrades, you won't know which change caused the problem. One dimension at a time, with validation between each step, gives you a clear audit trail and easy rollback path.

Knowledge Check

Test Your Understanding

Quiz 1: Your data feed fails at 10 PM. What should the scanner do?
Answer: Abort the scan and alert the operator. Never run the scanner on stale or missing data. The fail-safe principle is "when in doubt, do nothing." Send a critical alert (SMS + Discord) so you can investigate. Try the backup data source if configured. If backup also fails, skip tonight's scan entirely. No scan is always better than a scan based on yesterday's data labeled as today's.
Quiz 2: Why run the scanner at night instead of during market hours?
Answer: Three reasons: (1) Data completeness — EOD bars are finalized after market close, eliminating partial candles and delayed feeds. (2) Review time — Running at night gives you the overnight period to review results, check overnight news, and prepare orders for market open. (3) Signal quality — Daily timeframe signals (RSI, MACD, volume) are far more reliable than intraday signals, which generate excessive noise and false positives for swing trading.
Quiz 3: Your scanner's rolling 30-day hit rate dropped from 62% to 48%. What do you do?
Answer: This crosses the warning-to-critical threshold. Systematic response: (1) Check the regime classifier — did the market change regimes without detection? (2) Audit data quality — are there feed issues degrading signal accuracy? (3) Review recent losers — look for patterns (same sector, same strategy type, same signal failing). (4) Pull the kill switch if you can't identify the root cause within 1-2 days. (5) Re-run walk-forward backtest with the most recent 6 months to check if the scanner's edge has structurally decayed. Never keep trading a broken system while you investigate.
Quiz 4: What are the 5 rules of a good retrospective?
Answer: (1) Never skip it — even when winning, especially when winning. (2) Be brutally honest — don't rationalize losers or attribute losses to bad luck. (3) Track cumulative patterns — one bad trade is noise; four weeks of the same strategy failing is a signal. (4) Don't over-adjust — wait for 3+ data points before changing parameters. Knee-jerk reactions introduce instability. (5) Document everything — every retro goes into a permanent log for long-term pattern analysis and institutional memory.
Quiz 5: You have a new insider buying signal that improved backtest results by 8%. Should you deploy it?
Answer: Not yet. An 8% improvement is below the 10% threshold for promoting a variant to production. Steps: (1) Run it as Variant B in a 30-day A/B test with 20% capital allocation (or paper trade). (2) Verify the improvement holds across all walk-forward windows, not just the recent period. (3) Assess operational complexity — does the new signal add a data dependency that could break the pipeline? Only promote if the improvement is sustained, statistically significant, and operationally manageable. A simple scanner that works reliably beats a complex scanner that breaks unpredictably.
Quiz 6: What's the danger of scaling multiple dimensions simultaneously?
Answer: Each scaling step (larger universe, more strategies, higher frequency, more team members) introduces new failure modes. If you expand the universe AND add new strategies AND increase frequency simultaneously, and performance degrades, you won't know which change caused the problem. This makes debugging nearly impossible and rollback ambiguous. Scale one dimension at a time with a 30-day validation period between changes. If something breaks, you know exactly what caused it and can surgically roll back that specific change.
Key Takeaways

Part 6 — Key Takeaways

  • 5-stage pipeline — Data ingestion, regime detection, filter & score, selection & validation, output & alert. Each stage has quality checks and fail-safes.
  • Run at night, review in the morning — EOD data is clean, overnight gives review time, daily signals are more reliable than intraday.
  • Data quality is non-negotiable — If quality drops below 95%, abort the scan. Bad data produces bad picks. Always have a backup data source.
  • Multi-channel alerting with tiered urgency — Critical alerts via SMS, daily results via Discord, performance via dashboard. Tiered urgency prevents alert fatigue.
  • Monitor health continuously — Track data completeness, execution time, candidate count, rolling hit rate, and score distribution. Pull the kill switch when metrics breach critical thresholds.
  • Weekly retrospectives are mandatory — The feedback loop is what makes a scanner improve over time. Never skip it. Never rationalize losers.
  • A/B test before deploying changes — 30-day parallel test, 10% improvement threshold, verified across all walk-forward windows before promotion.
  • Scale one dimension at a time — Universe, strategies, frequency, team. Validate each expansion independently with 30-day checkpoints.
