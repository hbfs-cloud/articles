Introduction: The Age of Scarcity

The era of abundant cheap goods, cheap labor, and cheap capital is over. We are entering a decade defined by structural shortages. Understanding this shift is the key to generating alpha in the 2020s.

Supply Shock Inflation CAPEX Cycle Pricing Power

The Paradigm Shift

For the last 40 years (1980-2020), investors benefited from a powerful tailwind: globalization. Supply chains were optimized for efficiency, labor was abundant, and energy was cheap. "Just-in-time" was the mantra.

That era ended with COVID-19 and the geopolitical fractures that followed. We are now in a "Just-in-case" world. Resilience is replacing efficiency. This transition is creating massive bottlenecks across the global economy.

What is Scarcity Alpha?

Scarcity Alpha is the excess return generated by investing in assets that are in structural deficit. When demand exceeds supply for a prolonged period, prices rise, margins expand, and companies controlling the scarce resource see their valuations re-rated.

The Framework: Severity & Investability

Not all shortages are investable. A shortage of toilet paper is temporary and low-margin. A shortage of high-end GPUs is structural and high-margin. We evaluate every shortage on two axes:

Severity (1-10)
How deep is the deficit? How long to bring new supply online? Is demand inelastic?
Investability (High/Med/Low)
Are there liquid stocks/ETFs? Is the exposure pure or diluted? Is the valuation reasonable?

Historical Precedents

Scarcity has always been a driver of massive wealth transfer.

  • Oil (1970s): The OPEC embargo created a supply shock. Energy stocks outperformed everything for a decade.
  • Internet Infrastructure (1990s): The shortage of fiber and routers drove Cisco to become the most valuable company in the world.
  • China Commodities Boom (2000s): China's urbanization created a shortage of steel, copper, and coal. Mining stocks 10x'd.

The 2020s Difference

Today's shortages are concurrent. We don't just have an energy shortage; we have a shortage of chips, labor, copper, and grid capacity at the same time. This creates a "poly-crisis" but also a "poly-opportunity" for astute investors.
