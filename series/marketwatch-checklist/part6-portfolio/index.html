Foundation

Core vs Satellite: The 60/40 That Actually Works

Forget the classic 60/40 stocks-bonds split. For an active swing trader, the real 60/40 is systematic core positions (60%) vs tactical/opportunistic satellite positions (40%). The core keeps you in the game when you are wrong. The satellite is where alpha lives.

Core — 60%

Systematic positions from the scanner. High-conviction setups with 3+ confluence signals. Held for the full swing cycle (5-15 days). ATR-sized, stop-protected.

Satellite — 25%

Tactical trades based on regime shifts, sector rotation, or event catalysts. Shorter duration (2-5 days). Tighter stops, smaller size. This is where you express a macro view.

Hedge — 10%

Inverse ETFs, puts, or VIX exposure. Only deployed in Early Risk-Off or Risk-Off regimes. This bucket is often 0% in Risk-On — and that is correct.

Cash — 5%

Minimum cash buffer. In Risk-Off this can swell to 30-40%. Cash is a position. Deploying 100% of capital is a statement that you know the future — you do not.

Why 60/40 and Not 80/20?

Concentration kills in regime transitions. When the market flipped from Risk-On to Risk-Off in March 2026, a portfolio with 80% core systematic positions lost 12% in a single week. The same portfolio at 60% core with a 10% hedge bucket lost 4%. The satellite positions, being shorter duration, had already been closed before the damage compounded.

The 40% non-core allocation is not waste — it is optionality. It lets you respond to what the market is doing right now, not what it was doing when you built your weekly shortlist.

Sector Distribution

Sector Allocation: Beyond "What's Hot"

A portfolio concentrated in one sector is a bet, not a strategy. Even if tech is leading, having 70% tech exposure means a single sector rotation wipes out your quarter. The rule: no single sector above 25% of total portfolio, minimum 4 sectors represented.

Sector Risk-On Weight Neutral Weight Risk-Off Weight Why
Technology 20-25% 15-20% 5-10% High beta, leads in expansion, bleeds first in contraction
Healthcare 10-15% 15-20% 20-25% Defensive with upside. Biotech subset for satellites only
Energy / Commodities 10-15% 10-15% 15-20% Inflation hedge, low correlation to tech, DXY-sensitive
Financials 15-20% 10-15% 5-10% Rate-sensitive. Strong in early cycle, weak in credit stress
Consumer / Industrial 10-15% 15-20% 10-15% Economic bellwethers. Consumer staples shine in Risk-Off
ETFs / Macro 5-10% 10-15% 25-30% VIX, TLT, GLD, inverse ETFs. Portfolio-level risk tools

Sector Drift Is Silent

You do not decide to over-concentrate. It happens. Your scanner finds 6 tech setups and 2 healthcare setups, you pick the best 8, and suddenly you are 60% tech. Check sector exposure after building the shortlist, not before. If you breach 25%, drop the weakest setup from the overweight sector — even if it scores higher than alternatives.

Geographic Spread

Geographic Diversification: US, EU, Asia, ETFs

US markets dominate scanner output because liquidity is deepest, data is best, and coverage is widest. But US-only portfolios are correlated to a single monetary policy, a single currency, and a single political cycle. Geographic diversification is not optional — it is structural protection.

Minimum Allocation Rules
US: 50-70%  |  EU: 15-25%  |  Asia/EM: 5-15%  |  Global ETFs: 5-15%
US

United States

Core allocation. Deepest liquidity, best scanner coverage. NYSE, NASDAQ. Where most systematic setups originate. Cap at 70% to avoid single-country risk.

EU

Europe

Mandatory minimum 15%. Euronext, Xetra, LSE. Different rate cycle, different sector composition (luxury, industrials, banking). Often decorrelated from US tech moves.

AP

Asia-Pacific

5-15% via direct equities or ETFs (EWJ, FXI, INDA). Different trading hours create information gaps the scanner can exploit. Semiconductor supply chain exposure.

GL

Global ETFs

Broad exposure vehicles: VT, VXUS, EEM. Use as satellite positions when you have a macro thesis but lack single-stock conviction in a region.

The March 2026 Lesson

When US markets dropped 8% on tariff escalation fears, European defense and energy stocks gained 3-5%. A portfolio with zero EU exposure absorbed the full US drawdown. The same portfolio with 20% EU allocation limited the drawdown by nearly a third — not because EU hedged perfectly, but because the correlation between US tech selloffs and EU industrials was close to zero during that specific week.

Correlation

Correlation Management: Not All Moving Together

The worst portfolio failure mode is not picking bad stocks — it is picking good stocks that are all correlated. When everything drops together, diversification is an illusion. Manage correlation explicitly, not implicitly.

Max Pairwise Correlation

No two positions should have a 90-day rolling correlation above 0.75. If NVDA and AMD are both on the shortlist, pick one — they move together 80%+ of the time.

Beta Budgeting

Portfolio weighted beta should stay between 0.8 and 1.3 in Risk-On, and below 0.7 in Risk-Off. If your beta is 1.5, you are leveraged to the market without leverage.

Factor Exposure Check

Are all your picks growth stocks? All small-cap? All high-short-interest? Cluster your positions by factor and ensure no single factor dominates more than 40% of capital.

Correlation Breaks in Stress

In normal markets, GLD and SPY correlate near 0. In panic selloffs, everything correlates to 1 temporarily. Plan for stressed correlations when sizing hedges — your diversification will underperform when you need it most.

Practical Correlation Check

Before finalizing the weekly portfolio, ask one question: "If SPX drops 3% tomorrow, how many of my positions lose money?" If the answer is "all of them," you do not have a portfolio — you have a leveraged SPX bet with extra steps. Target: no more than 65% of positions should be directionally correlated to the broad market.

The Transition Trap

The hardest moment is the regime transition itself. Going from Risk-On (60% core) to Risk-Off (25% core) means liquidating 35% of your portfolio. Do not do it in one day. Spread over 2-3 sessions. Sell the weakest setups first. Keep the ones with the widest stops (most room to breathe). The scanner from Part 3 will stop generating aggressive setups naturally — let the pipeline dry up rather than forcing exits.

Pipeline

The Watchlist Pipeline: Scanner to Portfolio

The scanner (Part 3) generates raw candidates. The portfolio construction process filters them into deployable positions. This is a funnel, not a selection — and every stage has explicit rules.

1

Scanner Output

200+ tickers scanned. 40-60 pass initial filters. Raw candidates with composite scores.

2

Technical Validation

Manual chart review. Confluence check: 3+ signals required. 60% elimination rate. Down to 15-20 candidates.

3

Portfolio Filter

Sector cap (25%), geo minimum (15% non-US), correlation check (max 0.75 pairwise), factor diversity. Down to 8-10.

4

Risk Gate

Earnings calendar check (no ±5 days), MCap floor ($5B in Risk-Off), position sizing via ATR. Final 6-10 positions.

5

Staging

Top 6 go to the Long List (published). Next 4 go to Short List (reserves). If a Long List setup invalidates, promote from Short List.

6

Deployment

Size each position using ATR-based formula from Part 5. Set stops. Set TP1 and TP2. Enter when price reaches the defined entry zone.

Rebalancing

Rebalancing Rules: Three Triggers

Rebalancing is not "sell winners, buy losers." It is the process of keeping the portfolio aligned with the rules defined above. Three triggers, each independent.

Time-Based (Weekly)

Every Sunday during regime analysis: check sector weights, geographic mix, and correlation matrix. If any rule is breached, flag for Monday adjustment. This is the primary rebalancing trigger.

Threshold-Based (Intra-Week)

If any position grows to >7% of portfolio (from gains) or any sector breaches 30%, trigger a partial trim. Do not wait for Sunday — drift this large compounds risk daily.

Event-Driven (Immediate)

Regime change (score crosses a threshold boundary), unexpected earnings, major macro event (rate decision, geopolitical shock). These trigger immediate position review — not panic selling, but structured reassessment against the portfolio rules.

Rebalancing Decision Tree
Breach detected → Is it >2% over limit? → YES: trim same day  |  NO: flag for Sunday review

Guardrails

Anti-Concentration Rules: Hard Limits

These are non-negotiable constraints. They exist because every concentration-related loss in the retrospective was caused by a rule that existed "in spirit" but not as a hard limit. Now they are hard limits.

No single position > 5%

At entry. If a position appreciates to 7%+, trim back to 5%. No exceptions, not even for your highest-conviction trade. Conviction is not a risk management tool.

No single sector > 25%

Measured by capital deployed, not by number of positions. Three positions at 8% each in tech = 24%, fine. Four = 32%, breach. Drop the weakest.

No geographic zone > 70%

US exposure naturally drifts toward 80-90% because the scanner finds more US setups. Cap at 70% and force at least one EU or Asia position per week.

Max 10 open positions

Attention is finite. Beyond 10 positions, monitoring quality degrades, stop management slips, and average conviction per position drops below the threshold where sizing makes sense.

Max pairwise correlation 0.75

If two positions correlate above 0.75 on 90-day rolling, treat them as one position for concentration purposes. Either drop one or halve the size of each.

Zero duplicates across lists

A ticker on the Long List cannot appear on the Short List. No ticker from the previous week's list can reappear unless it is a genuinely new setup (different entry zone, different thesis).

Cash Is Not Laziness

In the Risk-Off portfolio, 60% cash looks like doing nothing. It is not. It is a deliberate decision to preserve capital when the expected value of new positions is negative. The scanner in Risk-Off mode generates fewer candidates, with lower scores, and shorter durations. Deploying capital into weak setups to "stay busy" is the single most expensive mistake a swing trader can make. The cash will be deployed aggressively when the regime flips — and it will flip.

Summary

Key Takeaways

Building the Resilient Machine

  • 60% core, 25% satellite, 10% hedge, 5% cash — this split adapts by regime but the structure is permanent. Core positions come from the scanner. Satellites are tactical. Hedges activate in Risk-Off.
  • No single sector above 25% — sector drift is silent and lethal. Check after building the shortlist, not before. Drop the weakest overweight setup even if it scores highest.
  • Minimum 15% non-US exposure — geographic diversification is structural protection. Different rate cycles, different currencies, different political risks. Force at least one non-US position per week.
  • Correlation is the hidden risk — five great stocks in the same factor cluster are one bet, not five. Max pairwise correlation 0.75. If SPX drops 3%, no more than 65% of positions should lose.
  • Rebalancing has three triggers — weekly (Sunday review), threshold (intra-week breach >2%), and event-driven (regime change). Each trigger has its own response protocol.
  • Cash is a position — 60% cash in Risk-Off is not laziness, it is the highest-conviction trade you can make when expected value is negative. The capital deploys when the regime turns.
