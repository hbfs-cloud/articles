Why Scanning Is Not Enough

The False Positive Problem

A well-built scanner can surface 20-40 candidates per day. That is its job — cast a wide net. But if you trade every candidate the scanner finds, you will lose money. Why? Because scanners optimize for recall (finding every possible setup), not precision (finding only the setups that work).

From our own data across 90+ setups analyzed since February 2026, the scanner's raw output has a hit rate of roughly 35%. After applying the 7-point validation checklist, the hit rate jumps to 58%. That is the difference between a losing system and a profitable one.

Scanner Output

30-40 candidates daily. High recall, low precision.

Validation Filter

7-point checklist. Kills 70-80% of candidates.

Final Shortlist

5-8 high-conviction setups per day.

Executed Trades

2-4 positions after sizing and portfolio fit.

The Costly Mistake

In early March 2026, our scanner flagged SMCI as a momentum breakout candidate. RSI was rising, volume was above average, and the stock had cleared a short-term resistance. We skipped validation because "the chart looked obvious." The stock dropped 18% in three sessions on dilution fears and insider selling — two red flags the checklist would have caught in under 60 seconds.

The 7-Point Entry Checklist

Seven Questions Before Every Trade

Every candidate that survives the scanner must answer seven questions. A score of 5/7 or higher means the setup is worth sizing. Below 5, it goes back on the watchlist — or gets discarded entirely. No exceptions, no "gut feeling" overrides.

1

Trend Alignment

The higher timeframe must confirm the direction of the trade. A long setup on the daily chart is invalid if the weekly trend is clearly bearish. Check: is the weekly EMA21 rising? Is price above the monthly EMA50? If you are buying a pullback, the primary trend must still be intact. A bullish setup inside a bearish macro trend has a 31% hit rate in our data — not worth touching.

PASS: Weekly uptrend, daily pullback to EMA21, monthly above EMA50
FAIL: Daily bullish signal but weekly making lower highs and lower lows
2

Support / Resistance Levels

Is the price at a meaningful level — not in no-man's land? The best entries happen at confluence zones: a horizontal S/R level that aligns with a moving average, a Fibonacci retracement, or a prior volume shelf. If price is floating between two levels with no clear anchor, the risk/reward becomes impossible to define cleanly. Our data shows setups at key S/R levels have a 64% hit rate vs. 41% for "in-between" entries.

PASS: Price touching EMA50 which aligns with horizontal support at $142
FAIL: Price at $156, midway between $148 support and $163 resistance
3

Volume Confirmation

Is smart money participating, or is this a low-conviction drift? Volume is the lie detector of technical analysis. A breakout on declining volume is a trap. A reversal candle on 3x average volume is institutional accumulation. Check the volume profile: is there a vacuum above (thin volume = fast move potential) or a wall (high volume node = resistance)? We require at least 1.5x the 20-day average volume on the signal day for breakout setups.

PASS: Breakout candle with 2.8x average volume, volume increasing for 3 sessions
FAIL: "Breakout" on 0.7x average volume — likely a liquidity gap, not demand
4

Catalyst Presence

What will make this stock move NOW? A technically perfect setup without a catalyst is a stock that can sit at support for months doing nothing — tying up capital. Catalysts include: upcoming earnings (within 2-6 weeks), FDA decisions, M&A rumors backed by filings, sector rotation confirmed by ETF flows, or macro events (rate decisions, CPI). The catalyst does not need to be public yet — sometimes the volume IS the catalyst (institutional accumulation ahead of news).

PASS: Earnings in 3 weeks, sector ETF inflows accelerating, unusual options activity
FAIL: No earnings for 8 weeks, no sector catalyst, flat options flow
5

Risk/Reward Ratio ≥ 1:1.5

This is non-negotiable. The distance to the target (TP1) divided by the distance to the stop loss must be at least 1.5. We calculate this using ATR-based stops, not arbitrary round numbers. If the stop is $5 away, the first target must be at least $7.50 away. Setups with R:R below 1.5 are filtered out regardless of how "clean" the chart looks. With a 58% hit rate and 1.5 R:R minimum, the system is mathematically profitable.

PASS: Entry $50, stop $47 (1.5 ATR), TP1 $54.50 — R:R = 1:1.5
FAIL: Entry $50, stop $46 (logical level), TP1 $53 — R:R = 1:0.75
6

Stop Loss Is Clear and Logical

The stop must be placed at a level where the thesis is genuinely invalidated — not at an arbitrary percentage or dollar amount. A logical stop sits below a support level, below the EMA that defines the trend, or below the low of the signal candle. If you cannot identify a clear invalidation level, the setup is not tradeable. Arbitrary stops (e.g., "I will risk 2%") lead to getting stopped out on noise and then watching the stock rally.

PASS: Stop below the daily EMA50 at $93.20 — if price breaks this, the uptrend thesis is dead
FAIL: "I will set my stop 3% below entry" — no structural justification
7

No Red Flags

The final gate. Even if a setup scores 6/6 on the above, a single red flag kills the trade. Red flags include: recent dilution (shelf offering filed), significant insider selling in the past 90 days, pending regulatory investigation, stock on the SEC threshold list (heavy short interest + FTDs), earnings within 5 days (too binary), or the company has a history of missing consensus estimates. This is the "trust but verify" step — and it has saved us from at least 4 catastrophic trades.

PASS: Clean SEC filings, no insider selling, no dilution risk, earnings in 4 weeks
FAIL: S-3 shelf registration filed 2 weeks ago, CEO sold $12M in stock last month

Scoring the Checklist

Each point scores 1 (pass) or 0 (fail). 5/7 minimum to trade. A score of 7/7 is rare but represents the highest conviction setups — these get full position size. A 5/7 gets half size. A 6/7 gets 75% size. Below 5, the setup does not enter the portfolio regardless of how good it "feels."

Point #7 (Red Flags) is a hard veto — if it fails, the score is automatically 0/7 regardless of other points. You cannot compensate for dilution risk with a clean chart.

Why Some Patterns Fail

The underperforming patterns share a common trait: they take too long to complete. A cup and handle that forms over 6 months has been seen by every algo, every fund, every retail trader on Twitter. By the time it "breaks out," the move is already priced in. The patterns that work — bull flags, EMA bounces, gap-and-holds — resolve quickly (3-10 days) before crowding can erode the edge.

Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Monthly → Weekly → Daily → 4H

Single-timeframe analysis is like reading one paragraph of a book and thinking you understand the story. Each timeframe serves a specific purpose in the validation process:

Monthly
The Macro Trend

Is this stock in a secular uptrend or a structural decline? EMA50 direction tells you.

Weekly
The Swing Trend

Is the intermediate trend healthy? Look for higher lows, EMA21 support, RSI above 40.

Daily
The Setup

This is where you identify the pattern, the entry zone, and the invalidation level.

4-Hour
The Trigger

Fine-tune entry timing. Wait for a reclaim, a volume spike, or a candle close above the level.

The Timeframe Alignment Rule

A valid setup requires alignment on at least 3 of 4 timeframes. If monthly is bearish but daily and 4H look bullish, you are fighting the tide. If monthly, weekly, and daily all agree but 4H has not triggered yet — wait. Patience is the cheapest form of risk management.

What to Check on Each Timeframe

Monthly Checklist

Is EMA50 rising or falling? Is price above or below it? Is RSI above 40 (for longs)? Has the stock made a higher low in the last 3 months? A monthly downtrend takes 6-12 months to reverse — do not fight it on lower timeframes.

Weekly Checklist

Is the weekly EMA21 rising? Is price making higher lows? Is the weekly RSI above 50? Is there a recognizable pattern (flag, base, wedge)? The weekly timeframe defines the swing trade thesis — most of our setups play out over 1-4 weekly candles.

Daily Checklist

Where is the setup relative to daily EMAs (8, 21, 50)? Is there a clean entry zone (pullback to EMA, breakout level, support test)? What does the daily RSI say — overbought already, or room to run? The daily chart is where the setup lives — this is your primary analysis timeframe.

4-Hour Checklist

Has the 4H candle closed above the key level (not just a wick)? Is there a volume spike on the trigger candle? Has the intraday noise settled — or is the stock whipsawing? The 4H is purely for timing. It answers one question: is the moment NOW, or should I wait one more session?

The Top-Down Sequence Matters

Always start from the highest timeframe and work down. If you start on the 4H chart, you will find bullish setups in stocks that are in monthly downtrends. The monthly chart sets the bias (long or short), the weekly confirms the swing, the daily defines the setup, and the 4H triggers the entry. Never reverse the sequence. A bottom-up approach produces the illusion of precision while missing the macro context entirely.

Volume Profile Analysis

Where Is the Real Support?

Traditional support levels are drawn on price charts — horizontal lines at prior lows, round numbers, moving averages. Volume profile reveals something deeper: where the most shares actually changed hands. A price level where 50 million shares traded over 6 months is far stronger support than a level where price bounced once on 2 million shares.

High Volume Node (HVN)

Price level where heavy trading occurred. Acts as a magnet — price tends to gravitate here.

Low Volume Node (LVN)

Price level with thin volume. Price moves fast through these zones — opportunity or danger.

Point of Control (POC)

The single price with the most volume. The market's "fair value" — strongest support/resistance.

Value Area (VA)

Range containing 70% of volume. Trading within VA = range-bound. Breaking VA = new trend.

How We Use Volume Profile in Validation

When validating a setup, we check: is the proposed stop loss below a High Volume Node? If yes, the stop is at a level where buyers have historically stepped in — it is a logical stop. If the stop is in a Low Volume Node, price could slice through it on a wick and recover, stopping you out unnecessarily.

Similarly, we check: is there a Low Volume Node above the entry? If yes, a breakout could move fast through that vacuum — higher R:R potential. If there is a massive HVN above, the stock will likely stall there — lower R:R, potentially not worth the trade.

Reading the Volume Map — A Practical Example

Consider a stock trading at $85 after pulling back from $95. Traditional analysis sees "support at $80" because that is where price bounced three months ago. But the volume profile tells a different story:

The second target is the HVN resistance at $92-95 — where you take partial profits because the stock will struggle there.

Volume Profile Tools

Most charting platforms offer volume profile as a built-in or add-on feature. TradingView has the "Visible Range Volume Profile" (VPVR) — the most useful variant. Set it to display the last 6 months of data for swing trades. For day trades, use the session volume profile. The key metric to watch is the POC (Point of Control) — the single price level with the highest volume. Think of it as the market's consensus on fair value.

The Confirmation / Invalidation Framework

Green Box / Red Box

Before entering any trade, we define two zones on the chart: the Green Box (confirmation zone) and the Red Box (invalidation zone). This removes emotion from the decision. Price either enters the Green Box and we act, or it enters the Red Box and we move on.

Green Box — Confirmation

  • Price closes above the breakout level (not just a wick)
  • Volume on the breakout day is ≥ 1.5x the 20-day average
  • The breakout holds for at least one full session (no same-day reversal)
  • RSI is between 55-75 (not overbought, but showing momentum)
  • No earnings within the next 5 trading days

Red Box — Invalidation

  • Price closes below the key support level identified in the setup
  • Volume spikes on a red candle (distribution, not accumulation)
  • The higher timeframe (weekly) breaks its trend structure
  • A red flag emerges after the scan (insider selling, dilution filing)
  • The macro regime shifts to Risk-Off after the setup was identified
The Decision Framework
Price enters Green Box = Execute with defined size   |   Price enters Red Box = Delete from watchlist

Why Pre-Defining Matters

The Green Box / Red Box must be defined before the market opens. If you define them in real-time while watching price action, your brain will rationalize staying in a bad trade. "It is just a wick." "Volume was low on that drop." "It will come back." Pre-definition removes the negotiation with your own psychology.

Key Takeaways

What to Remember from Part 4

  • Scanning finds candidates, validation finds trades. A raw scanner output has a ~35% hit rate. After the 7-point checklist, it rises to 58%.
  • 7 questions, no exceptions: trend alignment, S/R level, volume, catalyst, R:R ≥ 1.5, logical stop, no red flags. Score 5/7+ to trade.
  • Point #7 is a hard veto. Red flags (dilution, insider selling, regulatory risk) kill the trade regardless of chart quality.
  • Multi-timeframe alignment is mandatory. 3 of 4 timeframes must agree (monthly, weekly, daily, 4H). Full alignment (4/4) has a 72% hit rate.
  • Patterns that work are fast patterns. Bull flags, EMA bounces, and gap-and-holds outperform slow patterns (cup & handle, wedges) by 20+ percentage points.
  • Volume profile reveals true support. Place stops below High Volume Nodes, target moves through Low Volume Nodes.
  • Green Box / Red Box before the open. Pre-define confirmation and invalidation zones. If you define them in real-time, your brain will cheat.
