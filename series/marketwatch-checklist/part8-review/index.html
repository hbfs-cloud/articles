Section 1

Why Review Is the Most Neglected — and Most Valuable — Step

Every trader loves scanning for setups. Most enjoy executing. Almost nobody enjoys sitting down to review what happened. That asymmetry is exactly why review is where the alpha lives.

Consider two traders with identical strategies. Trader A executes for 12 months and never looks back. Trader B spends 30 minutes every Sunday reviewing the week. After a year, Trader B has made 52 micro-adjustments. The compound effect is staggering.

The Compound Improvement Formula
1.0152 = 1.68x — 1% improvement per week = 68% better in one year

The Review Paradox

The traders who need review the most are the ones who avoid it the most. When you're losing, reviewing feels painful. When you're winning, it feels unnecessary. In both cases, the discipline of scheduled review breaks the emotional cycle and replaces it with data-driven adaptation.

Key insight: Review is not about feeling bad for losses. It's about extracting signal from noise. Every trade — win or loss — contains information. Your job is to collect that information systematically.

Section 2

The Trading Journal: What to Log and Why

A trading journal is not a diary. It's a structured database of decisions, outcomes, and context. The goal is to make every future decision better informed by every past decision.

The 10 Fields of a Complete Trade Log

1

Entry Date & Price

When you entered, at what price, and what triggered the entry

2

Exit Date & Price

When you exited, the price, and whether it was at TP, SL, or manual

3

Thesis

Your written reasoning at the time of entry — not hindsight

4

Market Regime

Bull, bear, or range? Risk-on or risk-off? VIX level?

5

Position Size & Risk

% of portfolio, dollar risk, R-multiple at exit

6

Emotions

Confidence level 1-5, FOMO flag, revenge flag, fatigue flag

7

Screenshots

Chart at entry and exit — your future self will thank you

8

Setup Type

Which scanner pattern triggered this? Breakout, pullback, reversal?

9

Grade (A+ to F)

Execution quality regardless of outcome — did you follow the plan?

10

Lesson Learned

One sentence. What will you do differently next time?

Survivorship Bias Warning

The most dangerous distortion in your own track record is ignoring skipped trades. If your scanner flagged 20 setups and you only took 8, your hit rate is based on a biased sample. Log every setup your system generated — taken or not — and track what happened. The setups you skipped might reveal your best (or worst) instincts.

Minimum Viable Journal

If a full 10-field journal feels overwhelming, start with the MVJ: Ticker, Date, Direction, Entry, Exit, R-Multiple, Grade (A-F), One-Line Lesson. You can always add fields later. The most important thing is consistency — a simple journal maintained daily beats a complex one abandoned after two weeks.

Section 3

The Weekly & Monthly Review Rituals

Weekly Review: 30 Minutes Every Sunday

This is your non-negotiable feedback session. Block 30 minutes, no distractions, same time every week. Here is the agenda:

  • Trades taken this week: List every entry and exit. Calculate weekly P/L and average R-multiple.
  • Setups missed: Review scanner output vs. executed trades. Why did you skip? Fear? Already full? No conviction?
  • Execution quality: Grade each trade A+ to F. Did you follow your rules?
  • Regime check: Is the market regime the same as last week? Should you adjust exposure?
  • Emotional log: Were there moments of FOMO, revenge trading, or overconfidence?
  • One improvement: Write down exactly one thing to do better next week.

Monthly Retrospective: Deep Analysis

Once a month, zoom out. The monthly review goes beyond individual trades to examine patterns, strategy performance, and system health.

The MarketWatch Retrospective Methodology

Every week, we grade our scanner output using a structured rubric. This is not about whether the market went up or down — it's about whether our process was sound, our signals were actionable, and our risk management held.

The Grading Scale: A+ to F

GradeHit Rate TP1Avg R/RSignal QualityDescription
A+≥ 70%≥ 3:1Clean, actionableExceptional week. System firing on all cylinders.
A≥ 60%≥ 2.5:1Mostly cleanStrong performance, minor noise.
B+≥ 50%≥ 2:1GoodSolid week. System working as designed.
B≥ 45%≥ 1.5:1AcceptableAverage performance. No red flags.
C≥ 35%≥ 1:1NoisyBelow average. Investigate regime mismatch.
D≥ 25%< 1:1PoorSystem struggling. Reduce exposure.
F< 25%< 0.5:1BrokenFull stop. Reassess everything before next trade.

Grading Process vs. Outcome

A critical distinction: you can have an A+ process with a losing week (variance) and an F process with a winning week (luck). The grade focuses on process quality. Over time, good process converges to good outcomes. Chasing outcomes without process leads to inconsistency.

The "Mountain Plateau" Principle

Optimize for Robustness, Not Peak Performance

Imagine your strategy's performance as a mountain landscape. The peak is the single parameter set that produces the best backtest results. The plateau is the broad, flat region where many nearby parameter sets produce similar results.

Always choose the plateau over the peak. A strategy that works well with RSI(12), RSI(14), and RSI(16) is robust. A strategy that only works with RSI(13.7) is fragile and likely overfit.

Perturbation Testing: The Overfitting Detector

The Perturbation Test
If ±15% parameter change kills your edge → you're overfitting

The test is simple: take every parameter in your strategy and shift it by +15% and -15%. If performance drops by more than 30%, your strategy is curve-fitted to historical noise, not capturing a real edge.

  • MA period 20 → test 17 and 23: Results should be within 20% of original.
  • RSI threshold 30 → test 26 and 35: Still profitable? Good sign.
  • Stop-loss 2 ATR → test 1.7 and 2.3: Performance shouldn't collapse.
  • Holding period 5 days → test 4 and 6: Robust strategies tolerate this easily.

The Overfitting Trap

The most common mistake in systematic trading: optimizing parameters until the backtest looks perfect, then watching the strategy fail in live trading. If your strategy has more than 5-7 free parameters, you are almost certainly overfitting. Simplicity is a feature, not a limitation.

The 5 Levels of Parameter Robustness

Walk-Forward Analysis: The Ultimate Robustness Test

Beyond perturbation testing, the gold standard is walk-forward analysis: optimize on 12 months of data, test on the next 3 months, then roll the window forward. If out-of-sample performance is within 70% of in-sample, your strategy is capturing a real market phenomenon. Below 50%? You're fitting noise.

The key metric is the Walk-Forward Efficiency Ratio: OOS return / IS return. Target above 0.6 for reliable strategies. Below 0.4 warrants fundamental redesign.

Most traders search for the one big insight that will transform their results. In practice, edge comes from dozens of small refinements compounding over time.

Building Your Feedback Loop

1. Scan

Run your scanner. Generate setups systematically from market data.

2. Execute

Follow your rules. Enter, size, set stops and targets mechanically.

3. Review

Log everything. Grade yourself. Calculate metrics weekly and monthly.

4. Improve

One adjustment at a time. Test with perturbation. Keep what's robust.

5. Repeat

The loop never stops. Each cycle compounds your edge.

Section 6 — The Complete System

The Master Checklist: 50 Points of Systematic Trading

This is the culmination of all 8 parts. Every point has been covered in depth throughout the series. Print this, pin it to your wall, and run through it before every trade and every review session.

Part 1 — Framework & Mindset
  1. I have a written trading plan with clear rules for entry, exit, and sizing.
  2. My system is based on a defined edge, not gut feeling or tips.
  3. I understand that consistency beats intensity — process over outcome.
  4. I accept that 40-60% of my trades will be losers, and that's normal.
  5. I have defined my risk tolerance: max drawdown I can handle emotionally and financially.
  6. My system has fewer than 7 free parameters to avoid overfitting.
Part 2 — Market Regime
  1. I identify the current regime (bull, bear, range) before looking at individual setups.
  2. I use breadth indicators (% above 200MA, advance/decline) to confirm regime.
  3. I adjust my exposure based on regime: full in bull, reduced in range, minimal in bear.
  4. I track regime transitions and have rules for when to switch modes.
  5. I never fight the regime — no aggressive longs in a confirmed bear market.
  6. I monitor VIX and credit spreads as regime confirmation signals.
Part 3 — Scanner & Signal Generation
  1. My scanner runs systematically on a fixed schedule (daily or weekly).
  2. Signals are generated from data, not from social media or tips.
  3. I require volume confirmation for all breakout signals.
  4. I filter for liquidity: no stocks with avg volume < 500K shares/day.
  5. I log every signal generated, whether I take it or not.
  6. My scanner produces 5-15 signals per cycle — not 0 and not 50.
Part 4 — Technical Validation
  1. Every setup has a clear entry trigger (not "I think it looks good").
  2. I check multi-timeframe alignment: weekly trend supports daily entry.
  3. Support and resistance levels are identified before entry.
  4. I require at least 2 confirming signals (price action + indicator + volume).
  5. I check for nearby earnings, ex-div dates, or macro events that could invalidate the setup.
  6. I identify the invalidation point before calculating position size.
  7. I never chase: if the entry level is passed, I wait for a pullback or move on.
Part 5 — Risk Management
  1. I risk no more than 1-2% of equity per trade.
  2. My stop-loss is set at a technical level, not an arbitrary percentage.
  3. Position size is calculated from risk, not from conviction.
  4. I have a daily loss limit: if I lose 3% in a day, I stop trading.
  5. I have a weekly loss limit: if I lose 5% in a week, I reduce size by 50%.
  6. I never average down on a losing position.
  7. My portfolio heat (total open risk) never exceeds 6-8% of equity.
Part 6 — Portfolio Construction
  1. I maintain sector diversification: no more than 30% in one sector.
  2. I check correlation between positions before adding a new one.
  3. I size positions inversely to volatility (smaller size = higher volatility).
  4. I limit concurrent positions to 8-12 to maintain focus.
  5. I rebalance when any position exceeds 2x its initial allocation.
  6. Cash is a position — I'm comfortable being 30-50% cash in uncertain regimes.
Part 7 — Execution & Timing
  1. I use limit orders for entries, not market orders.
  2. I avoid trading the first and last 15 minutes unless my system specifically targets those windows.
  3. I scale in when conviction is high: 50% initial, 50% on confirmation.
  4. I take partial profits at TP1 (50%) and trail the rest to TP2.
  5. I move stop to breakeven after TP1 is hit.
  6. I have a pre-market routine: check overnight futures, economic calendar, gap analysis.
Part 8 — Review & Continuous Improvement
  1. I maintain a trading journal with at minimum: ticker, entry, exit, R-multiple, grade, lesson.
  2. I conduct a 30-minute weekly review every Sunday without exception.
  3. I run a monthly retrospective: hit rate, profit factor, P/L by strategy, drawdown analysis.
  4. I grade my scanner output weekly using the A+ to F scale.
  5. I perturbation-test any parameter change before deploying it live.
  6. I track skipped trades to detect survivorship bias in my own record.

Key Takeaways — Part 8: Review & Continuous Improvement

  • Review is alpha. The compound effect of weekly 1% improvements transforms your trading within a year.
  • Log everything. A simple journal maintained consistently beats a complex one abandoned after two weeks.
  • Grade process, not outcomes. An A+ process with a losing week is still an A+ process. Luck is not a strategy.
  • Robustness over optimization. Choose the plateau, not the peak. If a 15% parameter shift breaks your edge, you're overfitting.
  • The loop is the system. Scan, Execute, Review, Improve, Repeat. The traders who run this loop fastest win.
