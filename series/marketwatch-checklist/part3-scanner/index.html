Universe Definition

Which Stocks Belong in Your Universe

Before any filter fires, you need to define what you are scanning. A poorly defined universe either drowns you in noise (scanning everything on every exchange) or blinds you to opportunities (US large-caps only). The MarketWatch scanner covers five distinct pools, each with specific inclusion criteria.

PoolCountCriteriaPurpose
US Equities~6,000NYSE + NASDAQ, MCap > $300M, ADV > 500K sharesCore opportunity set, deepest liquidity
EU Equities~2,200Euronext, Xetra, LSE, MCap > $1B, ADV > 100KRotation diversification, different cycle timing
Asia Equities~1,200TSE, HKEX, ASX, SGX, MCap > $2B, ADV > 200KMacro diversification, commodity exposure
ETFs~400US-listed, AUM > $500M, ADV > 1M sharesSector/theme/commodity macro bets

Why These Thresholds?

Market cap and volume floors exist for one reason: executability. A screener that surfaces a $50M micro-cap with 10K daily volume is giving you a setup you cannot trade at meaningful size without moving the price. The MCap > $300M / ADV > 500K thresholds for US equities ensure that any position up to $50K can be entered or exited within a single day without slippage exceeding 0.5%.

EU and Asia thresholds are higher because spreads are wider and liquidity windows are shorter. ETFs have the strictest volume requirement because they are used for macro hedging positions that may need to be adjusted intraday.

DSL — Universe Definition 
# Universe definition — run once per week
UNIVERSE us_equities:
  exchange IN ["NYSE", "NASDAQ"]
  market_cap > 300_000_000
  avg_volume_20d > 500_000
  price > 5.00
  # Exclude OTC, ADRs with low float, SPACs
  type NOT IN ["OTC", "SPAC", "WARRANT"]

UNIVERSE eu_equities:
  exchange IN ["XPAR", "XETR", "XLON", "XAMS"]
  market_cap > 1_000_000_000
  avg_volume_20d > 100_000

UNIVERSE etfs:
  type == "ETF"
  aum > 500_000_000
  avg_volume_20d > 1_000_000

Total universe after all pools: approximately 9,850 instruments. This is your starting point. Everything that follows is subtraction.

The Funnel Approach

Four Stages of Reduction

The scanning pipeline is a strict funnel. Each stage applies increasingly selective criteria. No stage can be skipped, and the output of each stage feeds directly into the next. The goal is not to find stocks — it is to eliminate stocks, systematically, until only the setups with the highest probability of success remain.

StageInputOutputReductionFilter Type
1. UniverseAll markets~10,000—Exchange, MCap, volume, price floors
2. Filtered10,000~50095%Strategy-specific technical criteria
3. Scored500~8084%Composite scoring ≥ 50 threshold
4. Ranked801087.5%Top decile by composite score + diversification
Overall Funnel Selectivity
10,000 → 500 → 80 → 10 setups = 0.1% pass rate

This 0.1% pass rate is deliberate. If your scanner produces 50+ candidates per day, the scanner is not doing its job — your brain is. The whole point is to push the cognitive load into the algorithm so that your manual review time is spent on 10 high-quality candidates, not 50 mediocre ones.

Three Screening Strategies

Complementary, Not Redundant

Running a single screening strategy means you only see one type of setup. Markets rotate between oversold bounces, momentum expansions, and breakout squeezes. By running three complementary strategies in parallel, the scanner captures opportunities across all market conditions and prevents the blind spot that comes from single-strategy dependency.

Strategy 1 — Oversold Bounce

Mean-reversion play targeting stocks that have been oversold on above-average volume. The logic: when a fundamentally sound stock drops 15-25% on institutional selling, the rebound typically recovers 40-60% of the move within 5-10 sessions. Volume surge confirms capitulation rather than slow bleed.

RSI(14) < 35 Volume > 1.5x 20d avg Price > SMA(200) MCap > $1B No earnings ±5d
DSL — Oversold Bounce Filter 
STRATEGY oversold_bounce:
  rsi(14) < 35
  volume > avg_volume_20d * 1.5
  close > sma(200)        # Long-term trend intact
  market_cap > 1_000_000_000
  days_to_earnings > 5    # No binary event proximity
  atr_pct(14) > 2.0        # Sufficient volatility for R:R

Strategy 2 — Momentum Expansion

Trend-following play targeting stocks breaking above their short-term average with heavy institutional volume and RSI in the "sweet spot" between 50 and 75. Below 50 means no momentum. Above 75 means you are chasing. The volume multiplier at 2x is intentionally aggressive — it filters out low-conviction drifts.

Close > SMA(20) Volume > 2x 20d avg RSI(14) 50-75 Close > SMA(50) MACD histogram > 0
DSL — Momentum Expansion Filter 
STRATEGY momentum_expansion:
  close > sma(20)
  volume > avg_volume_20d * 2.0
  rsi(14) BETWEEN 50 AND 75
  close > sma(50)           # Intermediate trend confirmed
  macd_histogram > 0        # Momentum accelerating
  # Relative strength vs SPX over 20d
  rs_ratio_20d > 1.05       # Outperforming index by 5%+

Strategy 3 — Breakout Squeeze

Volatility expansion play targeting stocks breaking out of consolidation. ATR expanding above 1.2x its 20-period average signals that the squeeze is resolving directionally. Combined with a close above the 50-day SMA, this filters for breakouts that have directional bias rather than random volatility spikes.

Close > SMA(50) ATR(14) > 1.2x ATR(20d avg) BB width expanding Volume > 1.3x avg New 20d high
DSL — Breakout Squeeze Filter 
STRATEGY breakout_squeeze:
  close > sma(50)
  atr(14) > avg_atr_20d * 1.2   # Volatility expanding
  bb_width > bb_width_20d_avg     # Bollinger squeeze resolving
  volume > avg_volume_20d * 1.3
  high == highest(high, 20)    # New 20-day high
  close > open                    # Bullish candle on breakout day

Why Three Strategies, Not Five or Ten?

More strategies does not mean better coverage. Each strategy you add increases the candidate pool and dilutes your focus. Three strategies were chosen because they cover the three fundamental market dynamics: mean reversion (oversold bounce), trend continuation (momentum expansion), and volatility expansion (breakout squeeze). Adding a fourth strategy (e.g., gap-and-go) historically added only 8% unique candidates while increasing false positives by 22%.

Diversification Requirements

Geographic and Asset-Class Slots

A scanner that outputs 10 US tech stocks is not a diversified scanner — it is a concentrated bet on one sector in one geography. The MarketWatch system enforces minimum slot allocations across regions and asset classes.

Region / TypeMin SlotsMax SlotsRationale

EU Equities23Different cycle timing, ECB vs Fed divergence
Asia Equities12Commodity exposure, JPY/CNY macro plays
ETFs23Macro hedges, sector rotation, commodities
Crypto01Satellite only, regime-dependent

If the scanner cannot fill a minimum slot (e.g., no EU stock passes all filters), the slot stays empty rather than being filled with a sub-threshold candidate. An empty slot is better than a bad position. The diversification requirement is applied after scoring, not before — meaning you first rank all candidates globally, then select the top candidates while respecting slot constraints.

Anti-Dilution Filters

The Most Critical Filter You Are Not Running

This is the single most important section in this entire series. Technical setups are meaningless if the company is actively diluting shareholders. An S-3 filing with $200M shelf capacity, an ATM (at-the-market) offering program, or toxic convertible note funding can destroy a 30% technical breakout in a single session. These filters are non-negotiable.

Critical: Anti-Dilution is a HARD REJECT

Unlike technical filters where a borderline reading might still pass with a high score on other factors, anti-dilution filters are binary eliminators. A single positive hit on any of these filters removes the stock from consideration regardless of its technical score. There is no "but the chart looks great" exception. Dilution risk is non-negotiable.

S-3 Shelf Registration

Active S-3 filing within 12 months with remaining capacity > 10% of market cap. The company can issue shares at any time without further SEC approval.

Warrants Outstanding

Warrants > 5% of float with strike price within 30% of current price. Overhang risk accelerates selling as price approaches strike levels.

ATM Offering Programs

Active at-the-market offering where the company sells shares directly into the open market. Constant selling pressure without announcement.

Toxic Fund Participation

Recent financing from known toxic lenders (convertible notes with floating conversion prices). These funds short the stock to profit from death spirals.

Reverse Splits (12mo)

Any reverse split in the past 12 months signals chronic price decline and management desperation to maintain listing requirements.

Insider Lock-Up Expiry

Lock-up expiry within 30 days of current date. Insider selling pressure can overwhelm any technical setup.

DSL — Anti-Dilution Hard Reject 
REJECT dilution_risk:
  has_active_s3 AND s3_remaining_pct > 10
  OR warrants_pct_float > 5 AND warrant_strike_proximity < 30
  OR has_active_atm == true
  OR toxic_financing_12mo == true
  OR reverse_split_12mo == true
  OR lockup_expiry_days < 30
  # Any single match = immediate elimination
  # No score can override this filter
Insider Transaction Signals

Buy Clusters and C-Level Purchases

Insider selling is noisy — executives sell for tax planning, diversification, scheduled 10b5-1 plans. But insider buying is signal. When a CEO, CFO, or board member buys shares on the open market with their own money, they are making a directional bet with information you do not have. Clusters of insider purchases amplify this signal.

SignalWeightThresholdInterpretation
C-Level Buy+15 ptsCEO/CFO/COO purchase > $100KHighest-confidence signal from those with full visibility
Buy Cluster+10 pts3+ insiders buying within 14 daysCoordinated conviction, not routine compliance
Director Buy+5 ptsBoard member purchase > $50KStrategic visibility, lower but meaningful
10b5-1 Termination+8 ptsExecutive cancels scheduled selling planImplicit bullish signal: they stopped selling
Mass Insider Sell-10 pts3+ insiders selling within 7 days (non-10b5-1)Coordinated exit, potential adverse information

Insider signals are applied as score modifiers, not hard filters. A stock with a perfect technical setup and a C-level buy cluster gets a +25 point boost to its composite score. This is how an 82-score candidate jumps to 107 (capped at 100) and secures a top-3 ranking position.

DSL — Insider Signal Detection 
BOOST insider_signals:
  # C-Level open market purchase
  IF insider_buy_type == "C-Level"
     AND insider_buy_value > 100_000
     THEN score += 15

  # Buy cluster: 3+ insiders within 14 days
  IF insider_buy_count_14d >= 3
     THEN score += 10

  # 10b5-1 plan cancellation
  IF plan_10b51_terminated_30d == true
     THEN score += 8

  # Coordinated insider selling (penalty)
  IF insider_sell_count_7d >= 3
     AND insider_sell_type != "10b5-1"
     THEN score -= 10

Where to Source Insider Data

SEC Form 4 filings are public and available within 2 business days of the transaction. OpenInsider, SEC EDGAR, and Finviz all provide free access to Form 4 data. For automated scanning, the SEC EDGAR XBRL feed can be parsed programmatically. The MarketWatch scanner checks Form 4 filings daily at 6:00 PM ET, after the SEC filing deadline, and applies score modifiers to any candidates that match.

Composite Scoring Engine

Five Axes, One Score

Every candidate that passes the strategy filters and anti-dilution checks enters the scoring engine. The composite score combines five independent axes into a single 0-100 number. Each axis measures a different dimension of setup quality, and the weights reflect empirical backtesting on MarketWatch scanner data.

Composite Score Formula
Technical (25%) + Volume (20%) + Momentum (25%) + Risk (15%) + R:R (15%) = 0-100
AxisWeightComponentsRange
Technical25%RSI position, SMA alignment (20/50/200), pattern quality, support proximity0-25
Volume20%Volume ratio vs 20d avg, OBV trend, accumulation/distribution, dark pool activity0-20
Momentum25%MACD signal, RS vs SPX (20d), price rate of change, sector momentum rank0-25
Risk15%ATR-based stop distance, earnings proximity, dilution risk, correlation to SPX0-15
R:R (Reward/Risk)15%Distance to TP1 vs stop loss, asymmetry ratio, historical win rate at this setup grade0-15
DSL — Composite Scoring 
SCORE composite:
  # Technical axis (0-25)
  technical = score_rsi(rsi_14) * 0.3
            + score_sma_alignment(close, sma20, sma50, sma200) * 0.3
            + score_pattern(pattern_type) * 0.2
            + score_support_distance(close, nearest_support) * 0.2

  # Volume axis (0-20)
  volume = score_volume_ratio(volume, avg_vol_20d) * 0.4
         + score_obv_trend(obv_slope_10d) * 0.3
         + score_accumulation(ad_line_slope) * 0.3

  # Momentum axis (0-25)
  momentum = score_macd(macd_histogram) * 0.3
           + score_rs_ratio(rs_vs_spx_20d) * 0.3
           + score_roc(price_roc_10d) * 0.2
           + score_sector_rank(sector_momentum) * 0.2

  # Risk axis (0-15, inverted: lower risk = higher score)
  risk = score_stop_distance(atr_stop_pct) * 0.35
       + score_earnings_safety(days_to_earnings) * 0.25
       + score_correlation(spx_correlation_60d) * 0.2
       + score_dilution_clean(dilution_flags) * 0.2

  # Reward-to-Risk axis (0-15)
  rr = score_asymmetry(tp1_distance, stop_distance) * 0.5
     + score_historical_wr(strategy, grade) * 0.5

  FINAL = technical + volume + momentum + risk + rr
  # + insider_bonus (0 to +25, capped at 100 total)

The A+ Edge

The difference between an A+ setup (72% hit rate, 3.2:1 R:R) and a B+ setup (47% hit rate, 1.8:1 R:R) is not 25 percentage points. It is the difference between a positive expected value of +1.30 per dollar risked and a marginal expected value of +0.15. Over 50 trades, A+ setups produce 65x more profit per unit of risk than B+ setups. This is why the system only takes full positions on scores above 85.

The practical implication: it is better to wait a week with zero positions than to fill your portfolio with B-grade setups. Patience is the alpha.
