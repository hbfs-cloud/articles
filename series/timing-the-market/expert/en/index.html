Chapter 1 — Valuation Models: The Long-Term Compass

Valuation models are not timing tools — they are positioning tools. They tell you whether the wind is at your back or in your face over the next 5-10 years. Every serious market timer starts here.

Accuracy: Excellent for 10-20 year forecasting. Useless for short-term timing. Between 1981-2015, CAPE signaled overvaluation in 416 of 422 months — investors who followed missed massive gains. Jeremy Siegel (Wharton) argues CAPE has been "consistently wrong since 1990" due to accounting changes.

Verdict: Use CAPE to set expectations, not to time entries. At CAPE 40, expect 3-5% real returns over the next decade. Adjust equity allocation accordingly, but don't go to 100% cash.

Chapter 2 — Breadth & Momentum: The Market's Heartbeat

Price tells you where the market is. Breadth tells you whether the market is healthy. The most dangerous markets are those where the index rises on fewer and fewer stocks — a sign of internal rot.

Zweig Breadth Thrust — The Holy Grail

The rarest and most powerful bullish signal in market history. Only ~20 thrusts since 1950. 100% positive returns at 6 and 12 months after every signal.

Advance/Decline Line Divergences

When the S&P 500 makes new highs but the A/D line doesn't — beware. This divergence preceded the 1929 crash, the 2000 dot-com bust, and the 2007 top.

McClellan Oscillator

Extreme levels: +/- 70-100 (traditional), modern extremes reach +/-150+. March 2020 plunged to -300 — one of the most extreme oversold readings in 55 years. A 100-point move from below -50 to above +50 = "breadth thrust" — powerful shift from selling exhaustion to broad-based buying.

Hindenburg Omen — Handle With Care

Accuracy: only 20% — 80% false positive rate. Successfully flagged 1987 and 2008, but generates excessive noise. Only meaningful in clusters (2-3+ triggers within 30 days). Never use alone.

Coppock Curve

Only 8 buy signals on S&P 500 since 1980. Weekly signals within +/- 2 weeks of an IBD Follow-Through Day correctly identify rallies 79% in bull markets, 45% in bear markets. Feb 2001 sell signal avoided most of the 2000-2002 bear. June 2008 sell signal caught the exit before the second-half plunge.

Chapter 3 — Sentiment: Trade Against the Crowd

"Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful." — Warren Buffett. Sentiment indicators quantify exactly how fearful or greedy the crowd is — and the data is remarkable.

AAII Sentiment Survey

The gold standard of retail sentiment. Long-term averages: Bullish 38%, Bearish 30%, Neutral 31%.

CNN Fear & Greed Index

Scale: 0 (max fear) to 100 (max greed). Key levels:

  • March 2020 (COVID): Single-digit territory, March 5-23 — S&P lost 34%. Marked the bottom.
  • April 8, 2025: Plunged to 3 — lowest since March 2020. Massive buying opportunity.
  • Recovery above 25 from extreme fear tends to precede sustainable rallies.

Smart Money vs. Dumb Money Confidence (SentimenTrader)

Warning Signal: Dumb Money > 60% AND Smart Money < 40%.
Opportunity Signal: Dumb Money < 40% AND Smart Money > 60%.

At every major bottom since 2003, Smart Money confidence was elevated while Dumb Money was panicking. The inverse was true at tops. This is the single best composite sentiment indicator available (subscription required).

Put/Call Ratio (CBOE Equity)

Extreme fear: P/C > 1.23 (top 5%) — statistically significant above-average returns after 30 and 60 days.
Extreme greed: P/C < 0.72 (bottom 5%) — below-average returns, but NOT statistically significant.
Optimal holding period after fear spike: 7-15 days. Asymmetric edge: fear signals are more reliable than greed signals.

VIX Term Structure (Contango vs. Backwardation)

Contango (normal) occurs 84% of the time. Backwardation is the exception and indicates present panic/capitulation. Subsequent S&P returns after backwardation were historically positive. Steep contango = complacency. Neither is a standalone timing tool.

The Bottom Confirmation Checklist

A genuine bottom typically shows 7+ of these 12 signals:

  • VIX > 40 — Capitulation-level fear. Every VIX spike above 40 marked a local bottom.
  • AAII Bearish > 50% — Retail sentiment at extremes. 70.3% in March 2009 was the perfect bottom.
  • Fear & Greed < 10 — Maximum fear readings preceded every major bottom since 2012.
  • Put/Call Ratio > 1.20 — Extreme hedging = extreme fear = contrarian buy signal.
  • McClellan Oscillator < -150 — Extreme oversold breadth. -300 in March 2020.
  • <20% stocks above 200-DMA — Broad-based selling exhaustion.
  • Credit Spreads (HY OAS) > 500 bps — Credit markets pricing recession. 2,182 bps in Dec 2008.
  • Smart Money Confidence > 60% — Institutions positioning for a bottom while retail panics.
  • Insider buying surge — CEOs buying their own stock in size. Harvard: +6% annual outperformance.
  • Selling climax volume — Highest volume in weeks/months, then price stabilizes on lower volume.
  • VIX backwardation — Near-term VIX > far-term VIX. Present panic exceeds expected future panic.
  • Margin debt collapse — Forced selling creates feedback loops. Deleveraging washes out weak hands.

The Fed Pivot Trap

Conventional wisdom says "buy when the Fed cuts." The reality is nuanced: the market bottomed after a Fed pivot in 4 of 5 cycles since 1980, but on average the S&P 500 lost 23.5% after the first rate cut and took 200 days to find a bottom. The pivot is a process, not a moment. Don't front-run it blindly.

The Top Detection Checklist

  • Mega-cap concentration > 35% — Top 10 stocks = 40% of S&P. Last time it was this extreme: never. Dot-com peaked at ~30%.
  • A/D line divergence — Index rising while fewer stocks participate. Classic pattern before 1929, 2000, 2007.
  • RSI divergence on monthly — Price makes higher high, RSI makes lower high = momentum fading.
  • Small-caps underperforming — IWM lagging SPY for 6+ months = late-cycle signal.
  • Defensive sector leadership — XLU, XLP, XLV outperforming cyclicals = capital fleeing to safety.
  • IPO/SPAC mania — 1999: ~500 IPOs. 2021: 1,000+. When everyone wants to go public, the cycle is late.
  • Insider selling surge — Buy/sell ratio dropping below 0.20 = insiders know something you don't.
  • LEI declining for 5+ months — Conference Board Leading Economic Index turns negative ~7 months before a turn.
  • CAPE > 35 — Not a timing signal alone, but when combined with other warnings, raises the probability of a correction.
  • Credit spread compression < 300 bps — Extreme complacency in credit = underpricing risk.

Chapter 6 — Catching Trends: Ride the Wave, Don't Predict the Ocean

The most money is made not by predicting the market, but by identifying trends early and riding them. Here are the proven frameworks used by the world's best trend-followers.

Stan Weinstein's 4-Stage Model

1

Basing

30-week SMA flat. Accumulation. WATCH

2

Advancing

30-week SMA rising. Markup. BUY

3

Distribution

30-week SMA flat. Topping. SELL

4

Declining

30-week SMA falling. Markdown. AVOID

Only buy Stage 2 stocks. Never try to catch a falling knife in Stage 4. The 30-week (150-day) SMA direction is your single most important filter.

Minervini's Trend Template (SEPA)

Mark Minervini won the US Investing Championship with 155% returns in 2021 and 334.8% in 1997. His 8-point template filters only the strongest stocks:

  • Price above 150-day AND 200-day MAs
  • 150-day MA above 200-day MA
  • 200-day MA trending up for 1+ month (ideally 4-5)
  • 50-day MA above both 150 and 200-day
  • Price above 50-day MA
  • Price at least 30% above 52-week low
  • Price within 25% of 52-week high
  • RS Rating ≥ 85

VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern): 3-4+ progressive pullbacks where each one decreases (e.g., 18% → 12% → 6%). Forms over 4-12 weeks. Stop-loss: 5-8% from entry. The VCP is Minervini's highest-conviction setup.

IBD Relative Strength Rating

Formula: 0.4 × ROC(63) + 0.2 × ROC(126) + 0.2 × ROC(189) + 0.2 × ROC(252). Most recent quarter weighted 40%. From 1950-2008, the average RS of the best-performing stocks just before their winning runs was 87. Minimum threshold: 80. Sweet spot: 90+.

The "Time in Market" Myth — Nuanced

"Time in market beats timing the market" is repeated everywhere. The data supports it on average — missing the 10 best days costs dramatically. But the 10 best days almost always occur within weeks of the 10 worst days, and they happen during bear markets. The real lesson is: don't panic-sell at the bottom. Systematic timing (200-DMA, Dual Momentum) works because it removes emotion, not because it predicts the future.

Chapter 9 — The Master Plan: Becoming the Best Timer in the World

No single indicator works alone. The best market timers in history — Paul Tudor Jones, Stanley Druckenmiller, George Soros — all used multi-factor, multi-timeframe frameworks that combined technicals, macro, sentiment, and positioning.

Here is the integrated system:

The 3-Layer Framework

Layer 1: Macro Regime (Monthly) — "Which Season?"

  • Yield curve slope (normal vs inverted)
  • ISM PMI direction (expanding vs contracting)
  • Fed policy direction (cutting vs hiking)
  • Credit spreads direction (widening vs tightening)
  • CAPE + Buffett Indicator level (cheap vs expensive)

Output: Determines your base equity allocation (30-100% of portfolio).

Layer 2: Market Timing (Weekly) — "Which Phase?"

  • 200-DMA position (above vs below)
  • A/D Line confirmation vs divergence
  • Sector rotation leadership (cyclical vs defensive)
  • Monthly MACD direction on SPX
  • Breadth (Zweig Thrust, McClellan, % above 200-DMA)

Output: Determines risk-on vs risk-off positioning within your allocation.

Layer 3: Tactical Execution (Daily) — "Where Exactly?"

  • Sentiment extremes (AAII, F&G, P/C ratio, Smart/Dumb Money)
  • VIX level and term structure
  • Stage Analysis (Weinstein) for individual stocks
  • Minervini Trend Template for stock selection
  • Volume analysis (climax, exhaustion, breakout confirmation)

Output: Determines specific entries, exits, position sizing, and stop placement.

The Hardest Part

The system is straightforward. Executing it is not. Buying when VIX is 80 and AAII bearish is 70% means buying when every fiber of your being screams "sell." The system works precisely because it's psychologically brutal. The edge comes from emotional discipline, not intellectual complexity. Keep a journal. Follow the checklist. Ignore the noise.
