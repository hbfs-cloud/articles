The Market Players

Who Really Moves the Markets?

Every trade has someone on the other side

When you buy a stock, someone else is selling it to you. That someone could be a trillion-dollar hedge fund, an algorithm executing in microseconds, or a market maker who does this thousands of times per day. Knowing who you are trading against changes everything about how you should behave — and what advantages you actually have.

The 6 types of market participants

Retail (You!)
Individual investors like you and me. About 20-25% of US trading volume since 2020. Powered by apps like Robinhood and social media communities.
20-25% volume Flexible Emotional
Big Funds
Pension funds, mutual funds (Vanguard, Fidelity), hedge funds. They manage trillions of dollars and move markets when they buy or sell.
60-70% volume Big money Slow
Market Makers
Companies like Citadel Securities and Virtu. They are always ready to buy AND sell, making sure there is always someone on the other side of your trade.
Provide liquidity Always on
Robots (HFT)
High-Frequency Trading algorithms that make thousands of trades per second. They hold positions for milliseconds. About 50-70% of all trading volume!
50-70% volume Milliseconds
Dark Pools
Private exchanges where big funds trade without showing their orders publicly. About 30-40% of US stock trades happen here.
30-40% volume Hidden
Central Banks
The Federal Reserve, European Central Bank, Bank of Japan. They do not trade stocks directly, but they control interest rates and money supply, which moves everything.
Interest rates Most powerful

But wait... the percentages add up to more than 100%!

Good catch! Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so the same trade gets counted on both sides. Also, categories overlap — many HFT firms are also market makers. A single entity can fill multiple roles simultaneously, which is why the percentages add up to well over 100%.

A real-life example: GameStop (January 2021)

The GameStop saga is the perfect example of what happens when these players collide.

  • Retail investors on Reddit (r/WallStreetBets) noticed that a hedge fund had bet heavily that GameStop stock would go DOWN.
  • They started buying GameStop stock together, pushing the price from $17 to $483 in just 3 weeks.
  • The hedge fund (Melvin Capital) lost about $6.8 billion and had to be bailed out.
  • But people who bought at the top lost 60-80% when the price eventually crashed back down.

Lesson: Understanding who the other players are helps you know when you have an advantage and when you are the one at a disadvantage.

Key Takeaway

  • You (retail) are just one of many types of players in the market.
  • Big funds and robots control most of the trading volume.
  • Central banks (especially the Fed) are the most powerful force of all.
  • You do not need to compete with robots. Your advantage is patience and having no one to report to.

Market Scams and Manipulation

The 4 Most Common Scams to Watch Out For

Golden Rule

If it sounds too good to be true, it IS too good to be true. A "guaranteed" return of 10% per month, an "insider tip" on Telegram, a stock that "will go 100x this week" — these are scam signals, not opportunities. The stock market rewards patience and research, not greed.

Know the tricks, and they stop working

Market manipulation works by exploiting what you do not know. Manipulators rely on the fact that most investors have never heard of these tactics. The scams below are the most common ones used in financial markets. Once you know how they work, you will never fall for them.

1
Pump & Dump
Hype up a stock, then sell it to you at the top

Like a lemonade stand scam

Imagine someone opens a lemonade stand. They tell all their friends to come buy lemonade and create a huge line. People passing by see the crowd and think: "This must be amazing lemonade!" So they get in line too. But the original owner has already sold all the lemonade at a high price and walks away. The crowd is left with overpriced, mediocre lemonade.

How it works:

  • A group quietly buys a cheap stock (or crypto token).
  • They launch a massive promotion campaign on Telegram, TikTok, YouTube, and Twitter/X.
  • New buyers rush in due to FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out), pushing the price up.
  • The original group sells at the top. The price crashes. Latecomers lose everything.

Real example — Squid Game Token (2021): A crypto token named after the Netflix show gained +75,000% in a few days. The creators then drained all the money in one transaction (called a "rug pull"). Total loss for buyers: ~$3.4 million.

How to spot it:

  • Aggressive promotion by social media influencers (not financial professionals)
  • Buzzwords like "moonshot", "gem", "1000x", "before it's too late"
  • A tiny company with no real product, no revenue, and no identifiable team
  • Volume suddenly explodes 10x to 100x without any real news

How to protect yourself:

  • DYOR (Do Your Own Research): if you cannot explain what the company does in 2 sentences, skip it.
  • Be suspicious of anyone "advising" you to buy something without disclosing their own position.
  • Rule of thumb: If the tip comes from a "VIP" Telegram group, you are already too late.
2
Wash Trading (Fake Volume)
Buying and selling to yourself to create the illusion of popularity

Like buying your own concert tickets

Imagine a band that buys all their own concert tickets to make it look like they are sold out. When real fans see "SOLD OUT," they think: "This band must be amazing!" and pay more for resale tickets. But the "demand" was completely fake.

How it works: A person or group buys and sells the same stock or crypto token using different accounts they control. This makes it look like there is lots of trading activity and interest. Real buyers get tricked into thinking the asset is popular.

Very common in crypto: A 2019 study by Bitwise found that 95% of reported Bitcoin volume on unregulated exchanges was fake (wash trading).

How to protect yourself:

  • Use trusted exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken for crypto; NYSE, Nasdaq for stocks)
  • Compare the 30-day average volume. Sudden spikes without news are suspicious.
  • If price is not moving despite huge volume, something is off.
3
Insider Trading
Using secret information that regular people do not have

Trading on secrets is a crime

Insider trading means using confidential company information to trade stocks before that information is made public. It is illegal because it gives an unfair advantage over every other investor in the market. The SEC actively investigates and prosecutes these cases.

How it works: A company executive (CEO, director, or employee) knows something big is about to happen — a merger, great earnings, or a major problem — before it is publicly announced. They trade on this secret information, or they tip off a friend who then trades.

Famous case — Martha Stewart (2001): The celebrity sold her stock in a company called ImClone before bad news was announced. She avoided losing only about $45,000, but was sentenced to 5 months in prison. Not worth it.

Why you should care:

You cannot control insider trading, but you can look for its clues. If you see unusual trading volume on a stock right before a big announcement, that is often a sign that insiders were trading. You can check insider trades legally reported on the SEC website (Form 4 filings).

4
Social Media Scams
Influencers getting paid to promote stocks or crypto

Paid promotion disguised as advice

Many finance influencers on YouTube, TikTok, and Twitter/X are paid (in cash or tokens) to promote specific stocks and crypto projects — without disclosing it. Their followers trust the recommendation and buy in. But the influencer and their sponsors are already selling. The followers become the "exit liquidity" — the last buyers before the price crashes.

Red flags to watch for:

  • Finance advice from someone who is not a licensed financial professional
  • No disclaimer about whether they own the asset they are promoting
  • Urgency language: "Buy NOW before it's too late!"
  • Promises of specific returns: "This will 10x guaranteed!"

Your defense:

  • Never invest based on a social media post alone. Always do your own research.
  • Ask yourself: "If this person had a guaranteed way to make money, why are they telling 500,000 strangers?"
  • Check if the person has a legal disclaimer on their content.

Key Takeaway

  • If it sounds too good to be true, it is.
  • Never invest based on a tip from social media, Telegram, or a friend of a friend.
  • Always ask: "Who benefits if I buy this?" If the answer is the person telling you to buy, run.
  • Stick to well-known stocks and exchanges. Avoid penny stocks and unregulated crypto tokens.
The VIX: The Market's Fear Meter

What is the VIX?

Think of it like a weather forecast for stocks

The VIX is like a weather forecast for the stock market. Just as a weather report tells you whether to expect sunshine or storms, the VIX tells you whether investors expect calm markets or wild swings. A low VIX (under 15) means blue skies. A high VIX (over 30) means a storm is hitting. And just like weather, the VIX is a prediction — it tells you what people expect, not what has already happened.

How to read the VIX — it is just a number

The VIX is a number that usually ranges from about 10 to 80. Here is what each range means:

< 15

Calm / Blue Skies

Markets are super confident — maybe too confident. Ironically, big crashes often start when the VIX is very low, because nobody is prepared. What to do: Stay invested but keep some cash on the side just in case.

15-20

Normal

This is the historical average. Markets are functioning normally. What to do: Invest normally, nothing to worry about.

20-30

Nervous

Investors are getting worried. Markets might drop 5-10%. What to do: Be more cautious with new purchases. Do not put all your money in at once.

30-40

Fear

A crisis is happening. Markets are falling hard. But here is the counterintuitive part: this is where great buying opportunities start appearing. Warren Buffett says: "Be greedy when others are fearful." What to do: Start looking for quality stocks at a discount, but do not rush in all at once.

> 40

Panic

Total panic. This only happens a few times per decade (COVID crash in 2020: VIX hit 82, Financial crisis 2008: VIX hit 80). Historically, investors who bought the S&P 500 when the VIX was above 40 made an average of +25% over the next 12 months. What to do: If you have a 3+ year horizon and cash to invest, start buying — gradually.

Why does this matter to you?

The VIX helps you understand the mood of the market. When the VIX is high and everyone is panicking, that is often the worst time to sell and the best time to buy. When the VIX is very low and everyone is euphoric, that is when you should be cautious. It is a simple tool that helps you do the opposite of what your emotions tell you to do.

Warning: Never buy the VIX itself!

Some products let you "invest" in the VIX (like UVXY or VXX). Do NOT buy these. They are designed for professional traders and lose money over time. UVXY has lost 99.9% of its value since it was created. If you want to know the VIX, just check the number — you do not need to trade it.

Key Takeaway

  • The VIX is the market's "fear meter." Low = calm, high = scared.
  • VIX below 15 = maybe too calm. VIX above 30 = look for buying opportunities.
  • Do NOT try to trade the VIX. Just use it as a gauge.
  • Historically, buying when people are most afraid has been very profitable long-term.

When to Stay in Cash

"Cash Is a Position"

Sitting in cash is a strategic decision

Keeping cash in your investment account is not "doing nothing" — it is strategically waiting for a better opportunity. Cash gives you the ability to buy when prices drop, which is exactly when the best deals appear. Even Warren Buffett had $334 billion in cash at the end of 2024, waiting for the right moment to deploy it.

5 times when staying in cash makes sense

  • The market feels chaotic and you do not understand what is happening. When in doubt, do nothing.
  • You cannot find any good stocks to buy. Good opportunities only appear a few times per month. That is normal.
  • You are losing money on your current positions. Sometimes the best move is to step back, take a break, and re-evaluate.
  • A big event is coming (Fed meeting, elections, trade war announcements). Cash protects you from overnight surprises.
  • You just had a big win. Take a breather. The number one mistake beginners make is overtrading right after a win.

How much cash should you keep?

Market MoodVIX LevelRecommended CashWhy
Bull market (everything going up) < 18 10-15% Keep a small reserve to buy dips
Uncertain / nervous 18-25 20-30% Reduce risk, keep powder dry
Bear market / falling 25-40 30-50% Protect your money, prepare to buy the panic
Total panic > 40 Deploy your cash! This is when you buy — but gradually, not all at once

Do not confuse "being in cash" with "timing the market"

Staying in cash is a temporary, short-term decision (a few weeks to a few months). Trying to predict the exact top and bottom of the market ("timing the market") almost always fails. A JPMorgan study found that missing just the 10 best days of the S&P 500 over 20 years cuts your returns almost in half. Stay invested most of the time, with some cash on the side.

Key Takeaway

  • Holding cash is a valid strategy — it is not the same as doing nothing.
  • Keep 10-30% in cash depending on how nervous the market looks.
  • Never try to perfectly time when to buy and sell. Just stay mostly invested with some reserves.
  • When everyone else is panicking (VIX over 40), that is when your cash becomes your superpower.
Signal vs Noise: What to Pay Attention To

What Matters vs What is Just Noise

Most financial information is useless

Every day, you are bombarded with 500+ "signals": tweets, news alerts, Telegram messages, YouTube videos, broker notifications. 95% of it is noise — information that has zero impact on your investment returns. The real skill is not knowing more, it is knowing what to ignore. Only a handful of data points — Fed decisions, inflation numbers, earnings reports — actually move markets in a lasting way.

What the market REALLY pays attention to

SignalWhat It IsHow OftenImpact
Federal Reserve (Fed) Interest rate decisions 8 times per year BIG
Inflation (CPI) How fast prices are rising Monthly BIG
Jobs Report (NFP) How many jobs were created First Friday of each month BIG
Earnings Reports How much profit companies made Every 3 months BIG (for that stock)
Geopolitical Events Wars, trade deals, sanctions Unpredictable VARIES

What is NOISE (you can safely ignore)

  • Finance influencers on TikTok and YouTube — Most are paid to promote projects. 90% have undisclosed conflicts of interest.
  • "VIP" Telegram groups — If someone truly had a guaranteed way to make money, why would they share it with 5,000 strangers for $50/month?
  • Panic tweets: "CRASH COMING!!!" — Someone has been posting this every single week for 15 years. Even a broken clock is right twice a day.
  • "My barber told me about this stock..." — When your taxi driver starts giving you stock tips, it is a classic warning sign that the market is overheated.
  • Daily market commentators on TV — Studies show that TV pundits predict the market about as well as flipping a coin. It is entertainment, not information.

The 3-Question Filter

Before reacting to any piece of financial news, ask yourself these 3 questions:

  • 1. Is it a primary source or someone's opinion? A Fed press release = primary source. A tweet interpreting that press release = opinion. Always go to the source.
  • 2. Is it data or prediction? "Inflation is 2.8% in January" = data (fact). "Inflation will fall to 2% by June" = prediction (guess). Facts are facts. Predictions are bets.
  • 3. Does everyone agree or is this one person? If 20 analysts say a stock is worth $150 and one says $300, that one person needs extraordinary proof. Do not bet on the outlier.

Try this experiment

For one week, stop watching financial news, stop checking Twitter/X for stock tips, and stop reading Telegram groups. Just check your portfolio once a day (in the evening). At the end of the week, compare your results. In 80% of cases, people perform better when they consume less financial noise. The noise pushes you to overtrade, panic, and make emotional decisions.

Key Takeaway

  • 95% of financial news is noise. Only a handful of events really matter.
  • The Fed, inflation data, and earnings reports are the biggest movers.
  • Use the 3-question filter: Is it a source? Is it data? Does the consensus agree?
  • Less information often leads to better investment decisions.

Real Trends vs Passing Fads

Is It a Real Trend or Just Hype?

Think of trends like fashion

Some fashion trends last forever — jeans have been popular for over 100 years. Other trends last one summer and disappear (remember fidget spinners?). Investing works the same way. Some trends, like artificial intelligence, will reshape the world for decades. Others, like the metaverse, turned out to be a fad. Learning to tell the difference is one of the most valuable investing skills you can develop.

6 mega-trends that are here to stay (2025-2035)

Artificial Intelligence
Expected to be a $1.8 trillion market by 2030. Google, Microsoft, Amazon are spending $200B+ per year on it.
Aging Population
Japan, Europe, and China are getting older fast. Healthcare, biotech, and robotics will benefit enormously.
Clean Energy
$4 trillion per year in investment expected by 2030. Solar, wind, batteries, and new nuclear technology.
Cybersecurity
Cyberattacks cost $10.5 trillion per year. This industry grows regardless of whether the economy is up or down.
Reshoring
Companies are bringing factories closer to home (Mexico, India, Eastern Europe) after COVID and geopolitical tensions.
Defense & Security
European defense budgets are up 25%+ in 2025-2026. Record orders for aerospace and defense companies.

How to tell a real trend from hype

Check ThisReal TrendHype / Fad
Who is spending money? Big companies (Google, Amazon) are investing billions Only tiny startups with no revenue are involved
Are there ETFs and indices? Pension funds and ETF providers have created dedicated products Mostly retail speculators buying in
Are governments making rules for it? Regulatory frameworks being built (AI Act, IRA, MiCA) No legal framework, gray zone
The 10-year test Will still be around in 10 years (AI, aging, clean energy) Will be a memory in 3 years (metaverse, NFT art)

Fads that burned investors

  • Metaverse (2021-2022): Meta (Facebook) spent $36 billion on it. Almost nobody uses it. The concept was rebranded to "spatial computing."
  • "Blockchain for everything" (2017-2022): People claimed blockchain would fix supply chains, voting, identity, and more. 95% of those projects are dead.
  • Cannabis stocks (2018-2020): Legalization was supposed to create a $100 billion market. Instead, the black market persists, regulations choke margins, and stocks like Tilray lost 90%+.

A simple framework

Will it still be here in 10 years? If yes, invest in it (buy ETFs or the leaders in that space).
Will it be forgotten in 3 years? If yes, maybe trade it short-term with tight stop-losses, or just skip it.
Not sure? Do nothing. Wait for clearer signals. There is no penalty for sitting on the sidelines.

Key Takeaway

  • A real trend lasts 10-20 years. A fad lasts 6-18 months.
  • Check if big companies are spending real money on it. If only small startups and social media are hyping it, be skeptical.
  • AI, clean energy, cybersecurity, and aging populations are real mega-trends.
  • When in doubt, do nothing. The best investments will still be there when you are ready.
Politics and the Market

How Politics Moves the Market

The biggest risk is always the surprise

A new trade policy, an election result, or a war can change the investment landscape overnight. But here is the key insight: the risk is not the event everyone is talking about (that is already "priced in" — the market has already adjusted for it). The real danger is the surprise that nobody saw coming. The events on every front page are already reflected in stock prices. The ones that crash markets are the ones nobody expected.

The 4 biggest political risks for investors

RiskWhat Is at StakeAffected Sectors
Ukraine / Russia Energy prices (oil, gas), food prices (wheat) Energy companies, defense, agriculture
Taiwan / China Computer chips (TSMC makes 90% of advanced chips) Tech, semiconductors, Apple, NVIDIA
Middle East Oil prices (20% of world oil passes through the Strait of Hormuz) Energy, airlines, shipping
US / China trade war Tariffs on imports, supply chain disruptions Tech, autos, consumer goods

The #1 rule about political news

NEVER trade based on a tweet or social media post

When a political leader makes a dramatic announcement (tariffs, sanctions, threats), the market often drops 1-2% immediately. But 60% of political announcements are modified, softened, or reversed within 48 hours. If you sell in panic right after the announcement, you are selling at the worst possible time. Wait 24-48 hours. Let the dust settle. Then decide.

The presidential cycle — a fun pattern

The US stock market has followed a predictable pattern based on the 4-year presidential cycle:

  • Year 1 (just after inauguration): Usually the weakest year. The new president pushes unpopular reforms.
  • Year 2 (midterms): Volatile. Uncertainty about who will control Congress.
  • Year 3 (pre-election): Historically the BEST year (+16.6% average since 1928). The president stimulates the economy to get re-elected.
  • Year 4 (election year): Volatile but generally positive (+7.1% average).

Does this mean you should only invest in year 3?

No! This is a fun statistical pattern, but it should never be your entire strategy. Markets can do anything in any given year. Use it as a piece of context, not as a trading rule. The best approach is to stay invested through all 4 years and use cash reserves to buy opportunities when they appear.

Key Takeaway

  • Politics can move markets overnight, but most political drama fades within 48 hours.
  • Never sell in panic after a tweet or social media post. Wait and assess.
  • The real risk is the surprise nobody is talking about, not the crisis on every front page.
  • Stay invested through political cycles. Patience beats panicking every time.
Regulators: Your Protection

Who Protects You?

Regulators exist to protect you

Financial markets have regulators — government agencies whose job is to protect investors like you. They force companies to publish their financial results honestly, punish manipulators, and make sure your broker is not stealing your money. Knowing who they are and using their free tools gives you an advantage over 95% of investors who never bother to check.

The most important regulators

RegulatorCountryWhat They Do for You
SEC United States Forces US companies to publish their finances publicly. Punishes fraud and insider trading. Website: sec.gov
FINRA United States Regulates brokers. You can check your broker's record at brokercheck.finra.org for free.
AMF France Maintains a "blacklist" of unauthorized financial platforms. Always check before giving money to any platform.
FCA United Kingdom Registers and monitors brokers operating in the UK. Check if your broker is authorized.
ESMA European Union Oversees financial markets across Europe. Sets rules like MiFID II that protect retail investors.

3 free tools to protect yourself

1. FINRA BrokerCheck
brokercheck.finra.org — Before opening an account with any US broker, type their name here. You will see all customer complaints, legal actions, and disciplinary history. If a broker has dozens of complaints, run. It is free and takes 30 seconds.
2. AMF Blacklist (France)
amf-france.org/listes-noires — A regularly updated list of unauthorized financial websites and platforms. Before giving your money to any platform, check this list. It is in French but easy to navigate.
3. SEC EDGAR (Insider Trades)
sec.gov/edgar — Every time a CEO buys or sells their own company's stock, they must report it here within 2 days. When a CEO is buying heavily with their own money, it is a strong positive signal.

Why does this matter to you?

Regulators are your free bodyguards. They exist to make the market fair for everyone. Using their free tools (BrokerCheck, EDGAR, blacklists) gives you an informational advantage that most beginner investors never use. It takes 5 minutes and can save you thousands of dollars.

Key Takeaway

  • Regulators (SEC, FINRA, AMF, FCA, ESMA) exist to protect you. Use their free tools.
  • Always check your broker's background on FINRA BrokerCheck before opening an account.
  • Check the AMF blacklist before using any French financial platform.
  • Watch insider transactions on SEC EDGAR — when CEOs buy their own stock, it is a good sign.
Getting Started: Your First Steps

Time to Take Action

You now understand how the market works, who the players are, and how to avoid the traps. Great! But reading about swimming will never teach you how to swim. It's time to jump in — but smartly. This section shows you how to take your first steps without risking a single cent.

Opening an Account: Where to Start?

What is a brokerage account?

Your regular bank account can't buy stocks. You need a special account called a brokerage account. In France, there are three types — and picking the right one is super important for paying less tax.

The 3 Account Types

BEST CHOICE

PEA (Plan d'Épargne en Actions)

After 5 years, you pay almost no tax on your gains (only 17.2% instead of 30%). On €10,000 of gains, that saves you €1,280 compared to a standard account. You can deposit up to €150,000. The only downside: you can only buy European stocks and certain ETFs.

CTO (Standard Brokerage Account)

You can buy everything: US stocks, crypto, options... But you pay 30% tax on all your gains (the "flat tax"). No deposit limit. Use it alongside your PEA, or to buy US stocks.

Life Insurance (Assurance-vie)

Tax advantages after 8 years, great for passing wealth to your children. Fewer investment choices than PEA or CTO. It's a good complement, but not the first account to open if you want to actively invest.

Practice Risk-Free: Paper Trading

What is paper trading?

It's a stock market simulator. You buy and sell stocks with fake money, but the prices are real. It's exactly like a racing video game: you learn to drive without risking crashing the car. When airline pilots learn to fly, they spend hundreds of hours on a simulator first. Same thing here.

Paper trading lets you:

  • Test your ideas without losing money
  • Learn how to use your broker's platform (one wrong click can be expensive for real!)
  • Discover your style — are you patient or impulsive? You'll find out after a few trades
  • Build confidence before risking your real money

Set yourself a goal: 30 paper trades before putting in real money. For each trade, write in a notebook: why you bought, your target, and the result. After 30 trades, you'll know if your approach works. Most beginners who skip this step lose their money within 6 months.

5 Tax Tips Your Banker Won't Tell You

Two people who buy the same stocks can end up with very different results — because of taxes. Here are 5 simple tips to keep more money in your pocket.

1

Open your PEA now

The 5-year countdown starts when you open it. Even with €10. In 5 years, your gains will be taxed much less. Every day you wait is a day lost.

2

Fill the PEA before the CTO

The PEA has a cap of €150,000. As long as it's not full, every euro in a CTO pays 30% tax instead of 17.2%. You are losing 12.8% of your gains unnecessarily.

3

NEVER withdraw from PEA before 5 years

If you withdraw money before 5 years, your PEA gets permanently closed and you pay 30% tax on all gains. After 5 years, you can withdraw freely.

4

Always declare your losses

If you lost money in the stock market, declare it! Those losses deduct from your future gains for 10 years. It's money the government owes you.

5

Choose "accumulating" ETFs for US stocks

Dividends from US stocks in a PEA are taxed at 15% with no way to get it back. The solution: choose ETFs that reinvest dividends instead of paying them out (e.g., CSPX, PE500). You avoid this invisible tax.

Key Takeaway

  • Open a PEA first, even with €10 — the 5-year tax clock starts immediately
  • Practice with paper trading (30 trades minimum) before risking real money
  • Watch the pros for free via ARK Invest and ETF compositions
  • PEA before CTO — always — it's the golden rule of stock market taxation
Quiz: Test What You Learned

8 Questions to Check Your Understanding

Click each question to reveal the answer. Aim for 6/8 or better before moving on to Part 5.

1. What percentage of US stock trading volume is done by robots (HFT)?
50-70%. High-Frequency Trading algorithms handle the majority of all trading volume in the US. They trade millions of times per day and hold positions for milliseconds. As a regular investor, you do not need to compete with them — your advantage is patience and a long time horizon.
2. An influencer on TikTok tells you to buy a crypto token "before it's too late." What should you do?
Ignore it completely. This is most likely a pump & dump scheme. The influencer was probably paid (in cash or tokens) to promote the project. Their followers become the "exit liquidity" — the last buyers before the price crashes. Rule: never invest based on a social media recommendation from a non-professional.
3. The VIX is at 38. Is this good or bad? What should you do?
The VIX at 38 means the market is very scared (fear level). It feels bad, but historically, this is where great buying opportunities appear. Investors who bought the S&P 500 when the VIX was above 35 made an average of +25% over the next 12 months. What to do: Start looking for quality stocks at a discount, but buy gradually — do not put all your money in at once. And only invest money you will not need for at least 3 years.
4. What is the number one rule when a politician makes a dramatic market-moving announcement?
Wait 24-48 hours before doing anything. 60% of political announcements are modified, softened, or reversed within 48 hours. If you sell in panic right after the news, you are selling at the worst possible time. Let the dust settle, then make a calm, informed decision.
5. What is a pump & dump?
A pump & dump is a scam where someone secretly buys a cheap stock or crypto token, then promotes it aggressively to drive up the price (the "pump"), and finally sells at the top (the "dump"), leaving everyone else with huge losses. Signs: aggressive social media promotion, buzzwords like "moonshot" and "100x", tiny company with no real product, and volume that explodes without any real news.
6. How can you tell a real trend from a passing fad?
Ask 4 questions: (1) Are big companies (Google, Amazon) spending real money on it? (2) Have ETFs and professional indices been created for it? (3) Are governments creating regulations for it? (4) Will it still be here in 10 years? If the answer to most of these is yes, it is likely a real trend. AI, clean energy, and cybersecurity pass this test. The metaverse and NFT art did not.
7. When is it smart to keep 30-50% of your portfolio in cash?
During a bear market or correction (VIX between 25 and 40). Holding cash is a valid strategy when: (1) you do not understand what is happening in the market, (2) you cannot find any good stocks to buy, (3) your current positions are losing money, (4) a major event is coming (Fed meeting, elections), or (5) you just had a big win and need to reset. But remember: staying in cash should be temporary (weeks to months), not permanent.
8. Where can you check if your broker has a clean record?
FINRA BrokerCheck at brokercheck.finra.org. It is completely free. Type in your broker's name and you will see all customer complaints, legal actions, and disciplinary history. If a broker has many complaints, find a different one. For French platforms, also check the AMF blacklist at amf-france.org. It takes 30 seconds and could save you a lot of money.
Glossary

Key Terms Explained Simply

Here are the most important terms from this article, explained in plain English.

TermSimple Explanation
Bear Market When the market falls 20% or more from its peak. Named after a bear swiping downward.
Bull Market When the market rises 20% or more from its low. Named after a bull thrusting its horns upward.
Central Bank A government institution that controls interest rates and money supply (like the Federal Reserve in the US, or the ECB in Europe).
Dark Pool A private stock exchange where big institutions trade without showing their orders publicly. About 30-40% of US stock trades happen in dark pools.
DYOR "Do Your Own Research." A reminder to verify information yourself before investing, instead of trusting someone else blindly.
ETF Exchange-Traded Fund. A basket of stocks you can buy as one single investment. For example, an S&P 500 ETF gives you exposure to the 500 largest US companies in one trade.
Fed / Federal Reserve The US central bank. It sets interest rates and controls money supply. When the Fed speaks, the entire world listens.
FOMO "Fear Of Missing Out." The emotional urge to buy something just because everyone else is buying it. A common trap for beginners.
HFT High-Frequency Trading. Computers that trade thousands of times per second. You cannot compete with them on speed — compete with patience instead.
Insider Trading Illegally using secret company information to trade stocks before that information is made public. A serious crime that can result in prison time.
Liquidity How easy it is to buy or sell something. Apple stock has high liquidity (millions of shares traded daily). A rare painting has low liquidity (might take months to sell).
Market Maker A company that is always ready to buy and sell a stock. They ensure there is always someone on the other side of your trade, so you can buy or sell instantly.
NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls) A monthly US government report showing how many jobs were created. Released on the first Friday of each month. Markets react strongly to it.
"Priced In" When the market has already adjusted for expected news. If everyone expects a rate hike, the stock price already reflects it before the announcement.
Pump & Dump A scam where someone hypes up a stock/crypto, then sells when the price goes up, leaving everyone else with losses.
Retail Investor An individual investor (like you) who is not a professional fund manager. You and me.
Rug Pull A crypto scam where the creators drain all the money from a project overnight and disappear.
S&P 500 An index tracking the 500 largest US companies. The most-watched stock market benchmark in the world.
Short Selling Betting that a stock will go DOWN. You "borrow" shares, sell them now, and buy them back later at a lower price (hopefully). Very risky.
Stop-Loss An automatic order that sells your stock if it drops below a certain price. It limits your maximum loss on any position.
VIX The "fear index." Measures how nervous investors are about the next 30 days. Low = calm, high = scared. Ranges from about 10 to 80.
Wash Trading Buying and selling to yourself using different accounts to fake trading volume. Illegal but common in unregulated crypto.
