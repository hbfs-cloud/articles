"It should fill" is not a reason to buy anything. Yesterday's close is a price that existed. It is not a price the market owes you.

Plenty of gaps do close. The ordinary kind — no real news, thin overnight volume, a bit of drift — come back better than nine times in ten, usually inside a week. A gap that breaks a stock out of its range on heavy volume comes back maybe three or four times in ten, and can take months to do it. The chart will not tell you which one is in front of you. The reason for the gap will.

So read the reason before you draw a single line. The company's own filing or press release. The agency that publishes the macro number, not a headline about it. Whether the security is halted, sitting in an opening auction, or trading normally. The spread, the depth, and the trades that actually printed — not one lonely premarket quote. If the disclosure changed the business behind the old chart, the old chart is scrap paper.

![A fact is not a decision, and a decision is not an order](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hbfs-cloud/articles/main/substack-assets/schematics/decision_flow.png)

Then commit to one plan. These two cannot both be true, and picking after the fact leaves you holding neither.

**Continuation.** Price holds away from the old range and builds a new one. Nvidia raised its guidance in May 2023. The stock closed at $306 and opened the next session at $380, up about 24%. The continuation plan waited for the first five-minute bar to close green, bought at $382, and put invalidation at $374 — the low of that bar, not a round number and not the loss the trader would have preferred. The objective sat at $395, on VWAP (the day's average traded price, weighted by volume) plus an extension. $395 printed inside 40 minutes: 2.1% risked, 3.4% made, about 1.6 back per unit of risk.

**Rejection.** Price cannot hold the gap and falls back into the old range. CME bitcoin futures close Friday at 5 p.m. New York and reopen Sunday at 6 p.m., while spot bitcoin trades straight through. In January 2024 the futures closed Friday at $43,200, spot ran to $45,800 over the weekend on spot-ETF news, and the futures reopened roughly 6% higher. The rejection plan sold near $45,800, aimed at that Friday close of $43,200, and set invalidation at $47,000, one and a half times the size of the gap. Price came back in three days: 2.6% risked, 5.7% made, about 2.2 per unit.

Neither trade was justified by the gap being large. <mark>Each had a named reference price, a trigger, an invalidation taken from structure, and a clock.</mark>

Write the conditions the night before, not at 9:31.

- For continuation: a gap of at least 5%, premarket volume at least twice the 20-day average, a catalyst you can state in one sentence, and a first five-minute bar pointing the same way as the gap. Red bar on a gap up, stand down.
- For rejection: nothing in the first 15 minutes. That window is overnight orders clearing and market makers repricing, and the quotes are at their least reliable.
- Never fade a takeover bid, a regulator's decision, or an earnings surprise far past what was priced. Those changed the business, not just the price.
- Record entry, invalidation, size and every exit — including the one that says the news moved and the setup is void.

Opening structure fails without warning, and the depth you saw can be gone by the time your order lands. Limit orders may not fill. Market and stop orders can fill somewhere you never planned. Options bought after a gap carry inflated premiums and wide spreads. No pattern turns any of that into certainty.

> Trade the reason for the gap, never the size of it.
