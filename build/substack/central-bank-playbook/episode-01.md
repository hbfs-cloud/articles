*Part 1 of 6 in The Central Bank Playbook.*

Mid-2026, six central banks, six different policy rates: the Fed at 4.25-4.50%, the Bank of England at 4.50%, China's loan prime rate at 3.10%, the ECB deposit rate at 2.75%, the Bank of Japan at 0.50%, the Swiss National Bank at 0.25%. Same planet, same year. The spread is not a ranking of competence. It comes from different legal jobs, different economies, different starting points.

<mark>A mandate is the job a law hands the institution.</mark> Read it before you read anything the institution says.

![A fact is not a decision, and a decision is not an order](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hbfs-cloud/articles/main/substack-assets/schematics/decision_flow.png)

The Fed's is the unusual one. Congress gave it two goals at once — maximum employment and stable prices — which is why traders call it the dual mandate. It judges 2% inflation over the longer run, measured by the annual change in the personal consumption expenditures price index, as consistent with that mandate. Note what it does not do: it sets no number for maximum employment, because that level cannot be measured directly and shifts over time.

The ECB's primary objective is price stability in the euro area. Its strategy aims for 2% over the medium term, measured with the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices, and treats overshoots and undershoots as equally unwelcome. "Medium term" is doing real work in that sentence. It buys room for the inflation reading to sit off target for a while without forcing a move.

The 2% figure itself is a convention, not a law of nature. New Zealand's central bank popularised it in 1990. The Bank of England and the ECB followed. The Fed only made it official in 2012.

So write every policy note in four separate fields, and keep them separate.

### Four fields, never blended

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Target | the official goal and the exact measure — longer-run PCE, or medium-term HICP. |
| Tool | the rate, balance-sheet action, lending operation or wording actually used. |
| Event | the document, its publication time, its effective date, its time zone. |
| Interpretation | your reading, labelled as yours, never blended into the first three. |

Test the discipline on an awkward month: inflation running above target while one labour indicator softens. Nothing in that pair obliges the Fed to move either way. First check whether the inflation series you are looking at is the one the Fed actually targets — CPI is not PCE. Then check the rest of the labour evidence, the revisions, the risks, the current statement. "This raises the odds of easing" is a judgement you are making. Record it as one.

The history explains why the rules are written down. Turkey's president forced rate cuts while inflation hit 85% and fired four central bank governors in two years; the lira lost roughly 80%. Paul Volcker did the opposite in 1979, pushing US rates to 20% against 14% inflation, wearing a recession, and getting inflation to 3% by 1983. Independence is what makes the second story possible.

> Target, tool, event, interpretation — four fields, and only the last one is yours.

**Limitation:** a mandate map keeps you from category errors. It cannot tell you the vote, the timing, or how markets will take it. Shocks push inflation and employment in opposite directions, and policy reaches the economy with a lag of roughly 12 to 18 months.
