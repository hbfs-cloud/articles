*Part 4 of 6 in The 30-Second Trade Signal Check.*

Tapestry (`TPR`) closed at $153.74 on August 12, 2026. The company released results before the next
opening bell. The stock opened at $132.70, down 13.69%, and closed at $128.39, down 16.49% from the prior
close.

This case was selected after the event to show the mechanics of a gap. It does not estimate a typical
earnings move or the probability of another one.

![A stop cannot fill where no price exists](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hbfs-cloud/articles/main/substack-assets/schematics/gap_and_stop.png)

No regular-session trade occurred in the $21.04 between the prior close and the new opening price. A
stop placed inside that empty interval could not create a buyer at its chosen level.

A stop-market sell becomes a market order after the trigger condition is met. When a stock opens below
the trigger, the order can execute near the available opening price. A stop-limit order refuses to sell
below its limit, but it may remain unfilled while the stock keeps falling. One accepts price slippage; the
other accepts the risk of no exit.

Check the earnings date before entering a swing trade. When the report falls inside the planned holding
window, choose one treatment and write it down:

- Skip the trade until a post-release price structure exists.
- Exit before the report.
- Reduce the position to a size that can survive a larger gap.
- Hold it as a deliberate event trade and accept that the stop budget may be exceeded.

Reduced size requires a documented stress price and maximum event loss. It lowers exposure but does not
cap the loss. Without a matched protective option, a long stock can theoretically fall to zero.

Options can define some event risk, but the premium, expiry and liquidity change the trade. Price the
hedge rather than assuming it works.

Also check the broker's trigger convention, eligible session and time-in-force. A valid order at one
broker may trigger differently or expire earlier at another.

The calendar itself has limits. Companies can change dates, and unscheduled news can gap any stock.
Avoiding scheduled earnings removes one visible risk; it does not remove overnight risk. A diversified
ETF also reacts differently because one constituent usually has less influence on the whole fund.
