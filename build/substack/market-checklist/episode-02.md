A screen exists to throw things away. It is very good at that and very bad at the other job people give it. First place in a ranking means "look at this next." It never means "buy this."

Hunting by hand does not work either, and the reason is not laziness. You go back to the tickers you already know, the ones you traded last month, the ones in your feed. A screen has no memory and no favourites. It walks the whole list with the same indifference every evening.

![A fact is not a decision, and a decision is not an order](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hbfs-cloud/articles/main/substack-assets/schematics/decision_flow.png)

The 13 March 2026 run went like this: 6,142 symbols in, 847 passed the technical filters, 28 scored 85 or better, 14 survived the risk-off rules, and 10 reached the final list. That is roughly one name kept for every six hundred looked at. You are not reproducing that ratio by scrolling charts.

Split the work into three passes.

Eligibility comes first, and it is decided before you see any results. Tradable universe, allowed instruments, minimum price, minimum liquidity. Tie liquidity to the order you actually intend to send, not to a round number someone posted online. My floor is 10 million dollars of daily traded value; below that the spread eats the trade before the idea gets to play out. In risk-off conditions I also drop anything under 5 billion in market value, because institutional bids leave those names first.

Ranking comes second, and only on features tied to the setup. Trend persistence for a continuation trade. Compression for a breakout watch. Relative strength for a leadership list. Keep the inputs visible. A composite score of 93 out of 100, as one energy name printed that March session, is only useful if you can see it came from technical structure at 25% weight, volume at 20%, momentum at 20%, risk and range at 15%, liquidity at 10%, conviction at 10%. A score of 84 is a rejection. No rounding up, ever, or the threshold stops meaning anything.

The same setup does not work in every kind of market. On my own scans, oversold bounces hit about three times in four during risk-off stretches. Momentum expansion managed 43%. Breakout squeezes, 25%, and worse when volatility was high.

Verification comes last, and it is the pass most people skip. Open the issuer's filings in EDGAR. Read the latest annual and quarterly reports and any recent 8-K. Check the next earnings date, any new share offering, changes to the share count, new risk language. A small-cap ranked first that week on price near its range high and heavy volume. EDGAR showed a fresh registration for new shares. That does not prove the price will fall. It changes the supply and it changes the event risk, so the name moved from "write an entry plan" to "manual review." Since the strategy had no rule for financing risk, it was rejected.

Before you rank anything:

- Freeze the universe and the liquidity rules, then run the screen.
- Keep eligibility fields and ranking fields in separate columns.
- Check filings, event dates, spread, depth, and how the product is built.
- Save the timestamp, and record the reason for every manual override.

Limitation: a screen cannot see a change that has not reached its data yet, and delayed quotes make liquidity look better than it is. It will also throw out an excellent but unusual setup now and then. Overrides are fine when written down before entry; silent ones destroy the record. In March 2026 the eight European and Asian names I screened produced nothing at all, which is a small sample and a reason to look harder, not a law.
