The Illusion of The Crash

In August 2020, Apple (AAPL) executed a 4-for-1 stock split. The price went from roughly $500 on Friday to $125 on Monday. If our Go opportunity-analyzer looks purely at unadjusted closing prices, the resulting momentum (-75% return) guarantees a massive "Sell/Short" signal, triggering catastrophic live trading execution.

Calculating Cumulative Adjustment Factors

Instead of destructively overwriting the database history, we append a new pipeline step calculating the Cumulative Ratio Factor (CRAS). This guarantees our Execution Engine (Part 6) still knows exactly what physical price level to send Limit Orders at today, while our AI (Part 5) reads perfectly continuous "Adjusted" features.

-- models/silver/fct_corporate_actions
WITH splits AS (
    SELECT symbol, ex_date, split_ratio FROM raw_zone.corporate_actions WHERE type = 'SPLIT'
),
cumulative_factors AS (
    SELECT 
        symbol,
        ex_date,
        -- EXP(SUM(LN(factor))) is the mathematically stable way to do cumulative products in SQL
        EXP(SUM(LN(split_ratio)) OVER (
            PARTITION BY symbol 
            ORDER BY ex_date DESC
            ROWS BETWEEN CURRENT ROW AND UNBOUNDED FOLLOWING
        )) AS cumulative_split_factor
    FROM splits
)
SELECT * FROM cumulative_factors;

By joining this factor to the Gold Mart (Part 3), the AI Agent successfully computes that a historical $100 price, subjected to a cumulative 4x split, is mathematically equivalent to $25 today. The AI’s RSI and Bollinger Bands algorithms continue uninterrupted.

Conclusion

Our database now maps the raw reality of the exchange alongside the mathematically continuous reality required by neural networks. No biases exist.
