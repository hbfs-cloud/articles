#1

              
Adversarial analysis

            

            Most important
            
          

          

            
Forces the AI to become a bear analyst. The most important prompt — use it BEFORE every buy. "Role prompting" technique: assigning a role to the AI dramatically changes the quality of the response.

            
I want to buy [TICKER] at [PRICE]. Play the role of an aggressive bear analyst hired by a short-selling hedge fund. Give me 7 concrete reasons why this trade could fail:
— 2 macro risks (cycle, rates, geopolitics)
— 2 sector risks (competition, disruption, regulation)
— 2 stock-specific risks (valuation, insider selling, balance sheet quality)
— 1 risk that bulls systematically ignore
For each risk, quantify the potential impact on the stock price (in %). Don't be polite, be honest. Conclude with: "If you still buy after all this, your stop should be at [level]."

#2

              
"What am I missing?"

            

            Anti-bias
            
          

          

            
The "blind spot prompt" — the best weapon against confirmation bias. Use it AFTER every analysis, when you are convinced of your thesis. AI excels at finding what you are not looking for.

            
I am convinced that [TICKER] will [go up/go down] because [YOUR THESIS IN 2-3 SENTENCES]. Now, tear my thesis apart. Specifically:
1. What verifiable fact directly contradicts my main argument?
2. What competitor or substitute am I underestimating?
3. What implicit assumption in my reasoning could be wrong?
4. What calendar event (earnings, expiry, unlock, macro) could invalidate my thesis before it plays out?
5. If I am wrong, at what price will I know it? (technical invalidation level)
Don't give me a diplomatic answer. If my thesis is solid, say so — but if it has holes, show them.

#3

              
Sell-side initiation note

            

            Pro-grade
            
          

          

            
"Role prompting" taken to the extreme: the AI writes like a sell-side analyst at Goldman/Morgan Stanley. Result: professional structure, DCF, comps, dated catalysts.

            
You are a senior equity analyst at Goldman Sachs. Write an initiation-of-coverage note on [TICKER] in institutional format:

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (5 lines max)
RECOMMENDATION: Buy / Neutral / Sell — Price Target: $[X] (upside/downside %)

1. INVESTMENT THESIS (3 bullets max)
2. BUSINESS MODEL — Break down revenue by segment, geography, recurring vs one-time. MOAT: pricing power, switching costs, network effects?
3. FINANCIALS — Table: Revenue, EBITDA, EPS, net margin, FCF yield over 3 years (historical + 2 years forward). Bold the inflection points.
4. VALUATION — DCF (WACC, terminal growth, fair value) + comparables (EV/EBITDA, P/E, PEG vs peers). What multiple is the market pricing in? Is it justified?
5. CATALYSTS — 3 dated events that could move the stock (earnings, product, macro, M&A)
6. RISKS — Top 3 with probability (high/medium/low) and impact ($)
7. CONCLUSION — Conviction 1-10 and recommended time horizon

#11

              
Rug pull detector

            

            Danger
            
          

          

            
7 binary YES/NO questions to evaluate a suspicious token. Score ≥4 = do not touch. This scoring comes from Le Guide du Spéculateur and has filtered out dozens of rug pulls.

            
[TOKEN] is buzzing on social media and I am tempted. Before I make a mistake, answer these 7 questions with YES or NO:

1. Is the team anonymous (no LinkedIn, no verifiable track record)?
2. Is the contract unaudited OR audited by an unknown auditor?
3. Is the LP liquidity unlocked or locked for <6 months?
4. Do the top 5 wallets hold >50% of the supply?
5. Has the token existed for <30 days?
6. Is >80% of the volume concentrated on a single DEX/CEX?
7. Are there suspicious functions in the contract (unlimited mint, pause trading, blacklist, hidden fee)?

SCORE: Number of YES / 7
— 0-1: Acceptable risk → max sizing 2% of portfolio
— 2-3: High risk → max sizing 0.5%, mental stop -30%
— 4+: DANGER → Do not touch. Look for the next opportunity.

If score ≤3: still provide entry, stop (-30%), TP (+100% minimum to justify the risk), and sizing as % of portfolio.

#13

              
Insider buying signal

            

            Signal
            
          

          

            
Detection of significant insider purchases. Key rule: a single buy means nothing. What matters is cluster buying (≥3 insiders within 5 days) and amounts (>$500K = strong signal).

            
Analyze insider activity for [TICKER] over the last 6 months (SEC Form 4):

1. VOLUME — Total dollar amount of buys vs sells. Buy/sell ratio.
2. WHO — Which insiders bought? (CEO/CFO/Board/10%+ holder). A CEO buy > a Board buy.
3. CLUSTER — Were there ≥3 different insiders buying within a 5-day window? (= strongest signal)
4. AMOUNT — Individual purchases >$500K? (>$1M = very strong conviction)
5. CONTEXT — Were the purchases near the current price or much lower? (purchases near the current price = more significant)
6. TRACK RECORD — Have these insiders been right in the past? (average performance 6 months after their previous purchases)

VERDICT:
— STRONG SIGNAL (cluster + large amounts + positive track record) → Trade with conviction
— MODERATE SIGNAL (a few isolated purchases) → Watch, not sufficient on its own
— NO SIGNAL (net selling or routine buys/exercised options)

If STRONG SIGNAL: entry at current price, stop below the insiders' average purchase price, TP = consensus target.

Buyout rumor / M&A

            

            Special
            
          

          

            
Evaluate the credibility of a buyout rumor and the target price. Includes antitrust analysis (HHI) and the deal vs no-deal scenario — essential for calibrating risk/reward.

5. DEAL vs NO-DEAL SCENARIO:

#15

              
Market regime detection

            

            Macro
            
          

          

            
8 quantitative indicators to determine whether we are in Risk-On, Neutral or Risk-Off. This framework is used daily by the Market Watch scanner to adapt strategies to the current regime.

            
Answer these 8 questions with CURRENT NUMBERS to determine the market regime:

1. VIX: Current level? (<15 = Risk-On, 15-25 = Neutral, >25 = Risk-Off, >35 = Panic)
2. YIELD CURVE: 10Y-2Y spread? (inverted = recession likely, steepening = expansion)
3. CREDIT: HY (High Yield) vs Investment Grade spread? (<300bps = healthy, >500bps = stress)
4. BREADTH: % of S&P 500 stocks above their 200-day EMA? (>60% = healthy, <40% = weakness)
5. MOMENTUM: S&P 500 above or below its 50-day and 200-day EMA?
6. VOLUMES: 5-day average volume vs 20-day? (ratio >1.3 = heightened nervousness)
7. CURRENCIES: DXY 30-day trend? (rising = global risk-off, falling = risk-on)
8. COMMODITIES: Gold 30-day trend? (rising = flight to safety)

REGIME VERDICT: Risk-On / Neutral / Risk-Off / Panic
RECOMMENDED POSITIONING:
— Risk-On → overweight growth, tech, small caps, crypto. Underweight defensives.
— Neutral → balanced portfolio, no directional bet.
— Risk-Off → overweight defensives, gold, TLT, cash. Reduce leverage and position size.
— Panic → 30%+ cash, active hedges (puts, VXX), only buy on capitulation.

#16

              
Technical trade plan

            

            Trading
            
          

          

            
Turns an "I like this ticker" into a structured trade plan with entry levels, stop and targets. Without a written plan, you trade with your gut — and your gut is wrong 60% of the time.

            
[TICKER] is at [CURRENT PRICE]. I am [BULLISH/BEARISH]. Build a complete trade plan:

1. KEY LEVELS — Identify the 3 most relevant supports and 3 resistances (exact prices). Method: EMA 20/50/200, pivots, prior volume levels, unfilled gaps.
2. ENTRY — Optimal entry zone [price - price]. Justify: why this level and not another? What confirmation signal to wait for? (candle, volume, breakout)
3. STOP LOSS — Technical invalidation level (not an arbitrary %). Placed below which support / above which resistance? Loss in % and $.
4. TARGETS —
   TP1: [price] (+X%) — take 50% of position
   TP2: [price] (+X%) — take 30%
   TP3: [price] (+X%) — let 20% run with trailing stop
5. R/R RATIO — Calculated precisely. If R/R < 1.5:1, tell me the trade is not worth the risk.
6. TIMING — Which timeframe? (scalp / intraday / swing / position). Is there a calendar catalyst to respect?
7. MANAGEMENT — When to raise the stop? At what level to move to breakeven? Conditions to cut before the stop.

Format: summary table Entry | Stop | TP1 | TP2 | TP3 | R/R at the bottom.

#17

              
Position sizing & risk management

            

            Survival
            
          

          

            
The prompt that 90% of beginners forget. Position sizing IS your risk management. Includes the simplified Kelly formula and the 2% rule.

            
My total portfolio: [AMOUNT $/€]. My current open positions: [LIST + % ALLOCATION].

I want to take a trade on [TICKER] with entry at [PRICE] and stop at [STOP].

Calculate:
1. RISK PER TRADE — Loss in $ if stop is hit. Maximum acceptable = 1-2% of portfolio. If the trade exceeds 2%, say so clearly.
2. POSITION SIZE — Exact number of shares/units to respect the 2% rule. Amount in $ and % of portfolio.
3. SIMPLIFIED KELLY — With an estimated win rate of [X%] and R/R of [Y:1], what is the Kelly fraction? (Kelly% = Win% - (1-Win%)/R). If Kelly > 5%, cap at 5%.
4. PORTFOLIO HEAT — Total open risk (sum of losses if all stops are hit). If heat > 6% of portfolio = ALERT, reduce.
5. CORRELATION — Is this new trade correlated with existing positions? If yes, cut size in half.
6. CATASTROPHE SCENARIO — If ALL my positions hit their stop simultaneously: total drawdown in % and $. Is this survivable?

SUMMARY: Recommended position = [X] shares / [Y $] / [Z%] of portfolio.

#18

              
Pre-earnings setup

            

            Earnings
            
          

          

            
Prepare a trade before quarterly earnings. The real edge is in analyzing the "implied move" vs the historically realized move — if the market is overpaying for volatility, sell it. Otherwise, buy it.

            
[TICKER] reports earnings on [DATE]. Current price: [PRICE].

PRE-EARNINGS ANALYSIS:
1. CONSENSUS — Expected EPS: ? | Expected revenue: ? | Expected margin: ? (source: Yahoo Finance / Seeking Alpha)
2. SURPRISE HISTORY — Table of the last 4 quarters:
   | Quarter | Est. EPS | Actual EPS | Surprise % | Day+1 Move |
3. WHISPER vs CONSENSUS — Is the "real" number expected by the market above the official consensus?
4. IMPLIED MOVE — ATM straddle price / stock price = implied move in %. Compare with the average realized move over 4 quarters. If implied > realized: vol is expensive → sell (short straddle/iron condor). If implied < realized: vol is cheap → buy (long straddle/strangle).
5. IV RANK — Current IV vs 52-week range. IV Rank > 70% = expensive vol, < 30% = cheap vol.
6. POSITIONING — Unusual open interest? Call/put ratio? Gamma exposure?
7. RECOMMENDED STRATEGY —
   — If bullish + expensive vol: bull put spread
   — If bullish + cheap vol: long OTM call
   — If neutral + expensive vol: iron condor / short straddle
   — If unsure: stay flat, trade post-earnings the next day
8. TIMING — Enter the day before? Morning of? Or wait for after-hours and trade the next day?

            Dividend
            
          

          

            
Detects "yield traps" — a yield >8% is a warning sign, not an opportunity. Checks FCF coverage, streak, and imminent cut signals.

            
[TICKER] shows a yield of [X%]. Before I fall into the yield trap, audit this dividend:

   ☐ Sector in structural difficulty
   ☐ Buyback suspended (often the first sign before a dividend cut)

Key dates: next ex-date, next payment date.

#20

              
Valuation reality check

            

            Fundamental
            
          

          

            
5 cross-referenced valuation methods to know if a stock is expensive or not. The most powerful question: "what growth rate is the market pricing in?" (reverse DCF). If the answer is unrealistic, the stock is too expensive.

            
[TICKER] trades at [PRICE]. Is it expensive? Answer with 5 methods:

1. COMPARABLES — Table:
   | Metric | [TICKER] | Peer 1 | Peer 2 | Peer 3 | Sector Median |
   | Fwd P/E | | | | | |
   | EV/EBITDA | | | | | |
   | P/FCF | | | | | |
   | PEG | | | | | |
   Premium or discount in % vs median. Is the premium JUSTIFIED? (superior growth, moat, quality)

2. HISTORICAL — Current P/E vs 5-year average P/E. How many standard deviations above/below the mean? (>2σ = extreme)

3. SIMPLIFIED DCF — Assumptions: FCF growth [X%] over 5 years, terminal growth 2.5%, WACC [Y%]. Fair value: $[Z]. Upside/downside: [%].

4. REVERSE DCF — This is THE key question: at the current price, what annual growth rate is the market implicitly pricing in over 5 years? If > 25%/yr → the market is very optimistic. If < 5%/yr → the market is pessimistic. Is this rate realistic given the track record?

5. SUM-OF-THE-PARTS — If multi-segment: value each segment separately. Is there a conglomerate discount? A hidden segment worth more than the total market cap?

VERDICT: ☐ Undervalued ☐ Fair value ☐ Overvalued ☐ Bubble
Estimated buy zone: $[X] - $[Y] (15-25% discount vs fair value for margin of safety).

#21

              
Biotech: binary event

            

            Biotech
            
          

          

            
Biotechs are binary bets: +200% or -80% on an FDA/Phase III result. This prompt forces an expected value (EV) calculation to determine if the bet is mathematically profitable, even with a low probability of success.

            
[TICKER] is a biotech with an upcoming binary catalyst: [PHASE RESULT/PDUFA/DATA READOUT].

BINARY ANALYSIS:
1. PIPELINE — Candidate table:
   | Molecule | Indication | Phase | Market Size (TAM) | Results Date | Historical Success Probability by Phase |
   Reminder average probabilities: Phase I→II: 52%, Phase II→III: 28%, Phase III→Approval: 58%, PDUFA: 85%

2. SUCCESS SCENARIO — If positive result:
   — Estimated peak sales ($)
   — Revenue multiple for biotech at this stage (pre-revenue: 5-10x, commercial: 3-5x)
   — Target price on success: $[X] (upside: +Y%)

3. FAILURE SCENARIO — If negative result:
   — How much is the remaining pipeline worth? (cash + other candidates)
   — Estimated floor price: $[X] (downside: -Y%)

4. EXPECTED VALUE — EV = (success probability × upside) + (failure probability × downside)
   — If EV > 0: the bet is mathematically profitable
   — If EV < 0: even with the upside, the risk is not compensated

5. CASH RUNWAY — Current cash: $[X]M. Quarterly burn rate: $[Y]M. Runway: [Z] quarters. Dilution risk if <4 quarters.

6. TRADE — If EV > 0: max sizing 2-3% of portfolio (binary event = reduced sizing). Entry / Stop (if applicable — often no stop on binary) / TP.

Whale watching

            

            On-chain
            
          

          

            
Whales (wallets >$10M) are crypto's insiders. When they accumulate, it's bullish. When they deposit on exchanges, it's bearish. This prompt analyzes on-chain movements to detect accumulation or distribution.

            
Analyze whale movements on [TOKEN]:

1. EXCHANGE FLOWS — Inflows vs outflows on centralized exchanges (last 7 days):
   — Net inflow (deposits > withdrawals) = sell pressure → BEARISH
   — Net outflow (withdrawals > deposits) = accumulation → BULLISH
   Quantify in $ and % of circulating supply.

2. TOP WALLETS — Activity of the 20 largest non-exchange wallets:
   — How many have accumulated over 30 days?
   — How many have distributed?
   — Are there NEW wallets >$1M that appeared recently?

3. SMART MONEY — Historically profitable wallets (early entrants, known VCs):
   — What are they doing right now? Accumulating, holding, or selling?
   — Any movements from known VC wallets (a16z, Paradigm, Binance Labs)?

4. STABLECOIN FLOW — Are stablecoins flowing toward DEX/CEX? (dry powder = potential buyers)

5. LIQUIDATION MAP — At what price levels are the most long/short liquidations pending? Is price gravitating toward these levels?

6. FUNDING RATE — Positive (longs pay shorts) = market overleveraged long → risk of squeeze down. Negative = the opposite.

SIGNAL: ☐ Whale accumulation ☐ Distribution ☐ Neutral
If accumulation detected: entry zone and recommended sizing.
