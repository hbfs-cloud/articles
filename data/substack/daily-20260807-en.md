# The line was 45%. It's Friday.

*US prices are 6 August closes; commodities, rates and FX are live 7 August, read 07:37–07:49 UTC. Polymarket read 07:44 UTC.*

On Wednesday we wrote down the number that would prove us wrong:

> If the probability of a September hike falls below 45% by Friday while oil stays low, then the energy channel was working and we were wrong to dismiss it.

It is Friday. The September hike prices at **48.5%**. And oil did not stay low: Brent gained 4.07% on Thursday, and the crude ETF we quoted on Wednesday closed up 3.47% at 118.87.

Neither condition was met, so the call stands. That is not a victory lap. 48.5% against 48.5% for no change is a coin flip, which is exactly what we said it was.

## The part we got right by accident

We told readers to take something off MHK at 136.47, because it sat 1.8% from target and the half-size rule had been written before the week got good.

MHK closed Thursday at **132.42**. Anyone who followed that kept roughly 3% they would otherwise have handed back. We would like to call it foresight. It was a rule doing its job, which is the only kind of foresight that repeats.

## The new number: 12.66

The options market quotes a price for the next nine days of index movement: **12.66**. For the next six months: **20.91**. A rising curve like that is normal and means nothing on its own.

What those nine days contain is not normal.

- **Today, 8:30 New York.** The jobs report.
- **Wednesday 12 August.** Inflation.

Those are the two releases that settle a Federal Reserve meeting currently priced at 48.5% hike, 48.5% no change, 1.75% cut. The Fed held on 29 July with three officials dissenting in favour of a rise.

So short-dated protection is being sold at about 0.8% of expected movement per day, across a fortnight holding both prints.

Every other market already took a side:

- **Gold +1.09% and silver +3.80%** this morning, with the dollar flat at 99.96. A metal that rises without a currency move is hedging demand, not arithmetic.
- **The 5-year Treasury yield rose 6.5bp** on Thursday, more than the 10-year (5.3bp) and the 30-year (3.9bp). When the belly of the curve leads, the market is repricing the path of policy rates, not distant inflation.
- **Brent at 83.31**, up 4.07% on Thursday, on a strait that stays shut. Odds of Hormuz traffic returning to normal by 31 August: **12.5%**, unchanged in four days, while the odds of the ceasefire holding rose from 91% to 96.8%. Peace without the shipping lane is a floor under crude, not a resolution.

Four markets have positioned. The fifth is charging 12.66.

## Underneath the calm

On Thursday the S&P 500 moved −0.18%.

Also on Thursday: AppLovin fell **19.66%**, setting a new 52-week low intraday at 332.19 and closing 1% above it; Western Digital fell **13.03%** after covering a 15.5% range inside the session; SanDisk fell **6.81%**.

Volatility has not left the market. It moved out of the index and into the names. That is the configuration in which index protection is structurally cheap and stock picking is structurally dangerous.

Three names are not a theme, and we are not going to turn them into one. We have no confirmed catalyst to cite on AppLovin this morning; what is measurable is a forward earnings multiple of 15.8 against 32.2 on trailing results, which describes one stock being re-rated. The other two sell memory.

## What we are doing about it

Half size until 12 August on everything that lives off the cost of money, the consumer cycle or industrial volumes. No cleverness beyond that.

**The entry tolerance matters more than the printed R/R.** A trigger order filled 1.5% above the top of the zone destroys the ratio: at that price **seven of the eight lines fall below 1:1** — EWS 0.79, CPER 0.79, NSC 0.80, KBC 0.82, ROST 0.83, ITX 0.86, LMT 0.94, and only JCI holds at 1.06. The threshold that preserves at least 1:1.25 is far tighter: **+0.5% above the zone** for six of them, +0.66% for LMT, +0.90% for JCI. Past that, don't take it.

**Timing.** ITX.MC and KBC.BR trade from 09:00 Paris — their orders trigger *five hours before* the jobs number. Take them knowing that, or wait until Monday. The six US lines trigger between the 8:30 release and the 9:30 open, on a thin book: trigger orders only, never market orders.

**Two of these may not be buyable.** CPER and EWS are US-listed funds with no European key information document, and most European brokers block them for retail accounts. Check before building anything on them.

**Currency.** For a euro account, ITX.MC and KBC.BR are the *only* two lines with no FX exposure; the other six add EUR/USD to the result. For a dollar account it is the reverse. The stop protects the price, not the currency.

The five at half size are not bad lines. JCI carries the highest score of the eight and set its day high on Thursday at 155.98. They simply share the factor our own thesis says is exposed, and the scan lists a hot inflation print among JCI's own invalidations. When the signal and the thesis flag the same risk, you cut the size rather than the position.

Be clear about what that buys you: cutting five of eight to half size removes roughly 30% of the basket's exposure. It is not a hedge. A shock to the rate path hits all eight, banks included, just in the other direction.
