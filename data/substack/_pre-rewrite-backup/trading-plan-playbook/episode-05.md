*Part 5 of 6 in Build a Trading Plan You Can Execute.*

Admit a symbol to the watchlist only when it passes the plan's eligibility rules and has four recorded fields: context, trigger, invalidation, and expiry. A list of interesting names is not preparation. If a candidate has no decision attached, remove it until the research is complete.

Begin with the tradable universe. Filter for products permitted by the account and understood by the trader. Then apply the strategy's liquidity and event rules. Liquidity is not a permanent label; FINRA frames liquidity risk as the difficulty of cashing out when needed. Volume, quoted size, spread, and trading conditions can change, so the check belongs both at watchlist creation and immediately before an order.

For individual public companies, anchor event research in issuer filings. Investor.gov identifies Forms 10-K and 10-Q as periodic reports and Form 8-K as a source of current material disclosures. A watchlist note should link the filing or issuer release actually reviewed and record when it was available. A headline or later summary is not a substitute for contemporaneous evidence.

Consider a hypothetical candidate entered on Monday:

- **Context:** the stock is consolidating after a filed company update; no conclusion is drawn from the update alone.
- **Trigger:** a trade above the recorded range boundary while the spread remains within the strategy's predeclared tolerance.
- **Invalidation:** a separate price level that defeats the chart thesis.
- **Expiry:** remove after Thursday's close or earlier if a new filing changes the premise.

On Tuesday, the stock gaps above the trigger before the planned order can be evaluated. The trigger was not permission to buy at any price. The trader applies the order and risk rules again; if the new entry would violate them, the correct watchlist action is no trade. Record that non-trade so later review does not count only convenient signals.

Run a brief maintenance routine:

- Remove expired, ineligible, and invalidated candidates.
- Add the direct source and timestamp for material information.
- Update levels only under the plan's stated method.
- Set alerts at decision points, not at arbitrary prices.
- Keep skipped triggers and the reason for skipping them.

**Limitation:** a disciplined watchlist does not make the candidates representative or predictive. Screening can concentrate exposure in one theme, and repeated familiarity can create unwarranted confidence. New disclosures, halts, or abrupt liquidity changes can invalidate preparation before execution. Recheck the evidence rather than treating the list as authorization.
