*Part 1 of 6 in Build a Trading Plan You Can Execute.*

Write the trading plan before choosing today's candidate. The plan should decide which instruments are eligible, what constitutes a valid setup, how much capital may be exposed, what cancels a trade, and when results will be reviewed. If those decisions remain open after a price starts moving, the document is commentary, not a control.

A useful plan is an operating specification. Start with constraints that do not depend on a chart: account type, permitted products, use of leverage, trading hours, liquidity requirements, and the maximum loss you are willing and able to plan for. FINRA defines risk broadly as the possibility of a negative financial outcome that matters to you. Your limit therefore belongs to your finances and risk capacity; no universal percentage can supply it.

Then define the decision chain:

- **Universe:** instruments you understand and can trade under your account rules.
- **Setup:** observable conditions that make a candidate eligible.
- **Trigger:** the event that permits an order, not a prediction that price will cooperate.
- **Invalidation:** the market condition that defeats the trade idea.
- **Size:** the smaller amount allowed by planned loss, liquidity, and portfolio limits.
- **Exit:** instructions for invalidation, profit taking, time expiry, and exceptional events.

Consider a hypothetical long trade in a liquid stock. The planned entry is $50.20 and the invalidation level is $49.70, so the planned price risk is $0.50 per share. If this trader's independently chosen loss budget is $200, the arithmetic ceiling is 400 shares: $200 divided by $0.50. That is a ceiling, not an instruction. Expected fees, slippage, existing exposure, and the possibility of a gap through the exit can all require a smaller position or no trade.

Run this short plan check before an order:

- Confirm the instrument and setup are permitted.
- Record trigger, invalidation, and every exit path.
- Calculate size from the risk budget, then apply tighter portfolio and liquidity caps.
- Name any scheduled event that changes the decision.
- Define when the plan will be reviewed, without rewriting it during a live trade.

**Limitation:** a written plan cannot make a strategy profitable or force execution at a chosen price. It only makes decisions auditable. A sound plan may also require discretion for trading halts, broker outages, corporate actions, or new information that invalidates the original assumptions. Record such departures instead of pretending they followed the rule.
