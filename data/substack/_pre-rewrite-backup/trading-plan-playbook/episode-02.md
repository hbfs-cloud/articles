*Part 2 of 6 in Build a Trading Plan You Can Execute.*

Do not label a chart pattern an edge until a repeatable rule and an adequate record support it. First write the setup so another informed trader could classify the same historical observations without asking what you meant. A setup should specify context, eligibility, trigger, invalidation, and measurement. It does not become profitable merely because its story sounds plausible.

Keep three ideas separate. A **hypothesis** explains why a behavior might persist. A **setup** converts that hypothesis into observable conditions. An **edge** is a favorable distribution estimated from relevant evidence after costs and with uncertainty disclosed. One winning trade proves none of these.

Use this specification:

1. Name the eligible instruments and exclusions, including liquidity and event constraints.
2. Define context with data available before the decision.
3. List every required condition using values that can be recovered later.
4. State the trigger and the latest time it remains valid.
5. State invalidation independently of the desired position size.
6. Define how fills, costs, exits, and non-trades will be recorded.

For a hypothetical example, suppose a researcher wants to study breakouts after a multi-session range. The draft rule says: eligible common stocks only; no unresolved trading halt; the prior sessions must fit the predeclared range test; an entry may be considered only after a trade above the recorded boundary; invalidation is a return below the separately recorded level; the signal expires at the session close. The exact lookback and thresholds must be chosen before collecting results, then retained for losing as well as winning cases. Changing them after seeing outcomes creates a new setup and requires a new test.

Fundamental context needs the same discipline. For a public company, use filed information rather than a social-media summary. SEC materials describe Forms 10-K and 10-Q as periodic reports and Form 8-K as a source of current material disclosures. Record the filing accession or direct link and the time it became available. A later filing cannot justify an earlier decision.

Before accepting a setup, check:

- Can each condition be marked true or false from contemporaneous data?
- Are skipped signals retained, not silently discarded?
- Are costs and failed executions represented?
- Is the invalidation based on the thesis rather than the preferred share count?
- Would one rule still apply after an unattractive result?

**Limitation:** reproducibility does not establish economic value. Market structure, participants, volatility, and costs can change. A narrow sample may also be dominated by one issuer or regime. Treat the written setup as a testable claim, not a timeless market law.
