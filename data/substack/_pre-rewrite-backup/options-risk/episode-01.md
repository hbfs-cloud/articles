*Part 1 of 6 in Options Without the Hidden Risk.*

Before opening an option, write down four facts: what the holder may do, what the writer must do, what one contract delivers, and when the contract expires. If any answer comes from assumption rather than the contract specification, stop. A familiar ticker does not make its options familiar.

A call gives its holder the right to buy the underlying at the strike. A put gives its holder the right to sell. The writer takes the other side of that right and can be assigned. For standard US equity options, one contract usually represents 100 shares, but corporate actions can change the strike or deliverable. Index and ETF options can also differ in exercise style and settlement. Check the exact product instead of carrying one rule across every chain.

Consider a hypothetical standard equity call with a $55 strike and a $2.40 quoted premium. One contract costs $240 before fees because the quote is per share and the multiplier is 100. If held to expiration, the call buyer's gross breakeven is $57.40. A close at $56 does not produce a profit at expiration: the $1 of intrinsic value is smaller than the $2.40 paid. If the option expires out of the money, the buyer can lose the full $240.

The writer's economics are different. A short call collects the premium but accepts an obligation. If assigned, the writer must deliver the specified shares or settle in cash, depending on the product. A covered call has stock available for delivery, yet it still carries the stock's downside and gives up gains above the strike. An uncovered call can have theoretically unlimited loss because the underlying price has no fixed ceiling.

Premium also has two components. Intrinsic value is the amount an option is already in the money. Time value is the amount above intrinsic value. Time value can disappear by expiration even when the directional thesis is broadly right. That is why “the stock rose” and “the call made money” are not equivalent statements.

Use this contract check:

- Confirm the underlying, strike, expiration, multiplier and deliverable.
- Mark the option as American- or European-style and note the settlement method.
- Calculate premium paid or received in dollars, not quote points.
- Write the holder's right and the writer's assignment obligation.
- Calculate expiration breakeven and the worst contractual outcome you can identify.

The limitation is important: expiration arithmetic does not predict the option's resale value before expiration. Implied volatility, remaining time, rates, dividends, liquidity and changing sensitivities all matter. The contract check prevents category errors; it does not forecast price.
