*Part 2 of 6 in Bonds and Rates for Equity Traders.*

Use a duration measure that matches the instrument's cash-flow behavior, not maturity alone. Modified duration is the first-pass measure for fixed cash flows; effective duration is more appropriate when calls, prepayments, or other embedded options can change those cash flows. The operating approximation is: percentage price change equals negative duration multiplied by the yield change expressed as a decimal. Treat that result as a local estimate, not a loss guarantee.

Fixed-rate bond prices and market yields generally move in opposite directions. When comparable new bonds offer a higher yield, an older fixed coupon becomes less attractive unless its price falls. When market yields decline, the fixed cash flows become more valuable. The coupon does not change; the price required to produce the market yield does.

Yield and return are also different. Current yield divides annual coupon income by market price. Yield to maturity discounts the promised coupons and principal back to the purchase price under stated assumptions. Total return includes income, price movement and reinvestment, less costs and taxes. A high quoted yield can coexist with a negative holding-period return if price falls enough.

Modified duration summarizes first-order sensitivity to a change in yield. Longer-dated cash flows usually increase duration, while a larger coupon brings more cash forward and usually reduces it. Convexity describes the curvature that the straight-line duration estimate leaves out. Calls, prepayments and other options can alter both measures because the expected cash-flow dates may change.

Run a relative stress test on two bond funds. From each official fact sheet or prospectus, record effective duration, yield measure, credit mix, maturity profile and option exposure. Apply the same modest upward yield shock to each duration. The fund with greater duration produces the larger estimated price decline. Repeat with a downward shock, then ask whether credit spreads or nonparallel curve moves could dominate the rate effect. No forecast is required; the exercise reveals the risk already owned.

Before taking duration exposure:

- Verify whether the published measure is effective or modified duration.
- Pair duration with the exact yield and benchmark used.
- Check credit quality and embedded options.
- Stress both rising and falling yields.
- Add income, expenses and the intended holding period to the review.

Holding an individual bond to maturity can reduce concern about interim price marks only if the issuer pays as promised and the bond is not sold or called. A bond fund does not promise to return a fixed principal amount on a personal maturity date; its portfolio and net asset value continue to change.

Duration has a firm limitation. It is most useful for small yield changes and a specified curve shift. Large moves, changing spreads, nonparallel shifts, liquidity and option exercise can produce a materially different price. It measures rate sensitivity, not the full risk of the position.
