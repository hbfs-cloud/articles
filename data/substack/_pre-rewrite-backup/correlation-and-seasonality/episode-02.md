*Part 2 of 6 in Correlation and Seasonality Without Storytelling.*

Never make a gold decision from one move in the dollar, real yields, or the yield curve. Measure each relationship over a declared window, check whether it is currently stable, and size the position from the traded instrument’s own risk. These variables can move together, diverge, or react to information arriving at different times. A familiar inverse relationship is a hypothesis, not a law.

Define the inputs before interpreting them. The Federal Reserve’s nominal Broad Dollar Index is a trade-weighted measure against currencies of major U.S. trading partners. It is not interchangeable with every proprietary dollar index. Treasury real par yields are derived from indicative market quotations for Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities. They are market measures with a published construction, not a pure reading of expected inflation or monetary policy.

For each pair, use synchronized changes: gold returns versus dollar-index returns, gold returns versus changes in a selected real yield, and asset returns versus changes in a chosen term spread. State the gold venue and fixing time, the yield maturity, the return interval, and the rolling window. Compare the estimated relationship across adjacent regimes instead of averaging all history into one number.

Treat the yield curve probabilistically. A spread between a longer and shorter Treasury maturity summarizes curve slope. The New York Fed publishes a recession-probability model based on the ten-year minus three-month spread and explicitly says its estimates are not official forecasts. A different maturity pair, sampling date, or model answers a different question.

**Macro relationship check**

- Name the exact dollar index, gold price, and Treasury series.
- Align observation times and compute changes, not level correlations.
- Publish rolling estimates with window and sample dates.
- Separate curve description from recession forecasting.
- Suspend any rule whose sign or stability has broken.

**Worked micro-example:** In a hypothetical window, gold rises while both the broad dollar index and the selected real yield also rise. Do not label gold “wrong” or invent a new causal story. First verify timestamps, return units, and whether one outlier controls the result. Then mark the usual inverse rule inactive. A gold trade must stand on its own entry, invalidation, liquidity, and exit policy; the macro dashboard can reduce confidence or size, but it cannot overrule the traded market.

Official series still have limitations. Treasury curves use indicative bids and interpolation, index weights can be revised, and markets close at different times. Correlation cannot establish that the dollar or real yields caused a gold move. Event risk, positioning, liquidity, and other omitted variables may coincide with the same observation.
