*Part 1 of 6 in The Central Bank Playbook.*

Before interpreting a central-bank statement, write down the institution's mandate, policy target, target horizon, and official measure. Do not import one bank's framework into another. A mandate defines the objectives policymakers must pursue; it does not turn one inflation or employment release into an automatic rate decision.

For the Federal Reserve, Congress assigned monetary-policy goals of maximum employment and stable prices. The Federal Reserve also describes moderate long-term interest rates in the statutory formulation. The FOMC judges inflation of 2 percent over the longer run, measured by the annual change in the personal consumption expenditures price index, as consistent with its mandate. It does not set a fixed numerical target for maximum employment because that level is not directly measurable and changes over time.

The ECB's primary objective is price stability in the euro area. Its strategy aims for 2 percent inflation over the medium term, measured with the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices, and treats positive and negative deviations as undesirable. The medium-term language matters: it allows for short-term deviations and recognizes uncertainty and lags in transmission.

Build every policy note with four distinct fields:

- **Target:** the official objective and measure, such as longer-run PCE inflation or medium-term HICP inflation.
- **Tool:** the rate, balance-sheet action, lending operation, or communication actually used.
- **Event:** the exact document, release timestamp, effective date, and time zone.
- **Interpretation:** your conditional reading, clearly separate from the official facts.

Consider a hypothetical month in which an inflation measure is above target while one labor indicator weakens. The facts do not imply that the Fed must raise or lower its target range. First confirm that the inflation series matches the Fed's stated measure and horizon. Then inspect the broader labor evidence, revisions, risks, and the current policy statement. A conclusion such as "the data increase the chance of easing" remains an interpretation, not a mandate requirement or a causal fact.

Use this mandate check:

- Name the institution and decision-making body.
- Quote or paraphrase the current official objective precisely.
- Record the target measure and horizon.
- Identify the announced tool separately.
- Timestamp the source document in its stated time zone.
- Label every market inference as an interpretation.

**Limitation:** official frameworks leave room for judgment under uncertainty. Different shocks can push inflation and employment in conflicting directions, while policy affects the economy with lags. A mandate map prevents category errors; it cannot predict the vote, the timing of action, or the market response.
