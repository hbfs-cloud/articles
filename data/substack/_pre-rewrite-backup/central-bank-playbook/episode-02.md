*Part 2 of 6 in The Central Bank Playbook.*

Read a rate decision in three passes: the policy target, the settings used to implement it, and the market's interpretation. Record the announcement time and the effective date for each setting. A target-range change is a policy decision; an administered rate helps implement it; a bond or currency move is an observed response, not part of the decision.

In the United States, the FOMC sets a target range for the federal funds rate. In an ample-reserves framework, the Federal Reserve implements that stance primarily with administered rates. The New York Fed describes interest on reserve balances, or IORB, as the main tool supporting control of the federal funds rate. Overnight reverse repo and standing repo operations also help limit pressure in overnight money markets. These rates and facilities should not be casually relabeled as the federal funds target itself.

The ECB publishes three key rates with different functions: the deposit facility rate for eligible overnight deposits, the main refinancing operations rate for weekly collateralized borrowing, and the marginal lending facility rate for overnight credit. A note that says only "the ECB rate" loses operational information.

Take a hypothetical FOMC release that lowers a target range from 5.00-5.25 percent to 4.75-5.00 percent. That is a reduction of 25 basis points, or 0.25 percentage point. The implementation note may set related administered and facility rates with a stated effective date. Suppose a two-year Treasury yield nevertheless rises during the initial market window. The documented facts are a lower target and a higher observed yield. The claim that guidance was less accommodative than expected is a possible interpretation and requires evidence about prior expectations; it is not contained in the target change.

Use this release procedure:

- Save the official statement and implementation note.
- Record publication time, time zone, and each effective date.
- Extract the prior and new target or policy rates.
- Identify which body sets each tool and who conducts operations.
- Measure market prices in a predeclared window using timestamped data.
- Keep explanation separate from the policy and price facts.

Do not infer the full stance from the nominal target alone. Balance-sheet policy, lending facilities, collateral terms, and forward guidance can matter at the same time.

**Limitation:** implementation frameworks change, and the same target adjustment can occur under different economic conditions. Market prices can move because of positioning, other news, liquidity, or changes elsewhere in the statement. A clean decomposition improves the record; it does not identify a unique cause for the response.
