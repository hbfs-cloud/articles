*Part 5 of 6 in Read Market Evidence Without Inventing a Signal.*

Build the earnings timeline before judging a beat, miss, upgrade or downgrade. Record the official release time, EDGAR acceptance, conference call, 10-Q or 10-K filing, and each analyst note's publication time. Compare reported results only with estimates frozen before the release.

Companies commonly furnish earnings information under Item 2.02 of Form 8-K. The form's instructions generally allow four business days after the public announcement, so an 8-K can follow the first release rather than create it. Item 2.02 information is normally “furnished,” not “filed,” unless the company specifies otherwise. The EDGAR acceptance timestamp and attached exhibit show what entered the public record and when.

The quarterly report has a different role. Form 10-Q covers the first three fiscal quarters and contains financial statements, notes, management discussion and other required disclosures. Form 10-K is annual and includes audited financial statements. An earnings release may arrive before either report and may emphasize selected operating or non-GAAP measures.

For every non-GAAP figure, locate the most directly comparable GAAP measure and reconciliation. SEC guidance requires comparable GAAP presentation and warns against giving the adjusted measure greater prominence. Two companies can use the same label while excluding different items, so compare definitions before comparing values.

Analyst research sits outside the issuer's filing sequence. A rating or price target is an opinion published by a person or firm with its own methodology and possible conflicts. Preserve the author, firm, old view, new view and timestamp. A consensus is also vendor-built; save its cutoff time and contributor rules.

Suppose a company releases results after the market closes and an analyst raises a target the next morning. A later data screen may display the higher target beside the reported earnings. Chronology shows that the target was a reaction, not a forecast that anticipated the release. The pre-release estimate belongs in the surprise calculation; the later note belongs in post-event evidence.

Earnings chronology:

- Freeze estimates and guidance before publication.
- Save the issuer release and EDGAR accession.
- Read GAAP results before adjusted reconciliations.
- Time-stamp the call, filing and each analyst revision.
- Separate pre-event forecasts from post-event reactions.

Chronology cannot reveal why the price moved or make management and analyst judgments objective. The SEC sets disclosure rules but does not vouch for a company's filing. Analyst conflicts do not automatically invalidate research, and an earnings surprise does not isolate the effect of guidance, positioning or valuation. Keep those explanations conditional.
