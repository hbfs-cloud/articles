*Part 6 of 6 in Read Market Evidence Without Inventing a Signal.*

Do not add filings, options volume, short data and social mentions into a bullish or bearish score. Admit each item only after recording its provenance, as-of time, publication time, coverage and unresolved fields. A conclusion must be no broader than the strongest direct observation in the ledger.

Each row needs a source URL or accession number, the original field name, unit, effective date, public timestamp, revision status and a plain-English claim. Add a separate column in your working notes for what the source does not establish. This prevents a provider's convenient label from silently replacing the official definition.

Consider five observations about one issuer. A Form 4 with transaction code P supports a specific reporting person's purchase on the stated date. A Form 13F supports a manager's reportable holdings at quarter end, not a current trade. Call volume supports options activity, while the complete strategy remains unknown. FINRA short-sale volume supports qualifying off-exchange short-sale transactions, not an open short position or ATS-only activity. Social mentions support attention inside the measured corpus, not factual agreement.

None of those rows becomes stronger because another row is present. They may justify a narrower research question: Did the issuer disclose a new event? Did open interest change after the option activity? Does the later short-interest snapshot show a position change? The next step is verification, not arithmetic.

Use three evidence states. “Admitted” means the primary source and field definition support the written claim. “Quarantined” means the observation is authentic but too delayed, incomplete or ambiguous for the proposed claim. “Rejected” means the source, timestamp or semantics cannot be verified. Preserve rejected rows so the same error is not reintroduced later.

Ledger maintenance:

- Pin the raw official page or filing accession.
- Store effective and publication times separately.
- Preserve units, coverage and denominator definitions.
- Write one supported claim and one explicit unknown per item.
- Review amendments, revisions and later position snapshots.
- Reject any conclusion that depends on inferred motive.

The ledger does not remove judgment. Public data are delayed, amended and incomplete; private positions and motives usually remain invisible. Absence from a reporting system may reflect scope or threshold rules rather than absence in the market. The method improves the audit trail and limits overclaiming, but it cannot guarantee a complete information set, a causal explanation or a profitable decision.
