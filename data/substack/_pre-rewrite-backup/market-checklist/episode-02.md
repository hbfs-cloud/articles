*Part 2 of 7 in The Market Checklist.*

Let a screen reject candidates, never authorize a trade. Its first job is to remove securities that your account cannot trade safely: insufficient liquidity, unsuitable price behavior, an event inside the holding window, or missing disclosure. Only then should it rank the survivors. A high rank means “inspect next,” not “buy.”

Build the process in three passes.

**Pass one: eligibility.** Define the tradable universe, permitted instrument types, minimum price, and minimum liquidity before seeing results. Tie liquidity to your intended order rather than a fashionable threshold. Compare order size with displayed depth, typical spread, and normal traded value. Exclude products whose prospectus, leverage, reset mechanics, or borrow requirements you do not understand.

**Pass two: ranking.** Rank only features connected to the setup: trend persistence for a continuation trade, compression for a breakout watch, or relative performance for a leadership list. Keep the variables visible. An opaque composite score makes it hard to know whether one noisy input controls the result.

**Pass three: verification.** Open the issuer’s latest 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings in EDGAR. Check for the next earnings date, recent financing documents, material corporate events, and changes to the share count or risk disclosures. Then inspect the spread and depth during the session in which you expect to trade.

**Worked micro-example:** A hypothetical small-cap ranks first because price is near a range high and recent volume is elevated. The eligibility pass accepts its quoted spread, but EDGAR shows a recent filing for a new securities offering. That filing does not prove the price will decline. It does change possible supply and event risk, so the candidate moves from “ready for an entry plan” to “manual review.” If the strategy has no rule for financing risk, reject it. The ranking has done its job by surfacing a candidate; the filing determines whether the candidate fits.

**Before ranking candidates**

- Freeze universe and liquidity rules before running the screen.
- Separate eligibility fields from ranking fields.
- Verify filings, event dates, spread, depth, and product structure.
- Save the screen timestamp and the reason for every manual override.

A screen can miss a qualitative change that has not reached its data feed, and delayed prices can make liquidity or momentum appear better than it is. It can also exclude an excellent but unusual setup. Manual overrides are acceptable when documented before entry; silent overrides destroy the audit trail. No screen removes the need to decide entry, invalidation, size, and exit separately.
