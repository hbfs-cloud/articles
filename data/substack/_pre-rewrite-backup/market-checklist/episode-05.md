*Part 5 of 7 in The Market Checklist.*

No position is ready until its normal exit, gap response, profit exit, time exit, and portfolio drawdown action are written. “I will manage it” is not a policy. Decide what observation ends the thesis, what order you intend to use, and what loss remains possible if that order cannot fill near its trigger.

Separate three layers of risk.

**Position risk:** Define the thesis invalidation, initial stop mechanism, maximum holding period, profit-taking rule, and treatment of any residual tranche. State whether an intraday breach or a closing price controls. Check the broker’s trigger convention because order names and policies can differ.

**Event risk:** List scheduled company and macro events inside the holding window. For each one, choose before entry: exit, reduce, hedge, or accept a specifically stressed overnight loss. Read recent filings for financing, litigation, delisting, or other material disclosures relevant to the thesis.

**Portfolio risk:** Set a response to aggregate drawdown and failed execution. That response can halt new entries, reduce gross exposure, or require a reconciliation of positions and orders. It should not force indiscriminate liquidation into an unavailable market.

**Worked micro-example:** A hypothetical swing position is due to cross an earnings announcement before its technical target is likely to be reached. The written plan says to exit before the announcement unless the position is reduced to the separately calculated gap-risk size. If held, a material disclosure that invalidates the revenue thesis calls for an exit at the next usable market, with no averaging down. If the thesis remains intact after the event, the original time exit still applies. A favorable gap does not erase that deadline; partial profit is taken under the existing rule and any residual follows its trailing exit.

A sell stop is not a guaranteed floor. Once triggered, it becomes a market order and may execute far from the stop price in a fast or thin market. A stop-limit order adds price control but may not execute at all. Position size must carry the risk that neither produces the hoped-for result.

**Loss-policy check**

- Write normal, gap, profit, residual, and time exits.
- Check issuer filings and scheduled events.
- Stress leverage, liquidity, and total portfolio loss.
- Confirm broker order behavior and alerts.
- Define the action after a drawdown or execution failure.

No loss policy can enumerate every shock. Options protection has premiums, expiration, liquidity, and contract risks; diversification can fail under common stress. When a scenario cannot be bounded well enough for the account, avoiding the exposure is a valid risk decision.
