# The AI Chain Passed Its First Test. Now Watch the Weak Links.

*Draft for Substack · 27 August 2026 · market data checked 10:37 UTC*

The point of last night was not only Nvidia.

The real test was the whole AI chain:

**chips → custom silicon → servers and networking → cybersecurity and software → power demand.**

That chain matters because the market is no longer asking one simple question, like “Is AI big?” Everyone knows AI is big. The harder question is: **where does the money actually show up?**

Last night gave us a cleaner answer than expected.

One warning before the tickers: a pre-market gap is not a setup.

At 06:49 ET, the regular market was still closed. Extended-hours 15-minute bars can be thin and sometimes show unusable volume. That means we can read the gap and the prior-day structure, but we cannot confirm a trade until the regular session gives volume, VWAP, and an opening range.

Here is the short earnings scorecard:

| Company | What mattered | First read |
|---|---|---|
| NVDA | EPS **$2.22** vs about **$2.09** expected; revenue **$96.2B**; next-quarter revenue guide **$108B**. | AI capex engine still running. |
| CRWD | Revenue **$1.47B**, up about **26%**; ARR **$5.84B**; net new ARR **$333M**. | Cybersecurity is the cleanest AI derivative. |
| OKTA | Revenue **$805M**, adjusted EPS **$1.05**; cRPO **$2.585B**; Q3 revenue guide **$813M-$817M**. | Identity/security is getting pulled into the AI-agent story. |
| CRM | Revenue **$11.35B**, up **11%**; cRPO **$33.5B**; AI data/agent products near **$3.9B** ARR. | SaaS is not dead, but the bar is now proof. |

## 1. Nvidia was the trigger

Nvidia cleared the first hurdle.

Revenue was **$96.2 billion**, above the roughly **$92 billion** Wall Street expected. Data center revenue was about **$89 billion**. Next-quarter guidance was **$108 billion**.

That is why Nvidia is not just another earnings report. It is the starting pistol for the whole AI trade.

If Nvidia had missed or guided badly, the damage would not have stopped at Nvidia. The market would have immediately looked at **AVGO, AMD, MRVL, SMCI, ANET, QQQ**, then the power names like **CEG, VST, and GEV**.

That did not happen. Nvidia passed.

But the reaction still matters: a good number does not mean “buy anything.” Expectations were already high, and margin/tariff/supply questions are still alive.

**Read:** Nvidia confirms the AI capex cycle is not broken. It does not remove the need for discipline.

## 2. CRWD and CRM tested the diffusion

The second question was more subtle:

**Is AI spending only going into chips, or is it spreading into enterprise software and security?**

CrowdStrike gave the cleaner answer. Revenue rose about **26%** to **$1.47 billion**. Annual recurring revenue reached **$5.84 billion**. Net new ARR was **$333 million**, up more than 50% from last year.

That says companies are not only buying AI engines. They are also buying locks for the new factory.

Salesforce answered a different question. The fear was that AI agents might hurt old software seats. Salesforce pushed back with **$11.35 billion** of revenue, **11%** growth, and current remaining performance obligations up **14%** to **$33.5 billion**.

That does not prove every software company is safe. It proves the best software platforms can still argue that AI lives inside their workflows instead of replacing them.

Okta matters here too. Its strong reaction was a second signal that the market was willing to buy identity and security, not just GPUs.

**Read:** the AI trade is diffusing beyond Nvidia, but quality matters. CRWD is cleaner than broad SaaS. CRM helps the group, but it does not rescue weak software by itself.

## 3. The hardware propagation is the next tell

The next layer is **AVGO / AMD / MRVL / SMCI / ANET**.

This is where the market decides whether AI demand is broad or still too concentrated around Nvidia.

AVGO matters because it speaks to custom AI silicon. AMD matters because it is the clearest GPU alternative. MRVL, SMCI, and ANET matter because AI needs networking, servers, and data movement.

The early pre-market map showed real propagation:

That is a real blast radius. It is not just one megacap lifting futures.

But a blast radius is not an entry signal by itself. It tells you where to look, not where to chase.

**Read:** KLAC-style semi equipment read-through is cleaner than chasing the most extended sympathy gaps. Packaging, optical, servers, and networking can work, but entries matter more after a gap.

## 4. The setup quality is uneven

This is the important part.

The earnings reaction created a lot of green screens, but not all green screens are equal.

**The cleaner post-earnings reactions:**

- **CRWD:** best direct post-earnings setup. Strong gap, near the high of the pre-market range. Still needs regular-hours confirmation.
- **NVDA:** the gap is holding, but the prior regular session was weak. It needs to hold the gap and reclaim the upper pre-market range.
- **CRM / OKTA:** both worked, but both were already extended. That means no chase without a base.

**The cleaner follow-through watchlist after the open:**

- **MU:** one of the better semi sympathy structures because it reclaimed key moving-average territory.
- **ZS / SNOW / PANW / DELL / MDB:** cleaner software/cyber continuation candidates if they hold VWAP and opening range.

**The sympathy rebounds still under resistance:**

- **AMKR, KLAC, AAOI, VICR, AMD, INTC, AVGO, APLD, CIFR, WULF, CORZ, DDOG.**

These can become trades, but they are not validated just because they are green before the open.

**The event-veto bucket:**

- **CAN, IREN, MRVL, S.**

They have their own earnings risk today. Treating them as simple Nvidia read-throughs before their own numbers is sloppy.

**The “needs more data” bucket:**

- **LPLA, ALLR, LUNR, RZLV, MRSH, STRL, EQX.**

Some have interesting levels, but the regular session must prove the setup. No volume, no VWAP, no opening range, no confirmation.

The sector message is also mixed. **QQQ and XLK** have reclaimed key moving averages, but **SMH and SOXX** are still below their 50-day moving averages. That is constructive. It is not a full sector reversal yet.

The big names tell you if the story is alive. The smaller links tell you where the market is actually searching for leverage.

Here is the cleaner watchlist by role:

That list is not a buy list. It is a map.

The rule is simple: the further you move away from Nvidia, the more you must demand a better entry and a cleaner balance sheet. A supplier with real AI exposure and a broken chart can be interesting later. A supplier with dilution risk and a vertical pre-market gap can still be the wrong trade today.

## 6. Power is the second-order confirmation

The second-order AI trade is not software. It is electricity.

If Nvidia says the AI buildout is still alive, then the next question is simple: who powers the data centers?

That is why **CEG, VST, and GEV** matter. They are not AI companies in the usual sense. They are witnesses. If AI capex is real, demand for power, grid equipment, turbines, and energy infrastructure should keep showing up.

This is the part of the chain many people miss. AI starts with GPUs, but it ends with power bills.

**Read:** if chips rally but power names do not confirm, the market is treating AI as a short-term earnings trade. If chips, networking, and power all confirm together, the market is pricing a real infrastructure cycle.

## 7. Gold, crypto, and the high-beta tails

This morning’s market snapshot is not panic.

Gold is around **$4,655/oz**, basically flat. Silver is firmer. Bitcoin is near **$79,900**, up about **1.5%** on the snapshot. Ethereum is stronger, up about **3.2%**.

That matters because the AI trade is not the only risk-on chain in the market.

There is a second chain:

**scarce assets → high-beta miners → treasury/proxy equities → AI/HPC pivots.**

With Treasury yields around the mid-4% area, the market has less room for disappointment. Higher yields make expensive long-duration growth stocks harder to own. They also keep gold and crypto sensitive to every move in the dollar and real rates.

Gold and bitcoin are sending the same broad message in different languages:

- Gold is the old scarce asset.
- Bitcoin is the younger scarce asset.
- Silver and Ethereum are the higher-beta versions.

But the tradable beta sits one layer out.

For precious metals, the cleaner high-beta watch is **CDE / AG / EQX**. CDE was strong enough to make the scanner, and AG was rejected locally because its correlation with CDE was too high. That is the right lesson: two silver miners are often one factor twice, not two separate trades.

For crypto, the clean beta chain is:

| Role | Names | What they test |
|---|---|---|
| Bitcoin proxy | MSTR | Levered sentiment on BTC and balance-sheet premium. |
| Exchange / activity beta | COIN | Whether crypto volume and retail risk appetite are improving. |
| Pure miners | MARA / CLSK / CAN | High beta to BTC, power costs, dilution and hash economics. |
| Miner-to-HPC pivots | HUT / WULF / CIFR / CORZ / IREN | Whether the market pays for the shift from mining to AI/HPC data centers. |
| Ethereum treasury / proxy | SBET / BMNR | Higher-risk ETH beta; useful as sentiment gauges, not clean core exposure. |

The key is not “crypto up, buy every miner.”

It is more specific:

- If BTC and ETH rise but miners lag, the move is asset-led, not equity-led.
- If miners and HPC pivots outperform, risk appetite is spreading.
- If gold/silver miners lead while crypto fades, the market may prefer hard-asset defense over speculative beta.
- If both crypto beta and AI/HPC beta run together, the market is chasing power, data centers, and scarcity at the same time.

If yields keep rising and the dollar strengthens, gold and crypto can cool off. If yields stop rising while inflation worries stay alive, they can run again.

| CEG / VST / GEV | Does power confirm the AI infrastructure cycle? |
| Gold / silver | Do scarce metals hold while yields stay high? |
| CDE / AG / EQX | Do precious-metal miners confirm, or are they just stretched beta? |
| BTC / ETH | Do crypto majors hold the risk-on impulse? |
| MSTR / COIN / MARA / CLSK / CAN | Does crypto equity beta confirm spot crypto? |
| HUT / WULF / CIFR / CORZ / IREN | Does miner-to-HPC beta confirm AI infrastructure, crypto, or both? |

I would not chase the first candle after a big earnings gap.

The better test is the post-open tape: gap, VWAP, breadth, and whether buyers defend the move after the first wave of excitement.

There are also earnings vetoes. Names reporting today should not be treated like clean read-through trades before their own numbers. They carry their own binary risk.

So the next real checkpoint is not the pre-market quote. It is the first regular-session 15-minute structure with real volume:

- Does price hold above VWAP?
- Does it defend the opening range?
- Does the group confirm, or only one stock?
- Does the move clear resistance, or just run into it?
- Are we buying a setup, or only reacting to a headline?

The message from last night is not simply bullish.

It is more precise:

**Nvidia confirmed the AI capex engine. CRWD and CRM showed the spending is spreading. The next proof must come from custom silicon, hardware propagation, and power.**

That is a healthier AI trade. It is also a less forgiving one.
