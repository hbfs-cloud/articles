*Part 4 of 6 in Turn a Trading Journal Into a Feedback Loop.*

Give each review horizon one job: reconcile facts daily, diagnose repeated process behavior weekly, and consider strategy changes only on a slower schedule with enough relevant evidence. Do not change a rule because of today's profit or loss. That turns normal outcome variation into an unstable method.

The **daily closeout** is accounting. Match journal fills to broker records, enter fees, confirm open exposure, mark missing data, and grade adherence against the plan version active at entry. Keep hindsight in a separate note. A profitable rule break remains a rule break; a planned loss remains a compliant trade.

The **weekly review** is diagnosis. Group trades and eligible non-trades by setup version. Look for repeated late entries, size overrides, unrecorded event exposure, rejected orders, and differences between planned and actual execution. Choose one operational issue to investigate. Do not rank dozens of slices until something looks favorable.

The **monthly or strategy-cycle review** asks whether the method still deserves capital. Examine net outcomes, drawdown, costs, exposure, sample composition, dependence on unusually large winners, and behavior across predeclared conditions. FINRA notes that performance comparisons should use an appropriate benchmark and account for risk and costs. A short-term trading process may need more than one reference: a comparable passive alternative, cash or financing effects, and its own stated objective. No benchmark alone proves skill.

Consider a concrete review procedure. At the end of each week, freeze a copy of the journal, validate that every broker fill has a row, and produce an exceptions list. Suppose the list shows several entries submitted after their written expiry. The next action is not to alter the entry signal. It is to inspect timestamps, classify why the expiry was ignored, and test a procedural control such as an alert or order lockout. Measure the control over the next predeclared review window before keeping it.

Use this checklist:

- Reconcile trades, fees, and open positions.
- Preserve the plan version and source timestamps.
- Separate adherence findings from outcome findings.
- Disclose exclusions and missing observations.
- Compare only compatible periods and benchmarks.
- Approve at most one clearly specified change for the next test.

**Limitation:** calendar reviews do not create statistical independence or an adequate sample. Regime shifts, overlapping positions, changing capital, taxes, and multiple strategy variants can defeat simple comparisons. Waiting for a monthly meeting also cannot justify leaving an uncontrolled operational fault active; execution and risk defects may require an immediate pause while the strategy rules remain frozen.
