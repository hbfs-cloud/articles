*Part 6 of 6 in Build a Trading Plan You Can Execute.*

Use four states for every candidate: prepare, observe, execute, and record. Do not submit an order until the prepared trigger, current market, order type, size, and exit instructions agree. Afterward, preserve the actual decisions and fills instead of repairing the plan to match the outcome.

During **prepare**, write the setup, evidence timestamp, trigger, invalidation, expiry, intended order type, and size ceiling. During **observe**, update only fields the plan permits, such as the current spread or whether a trigger occurred. A moving price is not a reason to improvise a new setup.

During **execute**, translate the plan into the broker ticket. Investor.gov notes that a market order generally favors execution rather than a guaranteed price, while a limit order controls price but may not execute. Order routing and market movement can affect the fill. The order choice should follow the intended trade-off, not habit.

Take a hypothetical order. The prepared record permits a limit entry up to $18.40, thesis invalidation at $18.05, and no entry after 2:30 p.m. The broker fills 150 shares at $18.38. The trader immediately records the actual fill and recalculates planned price risk as $0.33 per share. If the intended protective instruction is rejected, the routine does not assume protection exists. It requires confirmation, correction if allowed by the plan, or an orderly exit under the exception rule.

During **record**, retain the order submission time, fills, rejected or canceled orders, fees, and the reason for every manual intervention. Investor.gov advises investors to understand transaction and account fees; even a commission-free label does not establish that execution has no cost. Performance review should use actual net outcomes, not ideal chart prices.

Run this compact check at the order ticket:

- Setup valid and source information still current?
- Trigger observed without anticipation?
- Order type, duration, and session correct?
- Size within trade, liquidity, and portfolio caps?
- Exit instruction accepted and visible?
- Actual fill and exception notes saved?

**Limitation:** a routine cannot prevent platform failure, rejected orders, delayed data, trading halts, or fills outside the expected range. Automation also introduces its own configuration and connectivity risks. When the observed state no longer matches the plan, abstaining or exiting can be valid; forcing completion of the original sequence is not discipline.

The final review question is narrow: did the trader follow the version of the plan that existed before the decision? Profit does not excuse a violation, and a compliant loss does not by itself invalidate the method. Strategy evaluation requires a larger, consistently measured record.
