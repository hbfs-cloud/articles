*Part 4 of 6 in The Central Bank Playbook.*

Process an FOMC event in document order and timestamp every update. The policy statement records the Committee's decision. The implementation note supplies operating settings. Projection materials contain participants' conditional assessments. The press conference adds explanation. Minutes arrive later and summarize the earlier meeting. Combining them into one simultaneous signal creates false chronology.

Build an event folder from the Federal Reserve's meeting calendar, not a third-party calendar. Before the event, record which materials the official page says are associated with that meeting and the stated time zone. At release, save the statement and implementation note as separate documents. If projection materials are published, preserve that version before reading commentary.

The Summary of Economic Projections is not a promised rate path. Federal Reserve materials describe projections as individual participants' assessments based on their own views of appropriate monetary policy and assumptions about the economy. A median dot is a summary of individual submissions, not a Committee vote, an unconditional forecast, or a commitment to act on a date.

Minutes have a different event time. The Federal Reserve says minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are released three weeks after the policy decision. They provide a summary of views and decisions from that prior meeting. Information released between the meeting and the minutes publication was not available to participants at the meeting, so the minutes should not be read as a current policy update.

Consider a hypothetical meeting with an unchanged target range and lower participant projections for a later year. A market yield falls when the documents appear, then reverses during the press conference. Record three observations: unchanged policy target, changed projection distribution, and a two-stage price path. Saying that the FOMC "promised cuts and the market rejected them" would overstate both the projections and the price evidence.

Use this event checklist:

- Confirm official document set, release time, and time zone.
- Diff the statement against its immediate predecessor.
- Read the implementation note for actual operating changes.
- Treat projections as conditional individual submissions.
- Timestamp press-conference observations separately.
- Read minutes against the meeting-date information set.

**Limitation:** textual differences can reflect drafting rather than a measurable change in reaction function. Press-conference answers are contextual, and market prices absorb positioning and unrelated information. Release order prevents look-ahead errors; it cannot assign a unique cause to every price move.
