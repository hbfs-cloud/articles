*Part 4 of 5 in Survive Gap Risk.*

Never enter solely because a gap “should fill.” The prior close is a reference price, not a target. Trade only after the current session supplies a valid setup, usable liquidity, and a complete exit policy. If the verified disclosure changes the business or payoff behind the old chart, discard the old technical premise and reassess from current information.

Begin with evidence. Check issuer filings and releases for company news, then confirm scheduled macro events through the responsible agency. Note whether the security is halted, in an opening auction, or trading normally. Observe spreads, depth, and completed transactions rather than relying on a single premarket print.

Choose one of two incompatible plans before entry:

**Continuation:** Price holds away from the prior range and forms a new, tradable structure. Entry requires a defined break or pullback, with invalidation inside the new session structure.

**Rejection:** Price cannot maintain the gap and re-enters a defined prior range. Entry requires observable rejection, not the size of the gap alone. The invalidation sits beyond the failed move, not at an arbitrary distance.

For either plan, set a maximum entry, order type, size, normal stop, profit rule, residual-tranche exit, time exit, and news cancellation. A fixed delay after the bell is not a substitute for these conditions. Some markets stabilize quickly; others remain untradeable much longer.

**Worked procedure:** Let `C` be the prior regular-session close and `O` the new opening price above it. The trader marks the opening structure only after executable spreads and depth meet the written limits. A continuation order can activate if price holds above that structure and then retests it without breaching the invalidation. A rejection order can activate only after price re-enters the prior range and fails to reclaim the gap structure. If neither occurs, no trade. `C` may inform a profit objective, but the plan never assumes price must reach it.

**After the gap**

- Verify the catalyst and market status.
- Require spread, depth, and order-size limits.
- Select continuation or rejection, never both after the fact.
- Write entry, invalidation, size, and every exit branch.
- Cancel when news, price, or time invalidates the setup.

Opening structure can fail without warning, and apparent liquidity can vanish after an order is sent. Limit orders may not fill; market and stop orders can fill at unexpected prices. Options opened after a gap can also carry elevated premiums and wide spreads. No chart pattern converts those execution risks into certainty.
