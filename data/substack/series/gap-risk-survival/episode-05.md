*Part 5 of 5 in Survive Gap Risk.*

Write the gap-crisis plan before the close. After an adverse open, follow evidence and liquidity, not a universal rule to sell immediately or wait a fixed number of minutes. A verified fraud, insolvency, delisting risk, or broken thesis can make delay costly. A disorderly auction and an unbounded market order can make instant execution costly. The plan must distinguish those cases in advance.

Run an overnight portfolio map for every position:

- next issuer, economic, regulatory, or contract event;
- stressed next executable price and resulting loss;
- stop behavior, hedge expiry, and open-order status;
- issuer, sector, currency, rate, commodity, and volatility exposures;
- margin requirement, available cash, and likely forced-liquidation risk;
- normal, gap, profit, residual, time, and thesis exits.

Aggregate the stress by shared driver. Several individually small positions can become one large exposure when a common event affects an index, suppliers, customers, or financing conditions. Correlation history can inform the review, but it cannot prove how those holdings will behave during the next shock.

**Worked micro-example:** A trader holds two companies in one industry plus an ETF that owns both. Each position passes its standalone loss budget. The portfolio map applies the same adverse industry event to all three and includes the ETF overlap. The combined stress breaches the portfolio limit and could reduce margin headroom. Before the event, the trader reduces the overlapping exposure and cancels stale stop orders that no longer match the revised size. The usable behavior is aggregation before the close, not guessing which ticker will gap most.

If a gap occurs, use a short response sequence. First, confirm account access, positions, open orders, and margin. Second, verify the catalyst through the issuer or regulator and note any halt or auction status. Third, cancel orders based on obsolete prices. Fourth, rank actions by threat to solvency and thesis, then use order types appropriate to available liquidity. Finally, document the evidence, fills, and any plan deviation before taking a new trade.

**Portfolio gap drill**

- Map events and gap stress before each overnight hold.
- Combine ETF overlap, leverage, and shared drivers.
- Preserve cash and margin headroom for stressed conditions.
- Prewrite evidence-based exit priorities and order choices.
- Close the review with one specific process correction.

The map cannot foresee unscheduled news, exchange outages, broker restrictions, or correlations that change abruptly. Hedges can fail to match and brokers may raise house margin requirements. Survival therefore depends on exposure that remains tolerable when the plan receives a worse price and slower execution than expected.
