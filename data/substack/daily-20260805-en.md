# The rally was about oil, not easing

*4 August closes. US markets open at 13:30 UTC today.*

Two days ago we published odds of 56.5% that the Federal Reserve raises rates in September. Those odds are now **45.5%**. Over the same two sessions, oil fell hard.

That is the session, and it is not what most write-ups will say.

## What actually moved

On Tuesday the S&P 500 rose 1.80% to 7,736.52. The Dow added 1.71%, the Nasdaq 2.59%, and the Russell 2000 of smaller companies 1.85%. Long-dated Treasuries rose too, with TLT up 0.77% and the 10-year yield down to 4.627%.

Then the one that explains the rest: **crude oil fell 5.19% in a single session** (USO). Gold rose only 0.66%, silver 2.63%.

A 5% drop in oil is a direct cut to next quarter's inflation. Take the inflation impulse away and the case for another rate rise weakens. Which is exactly what the betting market did.

## The numbers on the Fed

These are live prices from Polymarket, where people are paid to be right:

- Rate **rise** of 25bp in September: **45.5%** (was 56.5% on 3 August)
- **No change**: **51.5%**
- Rate **cut** of 25bp: **1.55%**
- No cuts at all during 2026: **88.65%**

Read the third line twice. A rate cut in September is priced at one and a half percent. Nobody is betting on easier money. What changed is that the *hike* stopped looking inevitable, and "no change" took the lead for the first time.

That distinction matters for what you own. A market relieved about a hike that may not come behaves differently from a market anticipating cuts. The first rewards whatever was being punished by high rates. The second rewards long-duration assets across the board.

## A correction to something we implied

An earlier draft of this note argued that owning gold, long bonds and small caps together is not real diversification because they move on the same idea.

We measured it. Over the last 60 sessions, gold and long bonds have a correlation of **0.21** — one of the loosest pairs on the board. Owning both is genuinely diversifying. The tight pairs are elsewhere: gold and silver at **0.85**, and small caps with the S&P at **0.78**.

So if you hold gold and silver thinking they are two positions, they are close to one. Same for a small-cap fund alongside an S&P fund.

## What settles it

**Today, 10:00 New York: the ISM services survey.** A warm-up.

**Friday, 8:30 New York: the jobs report.**

Here is the test, and it is a single number. If payrolls come in hot, the September hike contract should climb back toward the 56.5% we saw on Monday, and Tuesday's rally goes with it. If payrolls are soft, "no change" extends its lead and the move holds.

Watch that contract, not gold. Gold rose 0.66% on Tuesday, which is noise dressed as a signal. The hike probability moved eleven points, which is not.
