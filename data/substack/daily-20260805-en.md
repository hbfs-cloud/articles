# Monday's call, marked to market

*Prices: 4 August closes and live 5 August. Polymarket read at 13:59 UTC, 5 August.*

On Monday we published two things about the Federal Reserve, and a line at which we agreed to be wrong. Here they are, and here is where each one stands.

## The level call: right so far

We wrote that either crude turned back up, or the 56.5% odds of a September rate rise were too high.

Crude did not turn. USO, the crude ETF, fell 5.46% on Monday and another 5.19% on Tuesday.

The September hike now prices at **46.5%**, against 51.5% for no change. Ten points down in two sessions, and "no change" has taken the lead.

Full ladder, read at 13:59 UTC today: raise 25bp 46.5%, no change 51.5%, raise 50bp or more 1.45%, cut 25bp 1.55%. And no cuts at all during 2026 at 88.65%. Nobody is betting on easier money. What changed is that the hike stopped looking settled.

## The mechanism call: on probation, 1.5 points from failing

Monday also argued the hawkish votes were being held by the labour market rather than by energy. And it set a number:

> If the probability of a September hike falls below 45% by Friday while oil stays low, then the energy channel was working and we were wrong to dismiss it.

It is Wednesday, oil is low, and the print is 46.5%. That is 1.5 points from our own failure line.

There is a second piece of evidence, and it does not help us either. Monday named the ISM manufacturing survey as the first arbiter, with a bearish case built on a reading below 48. It came in at **55.6** — above the 54.0 expected and the highest since May 2022, with new orders at 56.7.

Work that through. A strong manufacturing print argues for a hike, not against one. If the hawks were driven by the real economy, those odds should have gone up on Monday afternoon. They went down ten points instead.

So the evidence is running against our own mechanism and toward the channel we dismissed. We are not calling it yet — 46.5% is above 45% — but if Friday's payrolls come in hot and the contract keeps falling, that is our answer, and we will print it.
