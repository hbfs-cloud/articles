Yesterday everything went up at once. That is the part worth stopping on.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,736.52, up 1.79%. The Dow added 1.71% to 54,085.88. The Nasdaq did 2.59%, to 26,584.99. Small caps came too: the Russell 2000 rose 1.85% to 3,036.98. Four indices, four green numbers, no laggard. Overnight the Nikkei went further and added 3.55%, to 66,226.

Normally that is where a market note stops. But look at what else moved.

Gold rose 1.77% to $4,226 an ounce. Silver rose 2.48% to $61.74. Long bonds rallied too, with TLT up 0.77%, and yields fell across the curve: the 5-year to 4.333%, the 10-year to 4.627%, the 30-year to 5.190%. Investment grade credit gained 0.61%, high yield 0.30%.

Stocks, gold and bonds do not usually rise together because they want different things. Stocks want growth. Gold and bonds want the opposite. When all three go up on the same day, they are not agreeing about the economy. They are agreeing about money: someone thinks it is about to get cheaper.

## The one number that did not join

The 13-week Treasury bill yield went the other way. It rose, to 3.730%.

That is the shortest, most literal instrument there is. It tracks what cash costs right now, not what someone hopes it will cost. Every other maturity fell and that one rose.

So the market bought a rate cut yesterday, but placed it somewhere out in the future rather than at the next meeting. That is a softer bet than the headline suggests, and it is a bet that has to be settled by data rather than by opinion.

Oil disagreed as well. WTI fell 1.06% to $74.97, Brent 0.69% to $78.81. A genuine growth rally usually takes crude with it. This one did not.

## What settles it

Two prints, and the second is the one that counts.

**Today, 10:00 New York time: ISM Services.** The services survey covers most of the American economy. Treat it as a warm-up.

**Friday, 8:30 New York time: the jobs report.** This is the argument, on paper.

Here is the useful way to watch it. If yesterday really was a bet on cheaper money, then a hot jobs number should hurt gold, bonds and small caps together, because all three are riding the same idea. If instead the rally was about growth, a hot number should only hurt bonds while stocks hold. Same headline, two very different reactions. Which one happens tells you what you actually own.

That is a testable claim, so you can hold me to it on Friday.

## Positioning

Nothing about yesterday requires you to act this morning. A one-day move of that size, two sessions before payrolls, is not an entry signal. It is a reason to check what you already hold.

If you own gold, long bonds and small caps at the same time, you may believe you are diversified. Yesterday suggests you are not. All three moved on the same idea and they will unwind on the same idea.

The dollar barely moved, at 99.855 on the index, and that is consistent with the rest: a currency that is not yet convinced.
