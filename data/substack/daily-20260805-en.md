# We said those odds were too high. They came down 11 points.

*4 August closes. US markets open 13:30 UTC.*

On Monday we published a call, in one sentence: *either the barrel turns back up, or that 56.5% is too high.*

The barrel did not turn. Crude fell another 5.19% on Tuesday, taking the two-session drop past 10%.

And the 56.5% is now **45.5%**.

## The scoreboard

These are live prices on Polymarket, where a wrong opinion costs money.

| September Fed meeting | 3 August | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Raise 25bp | 56.5% | **45.5%** |
| No change | 41.5% | **51.5%** |
| Cut 25bp | — | **1.55%** |

"No change" has taken the lead for the first time. And note the bottom row before anyone gets carried away: a cut is priced at one and a half percent. Nobody is betting on easier money. The bet that paid was simply that a hike had stopped being the base case.

We are marking our own call to market here because it is the only way a reader can tell whether these notes are worth reading. Monday's was right. It will not always be.

## What we still cannot explain

Honesty about the mechanism, since we published a view on that too.

Monday's note argued the hawkish votes were being held up by the labour market rather than by energy, on the grounds that a first 6% drop in crude had moved the contract by nothing at all. Two days later a second leg of similar size moved it eleven points.

Both cannot be fully right. Either the market needed the second leg to believe the first, or something else did the work and oil is a coincidence. No employment data was released between the two sessions, so the labour explanation has nothing to stand on this week. We do not know which it is, and we would rather say so than reverse-engineer a reason.

## What moved on Tuesday

The S&P 500 closed up 1.80%, the Russell 2000 of smaller companies 1.85%. Long bonds rose too, TLT up 0.77%. Gold added a modest 0.66%, silver 2.63%.

Everything up, oil down 5%. That shape fits a market relieved about rates rather than excited about growth — a genuine growth rally usually takes crude with it.

## What settles it

**Today, 10:00 New York: ISM services.**

**Friday, 8:30 New York: the jobs report.** This is the one that tests Monday's mechanism directly. If payrolls run hot and the September hike contract barely moves, the labour explanation we published was wrong, and we will say so here.

**Wednesday next week: consumer prices.** That tests the oil channel.

Two prints, two competing explanations, both ours. That is a more useful week than it sounds.
