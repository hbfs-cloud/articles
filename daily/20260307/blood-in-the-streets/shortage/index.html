Trade Levels Summary

Understanding the Shortage Investment Landscape

The energy transition is a multi-decade, multi-trillion-dollar structural shift. Understanding the key sub-sectors and their risk profiles is essential for navigating this space.

1. Nuclear: The Baseload Solution

Nuclear provides 24/7 carbon-free baseload power — something wind and solar cannot. AI data centers need consistent power (99.999% uptime), making nuclear the only scalable solution. Traditional reactors take 10+ years to build; SMRs promise 3-5 year timelines. The risk is pre-revenue companies burning cash before commercialization.

2. Uranium: The Fuel Play

Every reactor needs uranium fuel. Supply is constrained (Kazatomprom cuts, Russia sanctions, Niger instability) while demand accelerates (60+ reactors under construction). LEU and UEC give direct exposure to the uranium cycle without reactor technology risk. HALEU is the premium fuel for advanced reactors — LEU has a monopoly.

3. Solar: Rate Sensitivity

Residential solar economics depend heavily on financing costs. High rates = expensive leases/PPAs = lower demand. Rate cuts are the primary catalyst. ENPH (microinverters) is the quality play; RUN (installer) is the leveraged rate-cut play. Both benefit from IRA subsidies ($7,500 residential credit).

4. Hydrogen: The Long Game

Green hydrogen is the most speculative sub-sector. IRA provides $3/kg production tax credits, but adoption timelines keep slipping. PLUG is building the ecosystem; BLDP focuses on the strongest use case (heavy transport). Cash positions matter here — survival to profitability is the key question.

5. Risk Management Principles

This watchlist spans LOW to EXTREME risk. Total allocation should not exceed 10-15% of a diversified portfolio. Pre-revenue names (SMR, OKLO, NNE, PLUG) should be 0.5-1% each. Profitable names (ENPH, LEU, UEC) can be 2-3% each. Use stop losses religiously. Scale in over 2-3 tranches rather than all at once.
