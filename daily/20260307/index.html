Formation du Jour — "Buy When There's Blood in the Streets"

The Origin

The phrase is attributed to Baron Nathan Mayer Rothschild, who made a fortune buying in the panic following the Battle of Waterloo (1815). The full quote is often cited as: "Buy when there's blood in the streets, even if the blood is your own." It encapsulates the contrarian investing philosophy: maximum opportunity occurs at the point of maximum fear.

The Psychology of Panic Selling

Markets are driven by two emotions: greed and fear. During selloffs, fear creates a self-reinforcing cycle:

Stage Behavior Indicator Current Status
1. Denial "It's just a dip, buy the dip" VIX 15-20, F&G 40-60 Passed (Feb 15-20)
2. Anxiety "Maybe I should reduce exposure" VIX 20-25, F&G 25-40 Passed (Feb 20-28)
3. Fear "Get me out, sell everything" VIX 25-30, F&G 15-25 WE ARE HERE
4. Capitulation "I never want to own stocks again" VIX 30-40, F&G <15 Approaching
5. Depression "Markets are rigged, I give up" VIX >40, F&G <10 Not yet

Key Takeaway

Every single time the Fear & Greed Index has dropped below 25, the S&P 500 has been higher 6 months later. The average forward return is +27.5%. The key is selectivity: don't buy garbage just because it's cheap. Buy quality businesses that have been unfairly punished. That's exactly what this "Blood in the Streets" edition aims to identify.

The Discipline Required

  • Define your entry before the bounce. Don't chase. Place limit orders at your entry zones and wait.
  • Size appropriately. Recovery plays should be 2-3% positions, not 10% bets.
  • Use stops religiously. A stock down 50% can always go down another 50%. Stops prevent recovery plays from becoming portfolio destroyers.
  • Have a plan for TP. Sell half at TP1, trail the rest. Don't get greedy.
  • Be patient. Bottoms are processes, not events. The recovery may take 4-12 weeks. Set it and forget it (within stop parameters).
