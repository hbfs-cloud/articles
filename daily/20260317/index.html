Today's Lesson

How to Read the Fed's Dot Plot

The Dot Plot Decoded — Your Cheat Sheet for Wednesday

With the FOMC meeting today and the decision tomorrow, the dot plot will be the single most market-moving element. Here's everything you need to know to interpret it like a pro.

What is it? The dot plot is a chart showing where each of the 19 FOMC members expects the federal funds rate to be at the end of each year, plus the "longer run." Each dot represents one anonymous official's forecast. The median dot (the middle one) is what the market cares about most.

Why does it matter this time? The last dot plot (December 2025) showed two rate cuts expected in 2026. But the Iran oil shock has changed the inflation picture dramatically. If the median dot shifts to show one or zero cuts, that's hawkish — expect stocks to sell off and bonds to drop. If it still shows two cuts despite oil, that's dovish — risk assets rally.

What to watch:
1. 2026 median dot — The single most important number. Currently 3.25-3.50% (implying two cuts). If it moves to 3.50-3.75% (zero cuts) = bearish.
2. Dispersion — If dots are clustered = consensus. If scattered = uncertainty. Wide dispersion is bearish for volatility sellers.
3. Long-run dot — If the "neutral rate" estimate rises above 3.0%, the entire rate structure reprices higher permanently.
4. GDP and inflation revisions — The SEP also includes GDP growth and PCE inflation forecasts. Watch if they raise inflation + cut growth (stagflation signal).

Key Takeaway: Don't just look at the rate decision (almost certainly a hold). The dot plot and the SEP revisions are where the volatility will come from. Powell's press conference at 2:30 PM ET will add color. If he sounds worried about oil-driven inflation, expect a hawkish market reaction. If he emphasizes that oil is a supply shock (transitory), markets will rally.
