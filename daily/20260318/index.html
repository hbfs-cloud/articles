Formation: Deciphering the FOMC Dot Plot

What is the Dot Plot?

Every quarter, the Federal Reserve releases the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP), which includes the "Dot Plot." Each dot represents one Fed official's anonymous projection for where interest rates should be at the end of each year.

Why it matters today:

  • The Median Dot: The "average" of these dots is what markets use to price future rate cuts.
  • The "Hawkish Shift": If more dots move higher for 2026, it means the Fed expects inflation to be "stickier" for longer.
  • Market Reaction: A higher median dot = Bond yields up, Stocks down. A lower median dot = Risk-on rally.

Watch for the 2026 median. If it stays at 3.25%, the market will cheer. If it moves to 3.50% or 3.75%, prepare for a correction.
