Today’s Lesson: How to Trade FOMC Day

The FOMC Day Playbook

Today the Fed releases its rate decision, dot plot, and Powell speaks. FOMC days are some of the most volatile and tricky days of the year. Here’s a practical playbook.

1. The “Dead Zone” — 9:30 AM to 1:45 PM

Volume dries up as institutions wait. The morning session often drifts in a tight range with fake breakouts in both directions. Don’t chase. Use this time to set alerts, not trades.

2. The 2:00 PM Algo Spike

The moment the statement drops, algorithms parse the text in milliseconds and fire orders. You’ll see a violent whipsaw—often a spike one direction followed by a complete reversal within 5 minutes. This is not tradeable for humans. Stay flat.

3. Powell’s Presser — 2:30 PM

This is where the real move often begins. Listen for tone, not just words. Key phrases: “data dependent” (neutral), “further progress needed on inflation” (hawkish), “prepared to adjust policy” (dovish). The Q&A is often more market-moving than the opening statement.

4. The Day-After Rule

Historically, the directional move happens the day AFTER FOMC, not the day itself. The initial reaction is often reversed overnight. If you must trade, consider entering Thursday morning in the confirmed direction.

Practical Tips

• Reduce position sizes by 50% into FOMC
• Widen stops by 1.5x normal distance
• Avoid new entries between 1:30–3:00 PM ET
• Watch TLT & DXY—they often lead the equity reaction by minutes
• The dot plot median is more important than the rate decision itself
