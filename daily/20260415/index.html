What Happened — From Blockade Fear to Risk-On Reboot

Monday was the session where every bull thesis of the last 72 hours got validated at once. The market opened with an overhang of weekend blockade headlines, Sunday’s oil spike to $104 still fresh, and VIX futures printed at 22 pre-market. Then three things happened within two hours of the bell:

  1. Goldman Sachs blew out Q1. EPS $17.55 vs $16.32 est (+7.5% beat). Revenue $17.23B (+12% YoY). ROE 19.8%. Investment banking fees +22% YoY on the M&A revival and equity capital market issuance. This isn’t a “guided-down-then-beat” manoeuvre — it’s a genuine earnings surprise with clean components.
  2. Iran back-channel signals went public. Trump’s “called by the other side” line from Sunday evening was reinforced by Treasury officials confirming Qatar is hosting quiet talks. Oil shed the blockade premium: WTI from $104 to $91.30, Brent from $103 to $95.31 — both still elevated but no longer pricing a kinetic conflict.
  3. Breadth went vertical. Industrials led (+4% sector aggregate), Communication Services +3%, Consumer Discretionary +3%, Tech +2%, Financials +2%. Only Energy lagged (−2%) as the blockade premium unwound. Top movers tell the story: BE +23.98%, IONQ +20.16%, CRDO +18.73%, JBLU +16.91%. Small-cap momentum is back, risk appetite is broad, and the Russell’s +1.32% confirms it.
Why Tuesday matters more than Monday: Monday was a technical recovery driven by one earnings beat and a diplomatic signal. Tuesday is the first day of the real data cycle: JPMorgan reports at 6:45 ET, Citigroup at 8:00 ET, Bank of America at 6:45 ET, plus Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth, State Street, Bank of NY Mellon. If the banks print clean Q1 numbers like GS did, the risk-on reboot gets its fundamental stamp — and the S&P will be positioned to test the all-time high band around 7,000.

Formation du Jour — Reading Bank Earnings in One Page

What to watch in JPM, BAC, C this morning

Bank earnings reports are famously noisy — dozens of line items, reserve builds, capital ratios, legal provisions. But you only need four numbers to read them fast. Write these down on a post-it for today:

  1. Net Interest Income (NII) vs consensus. This is the engine of the bank. A beat here with guidance held or raised = bullish. JPM consensus: $23.4B (expected flat YoY because rate-cut expectations drag asset yields but also funding costs). BAC consensus: $14.3B. A ±2% surprise drives the full-day move.
  2. Investment Banking fees YoY. Goldman already set the bar at +22% YoY. If JPM prints +15% or better on M&A advisory + equity issuance, the entire sector rerates. If the number is flat or negative, GS looks like a one-off.
  3. Trading revenue (FICC + Equities). Q1 was volatile (tariff-selloff, Iran). Expect banks to report strong trading numbers — volatility = commissions. The question is whether they held the gains into quarter-end. JPM FICC consensus: $5.1B.
  4. Net charge-offs & loan-loss provisions. This is the credit-cycle read. If provisions are down sequentially, it confirms the soft-landing narrative. If they spike, it signals the banks see stress in credit cards / CRE / leveraged loans. JPM expected provisions: $3.0B (flat QoQ).

Decision rule: Two out of four positive = buy the bank. Three out of four = buy the sector (XLF). Four out of four = expect a leg up in the S&P (financials are 13% of the index). Negative on NII and provisions = skip the trade, the narrative turns.

Timing: JPM prints at 6:45 ET. BAC at 6:45 ET. Citi at 8:00 ET. Trade the reaction after the conference call starts (9:00 ET for JPM) — the initial print is always a knee-jerk.
