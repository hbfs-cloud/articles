🎓 Formation du Jour — Beginner

How Oil Shocks Trigger Recessions: A 50-Year History

With Brent at $100 and the Strait of Hormuz closed, today's lesson couldn't be more timely. Understanding the oil-recession transmission mechanism helps you anticipate where markets go from here.

💪 Why Does Oil Cause Recessions?

Oil is the blood of the global economy. Nearly every industry uses it: transportation, manufacturing, agriculture, chemicals, plastics. When oil surges, it acts like a tax on every business and consumer simultaneously. Companies face higher input costs → raise prices → consumers buy less → growth slows → layoffs → recession.

📈 The Historical Playbook (4 Major Oil Shocks)

1973 — OPEC Embargo: Oil +400%. US recession followed within 6 months. Stagflation (high inflation + recession) coined as economic term. S&P 500 fell ~48%.
1979-80 — Iran Revolution + Iraq War: Oil +160%. Fed raised rates to 20% to fight inflation. Double-dip recession 1980-1982. S&P fell ~27%.
1990 — Gulf War (Iraq invades Kuwait): Oil +120% in 3 months. US recession (mild). S&P correction ~20%.
2022 — Russia-Ukraine: Oil +80% (from $70 to $130). US avoided recession but inflation hit 9.1%. Fed raised rates 525bps.

🔎 What Makes 2026 Different?

The current shock is structural, not speculative. The Strait of Hormuz physically cannot transit oil while closed. Previous shocks saw some supply rerouting possible. Additionally, the US economy enters this with: (1) already-elevated inflation (2.4% CPI), (2) a Fed that cannot easily cut (stagflation trap), and (3) simultaneous tariff headwinds (another inflationary impulse). The dual shock — oil AND tariffs — is the key risk the market is pricing in.

💡 Practical Takeaway for Investors

  • Winners in oil shocks: Energy (XOM, CVX, EQNR), Gold miners (NEM, GDX), Agriculture commodities (DBA, CF)
  • Losers in oil shocks: Airlines, consumer discretionary, high-debt growth stocks, EV companies
  • Hedges: SQQQ (inverse Nasdaq), TLT (if recession materializes), GLD
  • Watch the Fed: If they cut despite inflation → stagflation accelerates. If they hold → recession risk grows.
