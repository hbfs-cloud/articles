Geopolitics — Iran Escalation Reshapes the Risk Map

US-Iran — Fresh Strikes, Diplomacy Dead?

The US launched fresh military strikes on Iran on Wednesday, retaliating against Tehran’s provocations in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump signaled he may abandon negotiations entirely. This represents a material escalation from the ceasefire framework that had been holding since late June. Markets are pricing this as a sector event (energy up, everything else pressured) rather than a systemic event (VIX still at 16.90, credit spreads tight). The key risk: if strikes intensify to the point of sustained Hormuz disruption, oil reprices toward $90+ and the calculus changes. For now, the market’s read is “contained escalation.”

Hormuz Supply Risk — Oil Premium Returns

WTI’s jump to $74.30 and Brent to $78.86 represents the re-insertion of a geopolitical risk premium into crude. During the June ceasefire, this premium had been fully unwound (WTI hit $67.87 on July 2). The refining complex is the direct beneficiary: Marathon Petroleum (MPC) and Valero (VLO) both hit 52-week highs. The danger is pass-through: if crude stays above $75 for two weeks, it feeds into CPI via gasoline and transportation costs. The July 13 CPI print becomes even more critical.

CPI Sunday/Monday — The Week’s Real Event

The Iran oil spike adds a new dimension to the CPI report due July 13. The oil pass-through into gasoline prices takes 2–3 weeks, so this spike won’t directly affect the July 13 print. But market expectations will front-run it — any CPI surprise to the upside, combined with rising oil, gives the Fed hawks ammunition. A CPI above 3.5% would pressure rate-sensitive names (REITs, homebuilders, long-duration tech) and could flip the regime from risk-on to neutral.

Breadth vs. Regime — The Contradiction

The regime model says risk-on 65.4%. The breadth says 29.4% positive. Which one is right? Probably both. The regime model looks at macro factors: credit spreads (tight), VIX (16.90, below average), SPX relative to its moving averages (above all three), DXY (weak, supportive). All green. But the breadth number captures what’s happening inside the market: a few sectors pulling up the average while most stocks decline. This is the classic late-cycle rotation signature — the index holds up because the winners are big enough to mask the losers. The danger point is when rotation exhausts all its leaders. Energy is this week’s hero; tech was Monday’s; healthcare was last week’s. When the rotation stops finding a new sector to lift, the index catches down to the breadth.

Formation — Reading Geopolitical Shocks: Why Oil Spiked 1% and Not 10%

The Question Everyone Is Asking

The US literally struck Iran on Wednesday. Oil rose 1%. In April, when the first Hormuz blockade hit, oil surged past $100 in one session. Why the muted reaction?

Three Factors That Determine Geopolitical Pricing

1. Prior pricing. Markets price in probabilities before events happen. After the June ceasefire collapsed and Tehran resumed Hormuz provocations last week, the market had already started rebuilding a risk premium. WTI climbed from $67.87 on July 2 to $73.52 by Tuesday’s close. By the time the strikes landed, crude had already moved +8% in a week. The marginal surprise was small.

2. Duration expectations. April’s blockade was treated as a potentially permanent closure of a critical chokepoint. The strikes on Wednesday are viewed as a tit-for-tat exchange with a finite endpoint. Markets price duration, not intensity. A full Hormuz closure for 30 days reprices oil +$30. A 48-hour military exchange reprices it +$2. The current pricing implies the market sees this as the latter.

3. Strategic reserves as a buffer. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve, though depleted from 2022–2023 releases, still provides a backstop. More importantly, US domestic production is at record levels (~13.4M bbl/day), making the global supply-demand picture less fragile than in previous Hormuz crises. The US is no longer as import-dependent as it was during the 2019 Iran tensions.

What This Means For Your Portfolio

Don’t overreact to geopolitical headlines. The market’s muted oil response tells you the smart money sees this as noise, not signal. The actionable takeaway: watch the second-order effects. If oil stays above $75 for two weeks, CPI expectations shift. That’s the real mechanism that could hurt your tech and REIT positions — not the strikes themselves.
