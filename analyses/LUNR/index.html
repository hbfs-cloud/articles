Verdict Express

C- Bullish Moderate confidence

Intuitive Machines is becoming a broader space-infrastructure prime, but the equity still carries financing and execution risk. Q2 revenue reached a record $206 million, more than four times the prior year, and backlog ended at $1.8 billion after $920 million of Q2 awards plus another $300 million early in Q3. The mix now includes spacecraft production, lunar delivery, mission operations, ground networks and a $600 million-plus commercial GEO satellite contract; national-security revenue rose from 3% to 30% of the quarter. Yet Q2 gross profit was only about $35.9 million before depreciation, operating loss was $47.1 million and operating cash use was $59.8 million. The company also sold stock: $235.2 million of net proceeds came through the $500 million ATM during Q2. The C- grade captures that tension. Status remains watch, with the existing $16.02 confirmation and $14.18 stop unchanged; backlog is not cash and a partial intraday reclaim is not activation.

Why Buy

  • Q2 revenue was a record $206M, versus $50.3M a year earlier.
  • Backlog reached $1.8B after $920M of Q2 awards and another $300M in Q3-to-date awards.
  • A $600M-plus contract covers three commercial GEO satellites.
  • National-security revenue expanded from 3% to 30% of Q2 revenue.
  • Management guides to $900M-$1.0B of 2026 revenue and positive adjusted EBITDA.

Why Avoid

  • Q2 operating loss was $47.1M and net cash used in operations was $59.8M.
  • Q2 gross profit before D&A was about $35.9M, only 17.4% of revenue.
  • $235.2M of Q2 net proceeds came from the $500M ATM, confirming active dilution.
  • Cash fell to $367.4M from $582.6M at year-end despite financing proceeds.
  • Launch failures, contract protests, milestone delays and customer concentration can move revenue between periods.

Business Overview

Intuitive Machines now generates revenue from spacecraft production, lunar payload delivery, mission operations and communications infrastructure. Q2's $206 million included $166.7 million of product revenue, $36.7 million of service revenue and $2.8 million of grants, showing that hardware production currently drives the mix.

The AI and defense transmission channel runs through orbital and lunar data infrastructure: spacecraft collect information, ground stations move it, and mission systems process it for civil, commercial and national-security customers. The Goonhilly and COMSAT acquisition broadens coverage, while NSNS investment targets recurring network revenue rather than one-off lander missions.

The model breaks when awards fail to convert into profitable milestones. Launch or spacecraft failures, fixed-price overruns, delayed government funding, protests, supply constraints and acquisition integration can consume cash before backlog becomes revenue. The $500 million ATM provides funding but transfers part of that execution risk to shareholders through dilution.
