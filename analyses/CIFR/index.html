Verdict Express

D Neutral Moderate confidence

Cipher Digital is a power-and-land development option whose near-term financial statements remain dominated by the shrinking mining base and construction financing. Q2 2026 revenue was only $25 million and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA was negative $30 million. Black Pearl delivered initial capacity in early August and rent began, while Apollo could add up to 900 MW. Yet the point-in-time market value of roughly $6.8 billion and enterprise value near $11.6 billion sit against just $191 million of trailing revenue. Cash of $832 million is supplemented by restricted bond proceeds, not freely available surplus; long-term borrowings were about $5.45 billion. The filing confirms a 15-year, 300 MW Black Pearl lease but does not disclose base rent or remaining capex, so a common-equity SOTP is not verifiable. The D grade is fundamental and appropriate. The unchanged $16.47 plan is rejected because $18.2299 and $18.735 provide only 1.17R and 1.50R against the $14.96 stop.

Business Overview

Cipher owns and develops large power sites, earning current revenue from bitcoin mining and seeking long-duration HPC rents as campuses are delivered. The economic drivers are energized megawatts, tenant lease commencement, mining hash economics and construction cost.

The AI channel runs through scarce power: Cipher secures interconnection and land, finances datacenter construction, and converts the site into rent when an HPC tenant accepts capacity. Black Pearl's early delivery is the first tangible step from option value to income.

The model breaks if optional sites never secure tenants, restricted financing cannot move between projects, construction costs rise, or mining cash flow weakens before leases ramp. A large warrant liability and rising share count also dilute the connection between asset growth and common-share value. The correct scorecard is rent-producing capacity, unrestricted parent cash and remaining project cost, not gross pipeline megawatts or restricted bond balances considered in isolation.

Dilution Risk

Cipher's capital structure is project-finance heavy. Shares outstanding rose to 414.3M from 405.0M at year-end; Google's 24,178,576 penny warrants imply a minimum 438.4M-share legal denominator if exercised, 5.8% above basic. That minimum explicitly excludes unvested awards and any additional shares or cash required by the $430M value-protection clause because the final outcome depends on future prices and settlement terms; it is a floor, not a fully diluted forecast. The $632.1M warrant liability is accounting value, not a share count. June cash was $831.8M, restricted cash about $3.73B and long-term borrowings roughly $5.45B. Restricted proceeds cannot be netted as parent cash.
