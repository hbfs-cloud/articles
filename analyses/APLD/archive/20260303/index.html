What Does Grade C+ Mean?

A C+ grade means that Applied Digital (APLD) is a speculative stock with high risk but also high potential reward. The company is in a very hot sector (AI infrastructure) and has secured important partnerships (like Oracle). However, it faces significant financial challenges, including high debt and unprofitability. This stock is suitable for investors who understand the risks of highly volatile growth stocks and have a higher risk tolerance.

Why Is Everyone Talking About APLD?

Applied Digital is building the essential "pickaxes" for the AI gold rush. They provide the computing power and data centers that companies need to run complex AI models. Recent news about a $2.15 billion funding deal with Oracle and a strategic move to create ChronoScale Corporation (for cloud services) shows the company is expanding aggressively in this vital area. However, a recent divestment by NVIDIA and extreme volatility keep investors on edge.

Company Profile: What Does Applied Digital Do?

In Simple Terms

Imagine you're running a very complex Artificial Intelligence program or a supercomputer simulation that needs a huge amount of processing power and data storage. Instead of building and maintaining your own massive computer facility, you "rent" space and power from a specialized company. That's what Applied Digital does. They build and operate these "digital infrastructure" facilities – essentially, large, powerful data centers – for companies that need a lot of computing muscle for AI, machine learning, and other high-performance tasks. They also have some services for crypto mining.

Applied Digital Corporation is based in Dallas, Texas, and focuses on designing, developing, and operating digital infrastructure solutions across North America. They cater to industries demanding high-performance computing (HPC) and artificial intelligence (AI).

Recent News: What's Happening with APLD?

Big News, Big Impact

Applied Digital has been very active with strategic moves that could shape its future. However, these also come with significant financial implications and market reactions.

March 2, 2026: $2.15 Billion Senior Secured Notes Offering

APLD announced a private offering of $2.15 billion in senior secured notes (a type of debt) due in 2031. The money raised will be used to fund the expansion of its Polaris Forge 2 AI Factory campus, which is leased to Oracle. This shows APLD's strong commitment to growing its infrastructure for AI and HPC, but also significantly increases its debt.

February 17, 2026: Strategic Transaction with Ekso Bionics Holdings, Inc.

APLD is creating a new publicly traded company called ChronoScale Corporation through a deal with Ekso Bionics. This new entity will focus on APLD's cloud services, with APLD maintaining about 97% ownership. This move aims to unlock value for its cloud business.

March 3, 2026: NVIDIA Sells Its Stake in APLD

NVIDIA, a giant in the AI chip industry, sold all its shares in Applied Digital (7,716,050 shares). While NVIDIA made a profit from this investment, the sale by such a prominent AI player can create negative sentiment and selling pressure on APLD's stock.

Revenue Trend: Fast Growth, But Unprofitable

Key Concept: Revenue vs Profit

Revenue is all the money a company takes in from selling its services. Profit is what's left after paying all the bills (employees, rent, interest on loans, etc.). APLD has strong revenue growth, but its operating and net profits are negative, meaning it's spending more than it earns right now. This is common for fast-growing companies investing heavily in their future.

Key Concept: Beta and Volatility

Beta measures how much a stock moves compared to the overall market. A Beta of 1 means it moves with the market. APLD's Beta of 7.34 means it moves more than 7 times as much as the market! This is extremely high and indicates that APLD is a very volatile stock, meaning its price can go up or down very sharply and quickly. The high ATR (Average True Range) also confirms this high daily volatility.

Key Concept: Support and Resistance

Support is a price level where a stock tends to stop falling and might bounce back up, like a floor. Resistance is a price level where a stock tends to stop rising and might fall back down, like a ceiling. These levels are important because many traders watch them for buying or selling opportunities. APLD's 200-day Moving Average is a very important support level.

What Is a "Swing Trade"?

A swing trade means holding a stock for a few days to a few weeks to capture a "swing" in price. It's different from day trading (buying and selling within the same day) or long-term investing (holding for months or years). For APLD, we're looking to buy near a support and sell near a resistance. Always use a stop loss order to automatically sell your shares if the price drops too much, protecting your investment.

What Is "Short Interest"?

Short interest shows how many investors are betting a stock's price will fall. They borrow shares, sell them, and hope to buy them back cheaper later. APLD has a very high short interest (31.25% of its available shares), meaning many traders are bearish. This can lead to rapid price changes: if the stock unexpectedly rises, these "short sellers" might rush to buy back shares to limit losses, pushing the price even higher (a "short squeeze").
