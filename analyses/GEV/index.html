GE Vernova is the broadest picks-and-shovels beneficiary of global electrification in the batch, with operating evidence across gas turbines, grid equipment and wind rather than a single AI contract. Business Overview

GE Vernova sells gas-power equipment and services, grid hardware and software, and wind turbines and services. Revenue is driven by equipment deliveries, long-cycle backlog conversion, service outages, pricing, manufacturing productivity and project execution across three distinct businesses.

The AI transmission channel is upstream power infrastructure: datacenter load requires new gas generation, transformers, switchgear and grid modernization. More than $5B of year-to-date data-center orders therefore enters Power and Electrification backlogs directly, while service agreements monetize the installed base for years.

The model breaks if turbine slots or grid equipment are delivered late, input inflation outruns contract pricing, wind losses persist, warranties rise or customers defer projects. A $176B backlog improves visibility but also locks in execution obligations across currencies and supply chains. At more than 60x trailing EV/EBITDA, the equity requires both backlog conversion and margin expansion. Investors should therefore monitor segment margin, working-capital release, cancellation behavior and service attachment as backlog converts, rather than treating every booked dollar as equally profitable.
