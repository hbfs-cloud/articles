Verdict Express

B Neutral Moderate confidence

Equinox has crossed into senior-producer scale, but the merger makes the current quarter a poor proxy for the combined company. Q2 pre-merger revenue was $769.8 million on 176,836 gold ounces, with $358.3 million adjusted EBITDA and $230.6 million GAAP net income. Following the July 31 Orla combination, management raised 2026 production guidance to 870,000-920,000 ounces, with consolidated AISC of $1,900-$2,000 per ounce and $600-$650 million growth capital. The transaction added Musselwhite and Camino Rojo, issued exactly 378,115,579 shares and produced a stated pro forma $214 million net-cash position excluding convertibles. Growth is credible but expensive: Valentine Phase 2 carries a $436 million initial budget, South Railroad requires about $395 million and first gold is targeted for 2028. Q2 all-operations AISC was already $2,175 per ounce, leaving execution sensitivity despite high realized gold. A B grade is defensible for scale and liquidity, but integration, incomplete purchase accounting and capex keep the setup in Wait until the existing $13.56 completed-bar and volume gate confirms. Value the combined asset portfolio, not backward-looking EPS alone.

Business Overview

Equinox earns revenue by producing and selling gold across Greenstone, Valentine, Nicaragua, Mesquite and, after the Orla merger, Musselwhite and Camino Rojo. Q2 production was 176,836 ounces; realized gold price, recovery, grade, AISC and growth capital determine cash generation. After the transaction, stated pro forma liquidity was $1.214B, but Valentine Phase 2 and South Railroad carry about $831M of combined initial budgets.

The AI transmission channel is remote: gold benefits mainly from monetary demand, real rates and risk hedging rather than data-center construction. AI can support broader power and capital spending, but it is not a measurable revenue driver for Equinox and should not inflate mine valuation.

The model breaks if Greenstone or Valentine ramps miss plan, integration fails, gold prices fall toward $1,900-$2,000 guidance AISC, or simultaneous Valentine and South Railroad spending consumes liquidity. Share issuance and convertible claims can also dilute otherwise successful production growth.
