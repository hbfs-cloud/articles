C+ Bullish Moderate confidence

Core Scientific now has a visible colocation business rather than merely a mining conversion story. Q2 2026 revenue rose to $164.2 million from $78.6 million, colocation contributed $136.7 million, gross profit reached $70 million and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA was $41.1 million. Billing capacity was 395 MW in Q2 and 437 MW by mid-July, supporting an annualized colocation GAAP revenue run rate near $635 million. The AMD partnership adds a 530 MW, 15-year anchor with more than $14 billion of potential base contract value, taking total leased power to 1.1 GW. The contradiction is cash intensity: Q2 capex was $797.5 million, trailing EBITDA remains negative in the point-in-time bundle, and the $1.155 billion GAAP net loss was distorted by fair-value changes on pre-existing warrants. An August facility added a $100 million revolver and $500 million letter-of-credit line with a $150 million liquidity floor but no new warrants. The C+ grade recognizes operating conversion but not yet mature returns. Trade status remains watch above $17.45.

Business Overview

Core Scientific develops high-density datacenters and sells colocation capacity under long-term contracts, while retaining a smaller digital-asset mining operation. Revenue is driven by billable megawatts, contractual pricing, power pass-throughs, uptime and residual mining hash economics.

The AI transmission channel is direct: customers such as CoreWeave and AMD reserve power and datacenter infrastructure, Core Scientific funds the conversion and build, and contracted fees begin as capacity is delivered. Billing power of 437 MW in mid-July versus 1.1 GW leased quantifies the remaining conversion runway.

The model breaks if construction is late, customer credit weakens, equipment or grid delivery fails, or financing covenants constrain the build. Potential contract value above $24B is undiscounted and delivered over many years. Warrant accounting can obscure operating results, while actual capex and liquidity determine whether common shareholders capture the residual economics. Investors should therefore track billing megawatts, customer acceptance, incremental rent and remaining construction cost together, rather than valuing the entire leased pipeline at an undiscounted contract headline.

Dilution Risk
