Business Overview

Target Corporation is one of the largest US general-merchandise retailers, operating roughly 2,000 large-format stores plus a fast-growing same-day digital business. Its differentiated "Tarjay" assortment — apparel, home, beauty and exclusive owned brands — sits alongside everyday consumables, and the company has leaned into "specialization at scale" with curated, higher-margin categories that Walmart and Amazon struggle to replicate. Trailing revenue is ~$106B with a 28% gross margin. Finviz· live

The investment story is the flywheel of high-margin ancillary revenue: Roundel retail-media advertising and Target Circle 360 memberships grew nearly 25% in Q1 FY2026, lifting gross margin to 29.0% from 28.2%. Same-day delivery (powered by Circle 360) is compounding at 27%+, and digital comps rose 8.9%. After a multi-year slump, Target just posted its first positive comparable-sales quarter in five periods — the clearest evidence yet that traffic and margin discipline are working in tandem. Reading the Numbers

Retail margins are structurally thin, so judge Target on cash generation and valuation, not net margin. At 9x EV/EBITDA and 0.7x EV/Rev, the market is pricing TGT for stagnation — yet revenue grew 6.7% and gross margin is widening as high-margin advertising and membership revenue scales. A 22% ROE on a cheap multiple is the core of the re-rating thesis. The Street's $131.81 mean target sits just below spot, which means the upside here is the analyst community raising targets as the turnaround proves durable.

Why the Stock Is Cheap

Target trades at a discount because the market remains skeptical of big-box retail and remembers the multi-year comp slump. The risk is genuine but bounded: a fortress balance sheet, 54-year dividend track record, and a turnaround now showing positive comps means the downside is a slow-grind business, not a solvency event. This is "cheap but turnaround-dependent" — own it for the re-rating, size it for the secular headwind.
