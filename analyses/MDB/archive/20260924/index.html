Business Overview

MongoDB sells Atlas, its managed multi-cloud database, and Enterprise Advanced subscriptions. Atlas revenue follows application usage, storage and transactions; Enterprise Advanced monetizes customer-managed deployments. RPO and current RPO add visibility to a model that still contains consumption variability.

AI agents need operational data, flexible schemas, vector retrieval and low-latency updates. Atlas can capture that workload through its document model, Vector Search and developer APIs. The financial signal is faster Atlas consumption and larger commitments, not downloads or prototypes that never reach production.

MongoDB can lose if hyperscalers steer developers toward native databases, relational incumbents close the document gap, or customers optimize Atlas usage. A 72% gross margin supports investment, but stock compensation and negative GAAP operations mean adjusted profit is not owner earnings.

The cleanest proof is Atlas growth remaining above company growth while current RPO converts and gross margin holds. If commitments rise because contract duration lengthens but consumption slows, the apparent visibility deserves a lower multiple.
