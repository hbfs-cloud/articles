Two prints defined the week

nLight is a laser company with a defense business growing fast. Its Q2 beat on both lines and the call highlighted aerospace and defense strengthZacks· Aug 6, 2026. The stock fell 15% after hours and finished Friday down 25.6% at 56.16 — and the driver was guidance, not the quarter: the current quarter was guided well below Q2's run rate, with a supply disruption deferring roughly $17M of revenue. A beat with a repairable supply problem, sold harder than most misses.

Applied Optoelectronics reported the same evening: record AI transceiver revenue, estimates beaten, a GAAP loss wider than a year ago even as non-GAAP swung to a small profitZacks· August 7, 2026. The stock is up roughly 44% on the week at 135.63, and it dragged the whole complex with it Friday: Coherent +13.4%, Lumentum +6.2%, Tower +12.4%, AXT +17.8%. The debate around the name is loud on both sides: short sellers went public against the stock the same afternoon, arguing transceivers are a commodity component, while a proposed US ban on Chinese transceivers runs the other way as a tailwindSimply Wall St· August 7, 2026.

Side by side, the two prints spell out what the market is paying for: AI datacenter optics revenue, this quarter, growing — and nothing else. A defense-laser beat from a company around breakeven lost a quarter of its value the same week a transceiver maker still losing money on a GAAP basis gained more than 40%. Whatever the print says, every name in the tables below gets marked against that one test.

AAOI traded a frenzied same-day call ladder from 135 up to 200 (7,800 contracts on the 140 calls alone, far out-of-the-money strikes at 160-200 lighting up) against protective puts clustered 120-135: classic post-print chase, both directions levered. Marvell fought a two-sided battle around 215-220, 12,300 same-day calls at 220 versus 11,600 puts at 215 — the market genuinely undecided about whether Friday's +3.9% reclaim holds. How to play it, tier by tier

Tier 1 — AI optics with earnings: hold trend, respect the calendar

Coherent (379.13, 50-day EMA 323), Lumentum (890.17, EMA 803) and Fabrinet (562.38, EMA 533) are in uptrends and report within days: LITE Tuesday Aug 11, COHR Wednesday Aug 12, FN the following Monday. The nLight precedent is the sizing instruction. Adding to an extended winner two sessions before its print, in a tape that took a beat down 26% over one soft guide, is donating your cost basis to whoever runs the post-earnings reclaim. Hold cores, let the prints happen, and treat the 50-day EMAs as the line between "pullback bought" and "distribution started". Fabrinet is the quietest of the three: the contract manufacturer building for everyone at 32× forward, profitable, and the closest thing this sector has to a safe hand — safe here still meaning a 25% drawdown.

Tier 2 — broken infrastructure: reclaim levels, not conviction

Marvell closed at 218.72, a hair above its 50-day EMA at 214 after Friday's bounce, still 34% off its high and below its flat 50-day SMA near 240; it does not report until Aug 27, so the level, not its own catalyst, is the trade. But its tape is not clean before then — Marvell trades the same AI-optics factor that moved it +3.9% on an AAOI print, and that factor's referendum is Tuesday and Wednesday. So the entry waits for the referendum, same as tier 1: if Lumentum and Coherent hold their gains, an entry near 218.72 risks about 2% to a daily close back below 214, against the flat 50-day SMA near 240 as first objective — roughly 4:1, with two quiet weeks before its own print. If either gets the nLight treatment, there is no reclaim trade to take; tier 2 breaks lower first. Either way, a close below 214 means the reclaim failed; no trade, no averaging. Ciena (412.39 vs EMA 431), Vertiv (272.40 vs 293) and Astera Labs stay in the penalty box until they close back above those averages; Ciena doesn't report until September, so nothing forces a repricing. Corning at −39% and IPG at −42% are the value trap exhibits: real companies, real cash flow, and no AI transceiver line for this market to pay up for.

Tier 3 — story stocks: no earnings floor, no averaging down

POET, Lightwave Logic, Sivers, Aeluma, Syntec: down 42% to 63%, pre-profit, and structurally unable to produce the one thing this tape rewards, which is a revenue print. They rallied Friday with the group (POET +4.5%, Sivers +10%), and that is what they are: high-beta expressions of the tier-1 tape, not independent theses. If you hold them, they are lottery tickets on co-packaged optics adoption, sized so that a zero doesn't matter. Buying more because they are 60% off the high assumes a floor these balance sheets do not provide. AAOI sits between tiers: real record revenue, still loss-making on a GAAP basis (non-GAAP just turned positive), a public short seller, and a 42% drawdown even after a +44% week. It also carries live dilution machinery — an active at-the-market equity program, convertible notes due 2030 and Amazon warrants deep in the moneySEC EDGAR· Aug 2026 — every rip can be sold into. Momentum only, with Friday's 130.50 low as the risk line.

Portfolio check: these 20+ tickers are one factor. A basket spread across transceivers, lasers, substrates and test gear feels diversified and trades as a single AI-capex bet — Friday proved it, when an AAOI print moved Stockholm-listed Sivers. Size the theme, not the line items. Vertiv isn't even optics; it is datacenter power and cooling riding the same factor.

The thesis, and next week's referendum

Base case: this is a violent mid-cycle correction in a sector whose AI demand is real, not a bubble unwinding. The evidence: record transceiver revenue at AAOI, tier-1 names holding rising 50-day trends, and the market still instantly rewarding AI-linked prints while punishing everything else. The pullback has been a sorting mechanism, not an exit.

The referendum is Tuesday and Wednesday. Lumentum (Aug 11)Yahoo Finance· live and Coherent (Aug 12)Yahoo Finance· live report into put-tilted positioning and an 8× and 4.5× year. Two outcomes matter: if they deliver AI-driven guidance and hold their gains — Coherent above 323, Lumentum above 803 — the correction in tier 1 is over and the tier-2 reclaims (Marvell 214, Ciena 431) become buyable follow-through. If either gets the nLight treatment (a beat sold hard), the sorting continues: tier 1 revisits its 50-days from above, tier 2 breaks lower, and the story tier has another leg down with no earnings to catch it. I'd rather buy tier 1 confirmed above trend after the prints than guess before them.

Kill switch for the whole thesis: an AI capex signal from a hyperscaler, not from photonics itself — that is the factor every tier actually trades on.
