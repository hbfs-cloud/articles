Business Overview

CrowdStrike sells cloud subscriptions to the Falcon platform, spanning endpoint, identity, cloud workload, log management, managed detection and security operations. Revenue is driven by customer count, module adoption, renewal and expansion, best tracked through ARR and net-new ARR.

AI creates two paid channels: customers need to secure more machine identities and AI workloads, while Falcon uses its telemetry to automate detection and response. The economic test is whether those products increase modules and net-new ARR, not whether management mentions agents.

The model breaks through a platform outage, missed detections, pricing pressure or slower expansion. Stock compensation and incident-related costs can keep GAAP economics far below the non-GAAP presentation even while free cash flow remains strong.

CrowdStrike must turn net-new ARR into reported operating profit after incident costs and equity compensation. Platform breadth matters only if release controls, retention and diluted-share cash generation improve together.
