Verdict Express

D Neutral Moderate confidence

TeraWulf is no longer economically just a bitcoin miner: Q2 2026 HPC lease revenue was $31.9 million, 71% of total revenue, and capacity reached 102 MW in early July. The 401 MW, 20-year Anthropic lease carries roughly $19 billion of base contract value, with Google credit support reducing tenant-credit risk. That pivot is real. It is also unfinished and expensive. Q2 revenue was only $44.8 million, non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA was negative $18.3 million, another 336 MW was under construction, and management's $8-$10 million cost per IT MW implies billions of future deployment spend. The April base offering priced 47.4 million shares at $19 and included a 7.11 million-share option; the Q2 10-Q proves the option was exercised, bringing completed issuance to 54.51 million shares and $1.0357 billion gross. Converts, project notes and warrants remain material. Cash and restricted cash near $3.0 billion cannot be viewed apart from more than $5 billion of debt. The D grade reflects weak current earnings and dilution. The unchanged $16.29 plan is rejected: $17.4899 and $17.8899 offer only 0.78R and 1.04R against the $14.75 stop.

Business Overview

TeraWulf operates power-secured campuses that now earn primarily HPC lease revenue, alongside residual bitcoin-mining economics. Revenue depends on billable IT megawatts, contractual rent and, for mining, hash production and bitcoin prices.

The AI channel is direct: TeraWulf develops powered shells and cooling, tenants install or contract compute, and rent starts as capacity is delivered. The 401 MW Anthropic agreement, supported by Google credit, can convert power access into long-duration datacenter cash flow from 2027 onward.

The model breaks if construction misses delivery dates, cost per MW exceeds plan, tenant support weakens or capital markets close before rent starts. Mining exposure adds commodity volatility, while converts, project debt, warrants and recent equity issuance divide future project economics among multiple claims. Investors must therefore compare delivered, billable capacity with construction spending and all equity-linked claims before capitalizing the full lease value into today's common shares.

Dilution Risk

TeraWulf's pivot has been funded through common equity, converts, warrants and project debt. The April final 424B5 priced 47.4M shares at $19 and described a 7.11M-share option; the Q2 10-Q proves that 54.51M shares were ultimately issued for $1.0357B gross, so the option was exercised and must be included. Preliminary prospectuses are the same program, not extra issuance. Against roughly 499.0M basic shares, initial conversion rates imply about 190.8M gross convertible shares across the 2030, 2031 and 2032 notes, and approximately 73.6M Google warrants are additional claims. The resulting 763.4M minimum observable basic-plus-quantified-claims sensitivity excludes other warrants and awards and is not a fully diluted forecast. Capped calls may reduce economic dilution but do not reduce the legal claims; project notes remain debt and restricted construction cash is not excess parent liquidity.
