CoreWeave is the purest listed expression of hyperscale GPU scarcity, but the equity is also a leveraged claim on flawless datacenter delivery. Q2 2026 revenue rose 112.5% to $2.58 billion and adjusted EBITDA reached $1.51 billion, while backlog stood near $104 billion before more than $25 billion of early-Q3 commitments. That demand signal is exceptional. The counterweight is equally concrete: Q2 capital expenditure was $6.42 billion, quarterly interest expense was $640 million, GAAP net loss was $626 million, and full-year capex guidance is $35-$39 billion. Customer concentration, lease commitments and a disclosed material weakness make the balance-sheet-funded growth model less forgiving than the revenue chart suggests. The July 31 legal denominator is 551.537 million Class A plus Class B shares, implying about $48.12 billion of equity value and $94.19 billion of enterprise value at the August 28 snapshot. That is roughly 12.4x trailing revenue and 24.8x EBITDA. The C- grade measures fundamental quality; the $87.73 setup remains only a watch.

Business Overview

CoreWeave rents GPU-accelerated compute and related storage, networking and managed software under capacity contracts. Revenue is driven by deployed clusters, contracted utilization, pricing and the speed with which powered shells become billable infrastructure.

The direct AI transmission channel is customer demand for training and inference on NVIDIA systems: signed commitments support datacenter and GPU purchases, and completed deployments convert backlog into cloud revenue. Active power of 1.5 GW versus roughly 3.7 GW contracted shows both the opportunity and the delivery gap.

The model breaks if a concentrated customer base delays workloads, facilities or chips arrive late, power is unavailable, or debt and lease funding becomes uneconomic. Because capex and interest precede revenue, even a modest commissioning delay can damage liquidity and equity value before it appears in backlog. Commissioning discipline must therefore be judged quarter by quarter against active power, capital expenditure, interest expense and customer collections, rather than inferred from the nominal backlog alone.
