Verdict Express

C+ Neutral Moderate confidence

Applied Optoelectronics has the batch's strongest reported revenue acceleration and its weakest equity-quality safeguards. Q2 2026 revenue rose 86.4% to $191.9 million, with data-center revenue up 140.4% to $107.7 million and 56.1% of sales. That is a direct AI transmission: 400G, 800G and 1.6T optical modules move traffic between accelerators, switches and racks. Yet GAAP gross margin fell to 27.7% from 30.3%, trailing operating and net margins remain negative, and the top ten customers represented 99% of quarterly revenue. The company is spending heavily to expand capacity, including $335.1 million of first-half capex, while a reviewed August prospectus establishes a $600 million ATM and other reviewed supplements contain convertible and active-warrant terms. Q3 guidance of $255-$290 million revenue and 29%-30.5% non-GAAP gross margin uses about 92.8 million shares, a forward diluted assumption rather than a current point-in-time total. With no captured August 28 RTH opening bar, the 54/100 grade and dormant wait setup are the only defensible posture.

Business Overview

AAOI designs and manufactures optical transceivers, lasers and broadband equipment for internet data centers, CATV, telecom and FTTH customers. Q2 revenue was split 56.1% data center, 42.0% CATV, 1.8% telecom and 0.1% FTTH/other.

The AI channel is optical bandwidth: larger accelerator clusters require more 400G/800G links and a transition toward 1.6T between servers, switches and racks. AAOI's in-house laser production and U.S., Taiwan and China manufacturing can improve control over critical components during a capacity ramp.

The model breaks if a few hyperscale customers delay qualification or deployment, pricing falls faster than manufacturing cost, or new capacity ramps with poor yield. A 99% top-ten customer concentration, negative trailing margins and repeated financing tools make revenue growth alone insufficient. The balance sheet was replenished through successive ATM sales while capex expanded, so revenue acceleration must be judged against a much larger share denominator. The model breaks if a few hyperscale customers delay qualifications, 800G pricing erodes, or CATV demand cannot offset data-center concentration.
