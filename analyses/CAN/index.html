Verdict Express

D Neutral Moderate confidence

Canaan is a distressed ASIC vendor with an attached self-mining operation, not a clean bitcoin proxy. Q1 revenue was $62.7 million, including $42.9 million of mining-machine product revenue and $19.1 million from mining, but gross loss was $22.9 million and GAAP net loss reached $88.7 million. The company produced 257 BTC and increased installed computing power to roughly 11 EH/s, yet negative gross profit shows that scale did not cover inventory, pricing and energy economics. July operations improved global operating hashrate to 14.24 EH/s and treasury to 1,917 BTC plus 3,952 ETH; Ethiopia was paused, so installed capacity overstates active output. The capital structure also matters: 63.66 million ADS were sold at $1.131 in November 2025, and the current 724.7 million share-equivalent snapshot leaves material dilution sensitivity. A D grade is appropriate before September 3 results. The event veto is absolute: neither the $0.45 reference activation nor valuation framing is actionable until fresh earnings reset the facts and geometry.

  • Ethiopia was paused, making installed capacity a poor output proxy.
  • 63.66M ADS were sold at $1.131 in the November 2025 offering.

Business Overview

Canaan sells Avalon ASIC mining machines and operates its own bitcoin-mining fleet. Hardware revenue depends on machine shipments, selling price and inventory write-downs; mining revenue depends on active hashrate, BTC price, network difficulty and power cost. Q1 product revenue was $42.9M and mining revenue was $19.1M. Subsequent customer cash collections of $42M matter because receivable conversion supports working-capital reuse.

The AI transmission channel is semiconductor-adjacent rather than direct. Canaan designs specialized compute silicon, but Avalon chips solve SHA-256 mining workloads, not AI training or inference. AI foundry demand can instead compete for fabrication capacity and raise wafer or packaging costs without creating Canaan AI revenue.

The model breaks when ASIC prices fall below inventory cost, customers delay payment, deployed machines remain offline, or power and difficulty erase mining spread. ADR dilution, foreign-private-issuer disclosure cadence and the paused Ethiopia fleet add financing and operating uncertainty that treasury coins cannot remove.

Dilution Risk

Canaan's ADS structure requires per-ADS discipline. The October 24, 2025 424B5 created a new up-to-$270M ADS ATM, with each ADS representing 15 Class A shares; it also states that the terminated 2024 program had sold 21,088,579 ADS for about $42.5M net. The November 4 filing separately records 63,660,477 ADS sold at $1.131. The minimum observable basic denominator in the current market snapshot is 724.7M ADS equivalents. A current fully diluted ADS denominator is explicitly unavailable because the reviewed filings do not reconcile options and warrants; diluted per-ADS NAV is therefore not calculable.
