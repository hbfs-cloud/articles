Verdict Express

B+ Neutral Moderate confidence

First Majestic offers unusually direct silver exposure with strong current cash conversion, but its B+ grade should not be read as immunity from cost inflation. Q2 revenue rose 57% YoY to $415.5 million, 60% generated from silver, while GAAP net earnings reached $109.4 million and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA $257.1 million. Free cash flow increased 150% to $194.6 million, leaving $1.25 billion of cash and restricted treasury assets against $331.1 million debt. The operational tension is visible in all-in sustaining cost of $25.68 per silver-equivalent ounce versus $21.02 a year earlier; the exceptional $63.98 realized silver price created a $40.27 AISC margin that could compress quickly. Los Gatos rockfall and San Dimas labor disruptions were resolved, while an 11% stronger Mexican peso remains a cost headwind. Inventory also matters: 1.01 million silver ounces and 4,730 gold ounces, worth $78 million, were excluded from revenue. Quality is above average within a cyclical miner, not absolute. The setup stays in Wait until a completed 15-minute close above $22.19 confirms with the stated volume condition.

Business Overview

First Majestic mines and sells silver and gold, with silver supplying 60% of Q2 revenue. Production, grades, recoveries, realized prices and Mexican operating costs determine margin; Q2 output was 3.80M silver ounces and 34,660 gold ounces across a portfolio requiring continuous sustaining investment. Another $78M of held silver and gold inventory was excluded from reported revenue.

The AI transmission channel is silver's industrial use in power electronics, solar and electrification supporting data-center infrastructure. This is a demand tailwind, not a dedicated AI revenue stream, and monetary or investment demand can dominate near-term silver pricing and inventory decisions.

The model breaks if ore grades or recoveries disappoint, the Mexican peso and labor costs rise, disruptions recur, or silver prices normalize faster than AISC. Inventory timing can shift reported revenue, while reserve replacement and sustaining capital remain necessary even during high-margin quarters and cannot be inferred from adjusted EBITDA alone.
