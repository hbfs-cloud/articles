Verdict Express

A- Neutral Moderate confidence

Sterling is a profitable infrastructure contractor with unusually strong data-center and manufacturing exposure, but the current growth rate includes large acquisitions. Q2 revenue rose 90% to $1.17 billion; CEC and Stone Ridge contributed $250.8 million, while management said organic growth was approximately 50%. GAAP net income increased 120% to $155.8 million, adjusted EPS rose 116% to $5.80 and adjusted EBITDA margin reached about 22%. Backlog was $4.33 billion, up 116%, but combined backlog of $5.62 billion includes $1.28 billion of unsigned awards, mostly from acquired businesses. E-Infrastructure is the engine: mission-critical data centers, manufacturing and semiconductor work represented 92% of its backlog. Transportation and Building were weaker. Management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $19.70-$20.30. The A- grade remains justified, but trade status stays wait because no regular-session opening bar was captured; the $512.36 activation and August 25 structural stop at $480.5799 remain inactive.

Business Overview

Sterling operates through E-Infrastructure, Transportation and Building Solutions. E-Infrastructure performs site development and electrical work for data centers, manufacturing and semiconductor facilities; Transportation builds public roads and related assets; Building supplies concrete and plumbing work to housing and commercial projects.

The AI transmission channel is physical: hyperscaler and enterprise compute spending becomes data-center land preparation, utilities and mission-critical electrical work. CEC expanded Sterling from site development into electrical services, increasing the amount of each project the company can capture. At Q2, mission-critical work represented 92% of E-Infrastructure backlog and carried the growth profile.

The model breaks if data-center awards are delayed, acquired businesses miss margin targets, unsigned awards fail to convert, or fixed-price labor and material costs outrun estimates. Housing affordability is already pressuring Building, while Sterling is deliberately shifting resources away from lower-growth Transportation projects toward higher-margin E-Infrastructure assignments.
