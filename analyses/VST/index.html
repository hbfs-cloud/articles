Management reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $6.8-$7.6 billion and adjusted free cash flow before growth of $3.925-$4.725 billion. The Helix digital joint venture adds up to $1 billion of initial commitment, but it is capital allocation, not guaranteed AI return. At roughly 10.4x EV/EBITDA and 2.45x market cap/revenue, valuation is more grounded than peers. The C grade reflects balance-sheet and hedge complexity despite strong cash flow. Trade state remains watch above $141.10.

Business Overview

Vistra owns generation and a large retail-electricity platform, earning from power production, capacity, hedges and customer margins. Revenue and cash flow depend on fleet availability, realized commodity spreads, retail load, hedging discipline, fuel and operating costs.

The AI transmission channel is wholesale and contracted electricity demand: new datacenters tighten regional power balances, support capacity prices and create opportunities for long-term supply or site partnerships. Vistra captures this through existing dispatchable and nuclear assets rather than by selling compute.

The model breaks through plant outages, adverse hedges, retail weather risk, regulatory intervention, excessive leverage or capital allocation into digital projects that earn less than repurchases. Heavy hedging stabilizes current cash flow but delays participation in higher future prices. GAAP results can swing with derivative marks even when underlying operating cash generation remains comparatively stable. Investors should therefore judge new digital investments against the proven alternative of repurchases, using incremental cash returns after financing rather than thematic exposure as the hurdle.
