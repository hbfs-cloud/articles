Verdict Express

A- Bullish Moderate

AerCap is the world's largest aircraft lessor at 8.5x forward earnings, and it keeps doing the two things the market pays for: beating estimates (six consecutive quarters, the July 29 print by 30%) and shrinking the share count ($1.4B repurchased year to date). Management raised full-year EPS guidance to roughly $16.80 at the last report. The aircraft shortage does the heavy lifting: lease rates keep repricing higher while the order book is sold out for years. Two things keep this at A- instead of A+. The stock sits within 1% of its 52-week high, so a fresh buyer at 155 risks more to a sensible stop than the next resistance offers. And the insider tape is loud: fourteen sales and zero buys in ninety days, including the CFO ($11M), the CEO ($7.4M) and a chief accounting officer who sold every directly held share in June (a trust-held balance remains); all before the earnings pop, though part of the spring activity traces to routine RSU vesting rather than discretionary exits. The trade is to let the price come to you at the 150 zone, where the risk/reward flips above 3:1.

Why Buy

  • Six consecutive EPS beats, the July 29 print came in at $5.14 adjusted vs $3.94 expected, a 30% surprise
  • FY2026 EPS guidance raised to ~$16.80, excluding any further gains on sale
  • 8.5x forward earnings and ~1.30x book value for a 22% ROE franchise
  • Buyback machine: 4.9M shares ($691M) retired in Q2 alone, over $1.4B year to date
  • Aircraft scarcity supports lease-rate repricing for years — the order book is effectively sold out
  • Beta 0.93 and 0.8% short interest: a calm, under-shorted compounder

Why Avoid

  • Insider distribution into strength: 14 sales, 0 buys in 90 days: CFO $11M, CEO $7.4M, and the chief accounting officer sold all his directly held shares on June 15 (a trust-held balance remains)
  • The customers are airlines: an oil shock or recession stresses lessee credit first — AerCap wrote off roughly $2.7B on its Russia fleet in 2022 (later partly recovered via insurance)
  • 0.7% below the 52-week high: at-market risk/reward is poor until a pullback or a confirmed breakout
  • AerCap raises guidance almost every quarter; the raise is partly expected, so its signal value is lower than it looks
  • Options positioning flags a bearish put/call tilt on the name
  • $43.1B of debt against $1.5B cash is the leasing model working as designed, but it makes the equity rate-sensitive

Business Overview

AerCap owns and leases roughly two thousand aircraft plus engines and helicopters, buying them in bulk from Airbus and Boeing and renting them to airlines worldwide. The June quarter delivered $726M of net income ($4.59 per share) and $811M adjusted ($5.14), with revenue of $2.17B. Book value per share reached $119.21 at June 30, up about 16% in a year, and management keeps buying back stock below 1.4x that book value, which mechanically compounds per-share value. The aircraft shortage caused by delivery delays at both airframers keeps used-aircraft values and lease rates firm, which is why gains on sale keep landing above book.

Thesis

An 8.5x-earnings compounder with six straight beats, a raised guide and a shrinking share count deserves a position, at the right price. Our number is 150: there the stop tucks under the EMA50, roughly one ATR below entry, and the first target pays 3.3 times the risk.

Catalysts

  • Next earnings October 28 — no earnings gap inside the trade window; the gap risk that does live inside it is macro: an August jobs or CPI print lands within 15-20 days, and this is a rate-sensitive book (see Invalidation)
  • Continued buyback execution shrinks the float every week
  • Air-show and delivery-delay headlines keep repricing lease rates upward
  • Analyst mean target $170.89 sits 10% above the current price, in line with the TP2

Invalidation

  • A daily close below 145.50 loses the EMA50 shelf and puts the base low (144.07) in play: stand aside
  • A fresh cluster of insider sales after the raise would upgrade the distribution signal from valuation to conviction
  • A hot jobs/CPI print that reprices rates sharply; cut the position, the funding-cost lag works against the model in fast moves
