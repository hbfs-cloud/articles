**Title:** Energy Broke Out. The Story Everyone Is Telling About It Is Wrong.

**Subtitle:** XLE leads all sixteen US sector funds on both relative-strength windows and cleared a three-week base on volume. But crude is up 4.80% over twenty sessions, not down — which changes the trade, not the direction.

---

The line making the rounds this week: oil is falling, energy equities are rising anyway, so the market is finally paying for buybacks and capital discipline instead of the barrel.

The first half of that sentence does not survive contact with the prices. Crude is up.

## What the tape shows

XLE closed at $61.02 on 12 August, the last complete session available: **+6.47% over five sessions** (from $57.31 on 5 August) and **+7.55% over twenty** (from $56.74 on 13 July). SPY did +0.35% and +3.11% across the same windows. That is roughly 6.1 points of relative strength over five sessions and 4.4 over twenty — against an index that is itself trending, with a 14-day RSI of 67.36 on 13 August and price above its 20-, 50- and 200-day averages ($759.95, $748.69, $708.00).

The breakout has a date. From mid-July to early August, XLE ground sideways between $57.11 (floor, 7 August) and $59.69 (ceiling, 31 July). On 10 August it closed at $60.18, with a session high of $60.21, on 33.68 million shares against 21 to 27 million in the preceding sessions. A range exit with participation behind it.

Technically, at 12–13 August: RSI 64.49, MACD at 1.004 above its 0.797 signal line, price above the 20-day ($58.80), 50-day ($57.70) and 200-day ($53.40) averages, average daily range $1.28. Engaged, not overheated.

And it is broad. Over twenty sessions: XOM +10.65%, CVX +11.75%, COP +13.82%, SLB +10.58%, OXY +6.86%. Five different business models pulling the same way.

## What we know

The catalyst has a date too, and a number. July US producer prices, released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on 13 August, came in at **0.0% month over month against +0.2% expected**, and +4.7% over twelve months against +4.9% expected. Ex food and energy: +0.2% against +0.3% expected. That repriced the rate-cut path and rotated money into cheap cyclicals. Energy is the natural first stop: low multiple, high cycle sensitivity.

Read the zero before celebrating it. Energy manufactured it: final demand goods fell 0.7% on the month, energy 3.1%, gasoline 5.7%. Strip trade services out as well and the underlying measure accelerates to +0.4%, from +0.1% in June. The rate repricing carrying this rotation rests on the most volatile component of the index, and the next print can take it back. That is exactly what the third falsifier says.

Now the barrel. The spot proxy — no contract-roll effect — closed at **$125.03 on 13 August, up 5.18% over five sessions** (from $118.87 on 6 August) **and 4.80% over twenty** (from $119.30 on 16 July). The front-month contract closed at $83.29 on 12 August, itself up from $75.22 on 5 August, and traded at $81.73 intraday on 14 August. A pullback on the front month with no equivalent on the spot proxy is the signature of a contract roll. That is where the "down 2.9%, below $81" figure in the popular narrative comes from.

Positioning, meanwhile, is empty. No unusual options activity was detected on XOM, CVX, COP, SLB or OXY at the time of collection. Short interest as of the 31 July settlement is light to moderate: XOM 1.05% of float (42.35M shares, 2.75 days to cover), CVX 1.11% (20.25M, 2.75), COP 1.58% (18.51M, 3.09), SLB 3.93% (55.96M, 3.85), OXY 2.31% (22.72M, 2.91). No pile of forced buy-backs waiting to be lit, and no aggressive institutional footprint captured on the options side.

The calendar is nearly empty, which is itself information. None of the twelve sector heavyweights — XOM, CVX, COP, WMB, SLB, EOG, PSX, MPC, OKE, KMI, VLO, OXY — reports earnings in the next ten days. The only dated checkpoint before the next reference session is the weekly US petroleum status report on **Wednesday 19 August**.

Inside the sector, dispersion matters. Integrateds and producers cluster around RSI 62 (XOM 62.09 at $158.61, CVX 62.15 at $197.70, COP 62.13 at $124.52). Services and infrastructure lag (SLB 56.73, OXY 56.85, OKE 57.84, KMI 53.04, WMB 51.74 with a negative MACD, EOG 51.57). Refiners are stretched: PSX 76.27 at $232.61, MPC 75.10 at $356.37, VLO 73.99 at $342.92 — MPC sits 22% above its 50-day average of $292.32.

## The thesis, and what would kill it

Crude is up 4.80% over twenty sessions. Energy equities are up 6.86% to 13.82% over the same window — 1.4 times the underlying move at the low end (OXY), 2.9 times at the high end (COP). That is not a disconnect; it is operating leverage doing exactly what it is supposed to do when a variable selling price meets a largely fixed cost base.

So: the 13 August print supplied the multiple, the barrel supplies the earnings, and the 10 August volume says someone actually bought at that level. Three inputs pointing the same way beats one very good one.

The operational corollary matters more than the thesis. In a divergence trade you ignore the barrel by construction. Here you cannot. If crude breaks, the amplification runs in reverse at the same coefficient.

**Three falsifiers, with levels:**

1. **The spot proxy back below $119.30** — its 16 July base — erases the entire twenty-session move. At that point equities are rising alone and the trade becomes a multiple bet with no earnings underneath it.
2. **XLE closing below $59.69**, the old range ceiling, means the 10 August break was a false start. Below $57.11, the base floor, there is no thesis left at all.
3. **A hawkish macro reversal.** The trade rests on a rate repricing, not on an earnings stream — there is none for ten days. And that repricing is fragile by construction: the measure excluding food, energy and trade services is already running at +0.4% on the month. One inflation print confirming that acceleration takes back what 13 August gave, quickly.

**XLE** is the core. Buy the retest of the broken $59.69 ceiling; stop at $58.50, below the 20-day average and back inside the old range, about 1.3 times the average daily range from mid-entry. Targets are the $62.56–$62.79 band, the closing and intraday highs of 27 March 2026, just before April's tariff liquidation dragged the fund to roughly $39. About 1-to-1.5 on reward-to-risk, which is unremarkable and deliberate: this leg carries theme exposure with zero single-name risk, it is not the return engine. Above $62.79 there is no identified resistance, so management switches to a trailing stop rather than a fixed target. Take it now — it depends on the rate repricing and the price structure, both already in place, not on the 19 August inventory print.

**COP** is the pure producer, the most direct exposure to the selling price, the best twenty-session performer of the five tested, and still the cheapest forward multiple of the group ex-EOG at 13.2x with RSI at 62. One honest caveat on the $119.30 stop: it is not a chart support. There is nothing between spot and the 50-day average at $116.60. It is a volatility boundary — beyond it, the pullback stops being a breath. The structural invalidation remains a close below $116.60. Half size before 19 August, complete after if crude holds.

**XOM** is the shock absorber, not the performance leg: downstream integration smooths part of what production absorbs when the barrel moves, and the liquidity lets you exit without a price concession on a bad day. Its 1.05% short interest is the lowest of the group. Same timing as COP. CVX substitutes without changing the thesis for anyone who prefers the 3.6% dividend — $197.70, RSI 62.15, $4.43 daily range, 50-day at $187.04.

**The refiners get nothing.** Three RSI readings between 74 and 76 in a sector whose fund reads 64.5: they have already walked the road XLE is starting. Buying here pays for the move exactly when the risk-reward inverts. It is not a short either — selling a trending stock because it is extended is a reliable way to lose money on a correct observation. Wait for RSI back under 65 or a pullback toward the 50-day.

Same reasoning kills the loudest number in the whole scan: the gold miners fund, +23.6% over twenty sessions and 20 points of relative strength against SPY, driven by bullion above $4,400. Spectacular number, bad entry. A break that just happened gives you a usable stop; a 23% move already delivered gives you none.

**Sizing has two guardrails, not one.** First, risk: these three legs are the same bet, so their risk adds rather than diversifies. Total theme budget 2% of the account — 1% XLE, 0.5% COP, 0.5% XOM. Second, notional: on a $100,000 account, 1% risk on XLE with a $1.65 stop buys 606 shares, roughly $36,500 of exposure. A tight stop is not a licence to size up. Cap theme notional at 30% of the account regardless of what the risk math says.

## What ends it

- Spot crude proxy below **$119.30** → exit COP and XOM, halve XLE.
- XLE close below **$59.69** → false breakout, exit XLE, no new single-name entries.
- XLE close below **$57.11** → thesis dead, theme abandoned until a new base forms.
- **19 August** petroleum inventories → the only dated checkpoint; the second half of COP and XOM is not sized before it.
- Macro repricing the other way — an inflation print confirming the +0.4% underlying PPI measure → halve the whole theme without waiting for stops.
- Refiners unwinding (PSX, VLO, MPC) → weighs on the fund without saying anything about the rest of the sector. Hold stops, do not add, do not confuse it with a thesis break.

One confirmation is missing and it is worth naming: no unusual options flow was detected on the five leaders tested. Conviction here rests on price and share volume, nothing else. That is already priced into the position sizing — it should not be paid for twice by exiting on the first shake.
