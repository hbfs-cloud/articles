Marsh is a resilient insurance-brokerage and consulting compounder, but Q2 was solid rather than uniformly strong. Revenue rose 6% to $7.4 billion and 5% on an underlying basis; GAAP EPS increased 7% to $2.63 and adjusted EPS rose 9% to $2.96. Consulting led with 8% underlying growth, including 13% at Marsh Management Consulting, while Risk and Insurance Services grew only 3% underlying and Guy Carpenter declined 2%. The business benefits from recurring client relationships, premium growth, specialized data and advisory talent, but it carries $22.38 billion of reported debt and remains exposed to insurance pricing, claims-related liability and acquisition integration. Capital returns are tangible: Marsh repurchased $750 million in Q2 and increased the quarterly dividend 10% to $0.99. The B- grade is unchanged. Business Overview

Marsh earns fees and commissions by placing insurance and reinsurance, advising clients on risk and capital, and selling consulting services through Mercer and Marsh Management Consulting. Q2 revenue split roughly $4.8 billion for Risk and Insurance Services and $2.6 billion for Consulting.

The AI transmission channel is productivity and advisory content. Better modeling can improve risk selection, placement analytics, workforce advice and consulting delivery; the Oliver Wyman-Anthropic relationship may deepen those tools. Revenue still depends on client retention, insurance pricing, exposure growth and billable expertise, not software-seat growth.

The model breaks if insurance rates soften faster than exposure grows, litigation or fiduciary claims rise, cyber incidents damage trust, or acquisitions fail to earn their cost of capital. Talent inflation can also absorb revenue growth because compensation is the largest operating expense. Guy Carpenter's Q2 decline shows that scale does not eliminate cyclical pockets.
