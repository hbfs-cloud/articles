Business Overview

Vicor sells high-density power-conversion modules in two product families, Brick Products and Advanced Products, and also earns royalty revenue from intellectual property. Revenue depends on design wins, production ramps, product mix and license economics rather than semiconductor units alone.

The AI transmission channel is rack power density. GPUs and custom accelerators require efficient conversion from rack-level distribution to low-voltage, high-current loads near the processor; Vicor's factorized power and vertical-delivery architectures target lower losses and smaller board area in those systems.

The model breaks if customers choose competing voltage-regulator architectures, design wins do not reach volume, manufacturing cannot scale, or royalty contributions prove episodic. Patent litigation, customer concentration and the rebuilt 76.0x EV/EBITDA multiple amplify any delay. Backlog rose to $379.7M, but customer acceptance, royalty accounting and the timing of high-density module ramps can make quarterly revenue uneven. The thesis fails if hyperscalers redesign power architectures, internalize conversion, or new capacity runs below the volume needed to support current margins.
