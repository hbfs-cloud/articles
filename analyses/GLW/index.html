What the Company Does

Corning is a 175-year-old specialty glass and ceramics company that manufactures optical fiber for telecom and data centers, display glass for TVs and phones, Gorilla Glass for mobile devices, automotive substrates for emissions control, and life sciences labware. The company is uniquely positioned at the intersection of multiple secular growth themes: AI data center infrastructure, 5G rollout, foldable displays, and electric vehicles.

Current Setup: GLW has pulled back -24.7% from its 52-week high of $162.10 (Feb 25) to $121.80. The correction follows a massive run-up driven by the Meta $6B fiber supply deal announcement. RSI at 44.6 indicates the stock is approaching oversold territory. EMA 50 ($119.25) is acting as immediate support. This is a classic "shakeout after parabolic move" setup.

3 Reasons to Buy

  • AI Data Center Megatrend: Multi-year $6B fiber optic supply deal with Meta Platforms. AI capex cycle just beginning — Corning is a primary beneficiary.
  • Accelerating Growth: Revenue growth of 20.4% YoY with earnings growth of 77.4%. Four consecutive earnings beats (Q1-Q4 2025). Margin expansion underway.
  • Technical Pullback Entry: -24.7% correction from highs, RSI at 44.6, EMA 50 acting as support at $119.25. Classic shakeout pattern after parabolic move.

3 Reasons to Avoid

  • Elevated Valuation: P/B of 8.95x and EV/Revenue of 7.3x are historically high for Corning. The stock has priced in significant growth.
  • Debt Load: $9.4B in total debt vs $1.5B cash. Net debt of ~$7.9B is manageable with EBITDA of $3.7B but limits flexibility.
  • Momentum Reversal Risk: MACD below signal line, price below EMA 20 ($135.6). Could see further downside to EMA 200 ($87.5) in a broad market selloff.

Competitive Moat: Corning's moat is its proprietary fusion manufacturing process for glass and its 175+ years of materials science expertise. The company holds thousands of patents in specialty glass, ceramics, and optical physics. Key moat sources: (1) Scale in optical fiber manufacturing, (2) Exclusive Gorilla Glass partnerships with Apple, Samsung, (3) 50%+ global market share in LCD glass substrates, (4) Deep R&D capabilities ($1B+ annual spend). Competitors cannot easily replicate the fusion process or match Corning's vertical integration.
